The **National Security Act of 1947** created the [CIA](/wiki/cia) and the [National Security Council](/wiki/national-security-council) — over the dead-serious opposition of [J. Edgar Hoover](/wiki/j-edgar-hoover).

## Passed on a lie

As Kiriakou tells it, Hoover liked everything as it was in 1947 and actively lobbied for no votes on Capitol Hill — enough to defeat the bill. Truman called him into the Oval Office and struck a deal: Hoover would endorse the Act, and in exchange the CIA would be a division of the FBI. *"That was a lie. He was lying to Hoover — he never had any intention."* The bill passed, Truman signed it, and Hoover realized he had been duped.<Cite s="2023-01-11-real-progressives" t="19:56" /><Cite s="2023-01-11-real-progressives" t="20:29" /><Cite s="2019-05-01-potkaars-podcast-potkaars-podcast" t="25:51" /><Cite s="2022-07-23-the-darkened-hour" t="17:09" />

## The 54-year grudge

Hoover's revenge outlived him: he ordered the FBI not to cooperate with the new organization — and that institutional freeze, Kiriakou says, lasted until after the 9/11 attacks, when the two agencies' computer systems were still incompatible and the CIA literally could not send the FBI its cables.<Cite s="2023-01-13-the-open-forum-podcast" t="44:04" /><Cite s="2026-01-05-lee-camp-unredacted-toni" t="33:21" /> Within a year of its independent birth, meanwhile, the CIA was already stealing the [Italian election of 1948](/wiki/italian-election-covert-action), financing the Christian Democrats.<Cite s="2019-05-01-potkaars-podcast-potkaars-podcast" t="26:21" /> Judged from 1947 onward, Kiriakou argues, the agency's record of analytic failure is the story nobody audits.<Cite s="2022-12-27-the-roundtable-gonzalo-l-the-roundtable-gonzalo-l" t="21:58" />
