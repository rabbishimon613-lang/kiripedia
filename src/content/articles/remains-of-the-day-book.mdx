**Remains of the Day** is a book by [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), announced for publication by Simon and Schuster in March 2026 and described by Kiriakou as *"a definitive guide to the historic cemeteries of Washington DC"* — the first installment in a planned series.<Cite s="2025-12-15-dead-drop-mini-episode-4-grave-concerns" t="01:04" /> Kiriakou produced a podcast series, *Grave Concerns*, as a companion to the book, profiling notable figures buried in the capital's cemeteries, including [Gore Vidal](/wiki/gore-vidal).<Cite s="2025-12-15-dead-drop-mini-episode-4-grave-concerns" t="01:04" />

Kiriakou has said the planned second book in the series will cover the Mafia graves of New York City.<Cite s="2025-12-15-dead-drop-mini-episode-4-grave-concerns" t="24:45" />

## Origin: a childhood cemetery discovery

Kiriakou traces his fascination with cemeteries to age nine, when he went looking for salamanders in a cemetery near his childhood home and found one under a rock beside a small tombstone bearing a Congressional Medal of Honor medallion. He and his mother went to the library to learn the story: the grave belonged to a local farmer who had fought with the 4th Pennsylvania Infantry and captured a Confederate battle flag at the Battle of Petersburg.<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="1:56:12" />

He says he decided to write the book during COVID, after searching online and finding nobody had ever written a book specifically about the cemeteries of Washington, D.C. — despite dozens of existing books about Arlington National Cemetery alone.<Cite s="2026-05-12-covert-operations-insight" t="27:59" />

## Writing and publication

His first seven books were about the CIA; *Remains of the Day* was his eighth, written in six weeks rather than the nine months a book normally takes him.<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="1:54:37" /><Cite s="2026-04-12-matthew-cox-inside-true-crime" t="1:56:26" /> After months of research, he identified 250 fascinating, largely unknown people buried in Washington, D.C. cemeteries for the book.<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="1:58:50" />

His publisher, Simon & Schuster, was impressed enough by the manuscript to commission four more cemetery books in the same vein: *Whispers in the Dirt*, covering the Mafia graves of New York City; and further volumes on the historic cemeteries of Chicago, the country-western graves of Nashville, and America's most notorious serial killers.<Cite s="2026-04-12-matthew-cox-inside-true-crime" t="1:56:26" /><Cite s="2025-07-25-gold-shields" t="1:14:30" /><Cite s="2024-10-06-joecat" t="1:14:16" /><Cite s="2026-05-12-covert-operations-insight" t="28:31" /> As of one 2025 interview he was 50% done with *Whispers in the Dirt*.<Cite s="2025-07-25-gold-shields" t="1:14:30" />

## Publication status and the ninth book (July 2026)

By mid-2026 Kiriakou described *Remains of the Day* as his eighth book and open for pre-order, with signed copies going out in February and general release in March — *"Barnes & Noble, Target, all over the place, Amazon"* — through rarebirdlit.com, and published by Simon & Schuster, an imprint he says he is *"very proud of."*<Cite s="2026-07-03-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-inside-fbi-recruiting-cia" t="00:30" /><Cite s="2026-07-03-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-inside-fbi-recruiting-cia" t="01:03" />

He put the follow-up, *Whispers in the Dirt* — the guide to New York City's Mafia graves — at about 70% complete, and described a working session on it: thinking about the mid-century Mafia figure Tommy Costello, finding an angle, and writing roughly 600 words in an hour.<Cite s="2026-07-03-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-inside-fbi-recruiting-cia" t="01:33" />

See [Kiriakou's writing process](/wiki/kiriakou-writing-process) for how the books get made.
