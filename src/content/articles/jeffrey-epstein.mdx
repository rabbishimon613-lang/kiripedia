**Jeffrey Epstein** was a convicted sex offender who died in U.S. federal custody. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s position on him, on the Carlos Watson program:<Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="48:49" />

## "An Israeli access agent"

Asked whether Epstein worked for the CIA: *"No way. … To spy on whom? The CIA's job is to collect foreign intelligence. What foreign intelligence did he have? He's spying on Bill Clinton and Bill Gates and Prince Andrew? That's the Israelis."*<Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="48:49" /><Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="49:21" />

## Suicide

Kiriakou believes Epstein died by suicide. His reasoning, drawn from his own time in the Bureau of Prisons: *"In the Bureau of Prisons everybody has his head up his ass. The cameras never work. The guards are always sound asleep. This happens all the time. These suicides — they happen literally every day in American prisons. Every day. When the only qualifications to be a prison guard are you have to be working on a GED or better and no felony convictions, you get what you pay for."*<Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="49:21" /><Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="49:51" /> Kiriakou has said the only formal qualifications to become a federal prison guard are a GED (or working toward one) and no felony convictions, and notes that inmates — not guards — handle mail call every day in federal prison because many guards cannot read well enough to sort names.<Cite s="2026-02-19-mario-nawfal" t="25:59" /> He has separately recounted that in his own federal prison, chapel religious services were frequently cancelled because inmates were having sex there, knowing the chapel's cameras did not work.<Cite s="2026-02-19-mario-nawfal" t="26:31" />

Kiriakou has laid out his reasoning for suicide over murder in detail elsewhere: he says he wrote publicly, based on his own two years in federal prison, that he did not believe Epstein's death was a conspiracy, because guards there are incompetent, cameras are routinely broken or turned off, and mandated cell checks rarely happen.<Cite s="2025-06-05-austin-and-matt" t="14:44" /> Epstein, he says, had expressed suicidal intent and should have been placed on formal suicide watch — stripped naked and given a tear-proof paper gown rather than sheets and clothes, then housed alone in a room glassed on three sides and watched continuously in shifts by other prisoners paid to do it.<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="1:48:50" /><Cite s="2026-02-19-mario-nawfal" t="27:36" /> None of that protocol was followed: Epstein kept his sheets and clothes, was not under continuous watch, and was housed with a cellmate — which Kiriakou has heard described alternately as no cellmate at all, or a cellmate who was an accused or convicted serial killer.<Cite s="2025-06-05-austin-and-matt" t="15:45" /><Cite s="2025-07-14-danny-jones" t="08:53" /> He notes Epstein was taken off suicide watch two days before he died,<Cite s="2025-07-14-danny-jones" t="10:26" /> and that the guard responsible for checking Epstein's cell every 15 minutes was working an unauthorized double shift and, in Kiriakou's assessment, was probably asleep, missing checks for at least 30 minutes.<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="1:50:23" /> His conclusion: *"I think he did commit suicide or was allowed to commit suicide in part because of the incompetent boobery of the Bureau of Prisons"* — compounded by the fact that many parties, including those wanting to protect Clinton, Dershowitz, and Trump associates, had reason to want him dead.<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="1:50:55" /> He has called for the government to release any video it has, saying it would settle whether the hyoid bone broke from the sheet or from the fall of his body.<Cite s="2025-06-05-austin-and-matt" t="15:45" /> Facing life without parole as a convicted pedophile who could never serve time in a minimum-security facility, Kiriakou believes Epstein simply had no way out.<Cite s="2025-06-05-austin-and-matt" t="16:47" /> Of all the factions that have been implicated or accused of possible involvement in Epstein's death, Kiriakou believes Israel had the most to gain from taking him out.<Cite s="2025-06-05-austin-and-matt" t="17:19" />

On the forensic detail that has fueled homicide theories — a fractured hyoid bone, more commonly associated with manual strangulation than hanging — Kiriakou has engaged with the same evidence differently across interviews. In one conversation he cited autopsy literature putting the incidence of hyoid fractures at somewhere between 17 and 76% of strangulation and hanging deaths, underscoring how equivocal the finding is on its own.<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="2:17:44" /> In a later conversation he noted that forensic pathologist Dr. Michael Baden called the fracture indicative of homicide by strangulation, and cited a more specific statistic — that only 25% of genuine hangings show one or two of the bones around the hyoid broken, whereas Epstein had all three broken.<Cite s="2025-07-14-danny-jones" t="06:17" /><Cite s="2025-07-14-danny-jones" t="07:19" />

## The Mossad access-operation thesis

Per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou): *"I've maintained from the very beginning that this has [MOSSAD](/wiki/mossad) written all over it. Mossad is incredibly active on U.S. soil — hundreds and hundreds of Israeli intelligence agents active across the United States at any given time. One of the things the Israelis are very good at is the cultivation and recruitment of what are called access agents. Say you want information from Bill Clinton and Hillary Clinton and Donald Trump and Prince Andrew — well, you're not going to recruit any of those people. So you do the next best thing: you recruit somebody who has access to all those people. Well, Jeffrey Epstein bore all the hallmarks of an Israeli access operation."*<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="2:47:15" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="2:47:45" />

Kiriakou dates his confidence in this reading to at least April 2023, when he told an interviewer flatly, *"I'm confident he was Intelligence… I'm convinced that Jeffrey Epstein was working for Mossad."*<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="1:40:57" /> His reasoning for why an access agent like Epstein has value even without a security clearance: *"Jeffrey Epstein's not going to give you classified information because he doesn't have access to classified information"* — his usefulness was getting powerful people to talk candidly with their guard down.<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="1:41:58" /> He has suggested the scientific gatherings on Epstein's island specifically targeted physicists and researchers working on programs such as nuclear research, DARPA projects, microwave weapons, and Havana Syndrome-related technology — work of interest to Mossad.<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="1:44:09" /> Kiriakou contrasts this patient cultivation with a CIA "cold pitch" — walking up and asking someone to work for you — which he says almost never works and, in his career, succeeded only once; ordinary recruitment takes a year or two of ingratiation.<Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="1:45:43" />

Kiriakou has also said he believes, more broadly, that Israel plants access agents like Epstein inside the United States to gather compromising material for shaping foreign policy — a position he says drew criticism from a former Mossad chief and from Dershowitz on Piers Morgan's show, discussed further below.<Cite s="2025-09-25-joe-mkhitaryan" t="1:19:20" /><Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight" t="27:12" />

## *"Kept it all in the family"*

Kiriakou's case for the Israeli connection extends to Epstein's principal lieutenant: *"His relationship with Ghislaine Maxwell, whose father was also a Mossad agent — a known Mossad agent — leads me to believe that they kept it all in the family. His access continued to improve. The wealthier he became, the source of his wealth is still not clear — we don't know where that money came from. It couldn't all have come from Les Wexner."*<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="2:48:16" />

On [Les Wexner](/wiki/les-wexner) specifically, Kiriakou goes further, offering it as his own unproven belief rather than a settled fact: *"I personally believe that Les Wexner and Jeffrey Epstein had some sort of sexual thing. That's just my belief. I don't have any proof."* In Kiriakou's reading, that relationship is what explains why Wexner — who had no due-diligence reason to trust a *"seventh grade math substitute teacher"* who *"never went to college"* — seeded Epstein's career with roughly a hundred million dollars.<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:17:26" /><Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:17:57" />

Kiriakou also flags the depth of the Rothschild family's relationship with Epstein as similarly undiligenced. He recounts an email in which Epstein told Elie de Rothschild that Harvard professors were teaching students that Hitler had been cared for in a homeless shelter owned by the Epstein and Rothschild families — a claim de Rothschild questioned as a conspiracy theory, which Epstein insisted was true.<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:18:28" /> Kiriakou separately notes it as suspicious that both [Les Wexner](/wiki/les-wexner) and Bill Clinton, in recent videotaped depositions, brought up the Rothschilds without being asked about them.<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:20:32" />

The operation Kiriakou imagines: *"His job was to get the most important people into compromising positions, say with underage girls maybe, and to be able to hold it over them — so you could extort information that you could then pass back to the Israelis who would then continue to fund you and to finance your lifestyle."*<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="2:48:46" />

## On the financial cover

From the Cleared Hot interview: *"Epstein was a really shitty trader. But if you're not worried about an ROI, who gives a fuck — you can do anything you want. You just pass the money through."* Per Kiriakou, Epstein's value to a state intelligence service was specifically *the ability to move money* under cover of a finance operation that didn't need to be profitable.<Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="53:09" />

## Epstein's vulnerabilities

Kiriakou's assessment of what made Epstein recruitable and controllable: *"As soon as they realized what his real vulnerabilities were, his vulnerabilities were an absolute love of money and pedophilia."* In Kiriakou's framing, [MOSSAD](/wiki/mossad) did not merely tolerate Epstein's conduct — it actively enabled it: *"I think it's obvious that they did. They set him up. Of course. Absolutely."*<Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:00:37" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:01:07" />

Kiriakou contrasts this with his understanding of other services: *"That would never happen at the CIA or at any other intelligence service that I'm aware of. And I think the Israelis were comfortable with it."*<Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:01:07" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:01:38" />

## The Eli Cohen parallel

Kiriakou compares the Epstein operation directly to the [Eli Cohen](/wiki/eli-cohen) case — the [MOSSAD](/wiki/mossad) penetration of Damascus in the 1960s: *"It's the exact same playbook. On a bigger scale."* The shared structure: place the asset in a foreign environment, fund a lavish lifestyle, build social connections upward through society, and harvest intelligence continuously. The key difference is motivation — Cohen acted for ideological reasons; Epstein acted for money.<Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="56:00" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="58:34" />

## Access agent — earliest statement of confidence, and the unreleased millions

Kiriakou's access-agent theory (developed further under "Zeteo," below) traces back at least to late 2023, when he told an interviewer he felt confident in his analysis and believed Epstein was a Mossad access agent.<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="2:57:46" /> In that same conversation he described [Ghislaine Maxwell](/wiki/ghislaine-maxwell)'s father as "a very well-known Mossad asset."<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="2:57:14" />

Kiriakou has also noted that roughly three to three-and-a-half million pages of Epstein-related documents remain unreleased, and that the president has said they will not be released — allegedly to avoid embarrassing his friends.<Cite s="2026-04-29-jay-dyer-they-really-did-this" t="09:02" /> This is despite Congress voting overwhelmingly for disclosure — 419 to 1 in the House, 100 to 0 in the Senate — and the president signing that release law before then declining to comply with it.<Cite s="2026-07-03-deep-focus-dorey-examines-epstein" t="01:04" />

## Wexner, the Rothschilds, and Epstein's implausible credentials

Kiriakou has said the depth of the Rothschild family's relationship with Epstein bothers him greatly, given that the family — wealthy since the 18th century — is not known for skipping due diligence on someone whose actual credentials were a seventh-grade math substitute teacher who never attended college (the same improbable entry point that got him referred as [Les Wexner](/wiki/les-wexner)'s tax advisor, per Kiriakou's Rothschild-referral analysis elsewhere in this article).<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:17:26" /><Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:17:57" />

On the Wexner relationship itself, Kiriakou has said he personally believes — without proof — that Wexner and Epstein had a sexual relationship, which in his view would help explain why Wexner seeded Epstein's career with roughly $100 million.<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:17:26" /><Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:17:57" />

Kiriakou has also cited an email in which Epstein told Elie de Rothschild that Harvard professors were teaching students that Hitler had been cared for in a homeless shelter owned by the Epstein and Rothschild families; when she questioned whether this was a conspiracy theory, Epstein insisted it was "100% true."<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:18:28" /> Separately, he has flagged as suspicious that both Wexner and Bill Clinton, in recent video depositions, volunteered the Rothschild name unprompted, without being asked about them.<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:20:32" />

## Boies review and the statute-of-limitations problem

Kiriakou has cited attorney David Boies — who represented Epstein accusers — as telling him that his staff at the firm Perkins Coie reviewed every page of the roughly three million released Epstein documents and found no evidence of any crime committed by either Bill Clinton or Donald Trump.<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:20:32" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:00:06" />

What angers Kiriakou most about the broader Epstein situation is that damaging revelations keep surfacing only after the relevant statute of limitations has already expired, meaning no one will ever face justice for them.<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:26:15" /> He has noted that the federal statute of limitations for conspiracy is five years, and remarked on the coincidence that damaging revelations are surfacing just over five years after Epstein's death — which he suggests may make some individuals feel it is now "safe" to let more come out.<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="2:27:20" />

On Clinton and Trump specifically, he references David Boies — described as *"one of the most important criminal defense attorneys in America"* who represented Virginia Giuffre — appearing on Piers Morgan to state that there are *"thousands and thousands of emails"* between Epstein and each man, and *"zero evidence that either Bill Clinton or Donald Trump committed a crime."*<Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:00:06" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="59:36" />

The second involves Epstein associate Greg Brown, whom documents reportedly show emailing Epstein about a plan to recover 80 billion dollars — by some accounts as much as 200 to 400 billion — in frozen Libyan assets, proposing to identify and recover 5 to 10% of the money in exchange for a 10 to 25% fee, using contacts described as formerly with MI6 and Mossad.<Cite s="2026-02-19-mario-nawfal" t="09:51" /> Kiriakou notes that under U.S. Treasury rules, a person who effects the repatriation of frozen assets through a legitimate whistleblower complaint can claim a reward of 26 to 34%, which he says is why Epstein was working with a major American law firm to try to claim a share of the frozen Libyan billions.<Cite s="2026-02-19-mario-nawfal" t="11:23" /> In his assessment, a private citizen could not plausibly pursue such a claim without very high-level intelligence or governmental ties, comparing the scheme to the arms dealers of the Iran-Contra era who worked directly with Saudi, U.S. and Israeli intelligence.<Cite s="2026-02-19-mario-nawfal" t="12:27" />

## "Perfectly intersecting interests" — the blackmail-operation characterization

Kiriakou characterizes Epstein's relationship with the intelligence services that used him as one of "perfectly intersecting interests": Epstein was, in his description, a sex addict who wanted access to intelligence work, and the agencies used his perversion to further their own interests.<Cite s="2026-02-19-mario-nawfal" t="39:28" /> He says cameras hidden in bathrooms and in every room of all of Epstein's houses is not explicable as ordinary security consciousness — nobody installs cameras in bathrooms for legitimate reasons — and that it most plausibly indicates a blackmail operation.<Cite s="2026-02-19-mario-nawfal" t="38:56" /> Kiriakou says he has never believed the operation was purely blackmail; his view is that whatever intelligence service ran it, most likely Mossad, was after material it could hold over the people implicated.<Cite s="2026-02-19-mario-nawfal" t="53:22" />

Kiriakou has also said he personally witnessed, on one occasion in his own CIA career, a conversation in which colleagues discussed "quietly encouraging" a problem source to commit suicide because the source had become a liability — a conversation from which nothing ultimately came.<Cite s="2026-02-19-mario-nawfal" t="42:31" /> He contrasts this with his broader CIA training, which held that sexual exploitation and coercion of sources does not work, because it risks turning a source into a double agent — and says he never personally saw anything at the CIA resembling what has emerged in the Epstein case.<Cite s="2026-02-27-the-ripple-effect-podcast" t="30:12" /> He separately notes that senior officials at NSA and the Pentagon in the 1980s and 1990s were documented members of the Church of Satan, including one major who later wrote a book about it.<Cite s="2026-02-27-the-ripple-effect-podcast" t="42:09" /> On Mossad's operating ethos specifically, Kiriakou describes it as mirroring the Israeli state's own "survival or death" mentality — willing to do "literally anything" to ensure Israel's survival — which he contrasts with what he calls the more restrained practices of American intelligence, attributable to congressional oversight.<Cite s="2026-02-19-mario-nawfal" t="44:03" />

## Ed Epstein, and the brother's account

Kiriakou has been careful to distinguish Jeffrey Epstein from Ed Epstein, an unrelated writer who reached out to challenge him over his remarks on a "Shadow Commission"; Kiriakou says the two men are not related.<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="30:49" /> He has spoken more than once with Jeffrey Epstein's brother, describing him as a seemingly ordinary, traumatized man who personally identified the body and took it to the crematorium, and who has himself questioned whether Epstein was murdered.<Cite s="2026-07-06-the-jason-jones-show" t="29:08" /><Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="32:20" /> Kiriakou also relays that attorney David Boies — who represents Hillary Clinton — told him his firm reviewed all roughly 3 million released Epstein documents and found no evidence of any crime committed by either Bill Clinton or Donald Trump.<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="34:24" /> He notes that the standard federal statute of limitations for conspiracy is five years, and remarks on the coincidence that the current wave of revelations is surfacing just over five years since Epstein's death.<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="35:28" /> He has also flagged as suspicious a claim in an Epstein email to Elie de Rothschild that Harvard professors taught students Hitler had been sheltered in a homeless shelter owned by the Epstein and Rothschild families — a claim consistent with his broader skepticism of the Rothschild family's undiligenced relationship with Epstein described elsewhere in this article.<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="31:49" />

## Early accusers and comparisons to other celebrity cases

Kiriakou recounts that two of Epstein's earliest accusers, sisters Maria Farmer and Annie Farmer, were ignored when they first came forward. Maria Farmer worked the front desk at Epstein's Manhattan office and said Trump visited three days a week; on one occasion, Kiriakou relays, Epstein told Trump about a particular girl, *"she's not for you."*<Cite s="2025-07-14-danny-jones" t="41:02" /> Kiriakou has also weighed in on a comparable celebrity prosecution, saying he followed the Sean "Diddy" Combs case closely from the start and — while calling Combs an epithet — did not see a prosecutable crime.<Cite s="2025-07-14-danny-jones" t="43:11" /> He rejects the reflexive skepticism directed at accusers in both cases, noting people who dismiss the Diddy allegations by asking why the alleged victims did not go to the police sooner draw the same comparison to Harvey Weinstein's accusers.<Cite s="2025-07-14-danny-jones" t="42:37" /> On the broader question of accountability, Kiriakou states he does not believe Ghislaine Maxwell is the only person deserving indictment in the Epstein case.<Cite s="2025-07-14-danny-jones" t="42:37" />

## General policy on sexual exploitation of sources

Kiriakou has said there is no rule barring a CIA case officer from procuring a child for a source, which he says means some officers do so — a policy gap he connects directly to situations like the one involving Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell.<Cite s="2024-10-30-bulwarg" t="11:06" /> Asked directly, without discussing method, whether he believes Epstein is in fact dead, Kiriakou has said yes, while adding he believes something troubling lies behind how he died.<Cite s="2020-11-23-revolutionary-change" t="53:54" />
