The **Central Intelligence Agency** (CIA) is the principal foreign intelligence service of the United States. It is both the institution that trained and employed [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) for fourteen years and the institution that, through allied prosecutors, pursued him after he publicly described its post-9/11 [enhanced interrogation](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation) program as torture.

## Organization (as described in the source corpus)

Articles already exist for two of the agency's internal divisions:

- [CIA National Resources Division](/wiki/cia-national-resources-division) — domestic intelligence collection from American businesspeople returning from "denied areas"
- [CIA Political Psychology Division](/wiki/cia-political-psychology-division) — long-distance psychiatric profiles of foreign leaders

Other entities mentioned in the source corpus, awaiting their own articles:

- **Directorate of Intelligence (DI)** — analysis. Kiriakou's first seven years at the agency were spent here, including time as the chief CIA historian for Saddam Hussein in the period leading into the First Iraq War.<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="06:14" />
- **Directorate of Operations (DO)** — case officers and covert action
- **Counterterrorism Center (CTC)** — the unit that doubled in personnel size within two to three days of September 11, 2001 as case officers and analysts were redirected and as paramilitary operators were detailed in from across the U.S. armed forces<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="55:02" />
- **Special Activities Division (SAD)** — the agency's paramilitary arm. A subcomponent of CTC, **Special Activities Group (SAG)**, is the counterterrorism-focused paramilitary unit. Immediately after September 11 SAD and SAG were augmented by detailed operators from Navy SEAL teams, Delta Force, and Army Rangers.<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="51:21" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="51:53" />

## Operational training

CIA case officer training is conducted at [The Farm](/wiki/the-farm) in Virginia, where approximately 80% of all CIA operational training takes place.

## Cable conventions

CIA operational cables are written exclusively in capital letters — a convention carried forward from the 1950s teletype-era infrastructure and never updated. The all-caps convention is the substrate for the long-running [Soylent Green cable easter egg](/wiki/soylent-green-cable-easter-egg) in which case officers spell out *"Soylent Green is people"* by scattering individual lowercase letters through their cables.<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="17:35" />

## Senior officials of the post-9/11 era

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) identifies a number of senior CIA officials of his era as personally responsible for crimes committed in the name of national security, including:

- [George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet)
- John McLaughlin
- Michael Morell
- [Jose Rodriguez](/wiki/jose-rodriguez)
- Rick Pavitt
- [John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan)

Of these, Brennan is identified as categorically distinct on the grounds that he *"plotted against an elected president of the United States."*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="33:50" />

## Resistance to presidential oversight

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) has described the [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s Senior Intelligence Service — the agency's equivalent of the federal [Senior Executive Service](/wiki/senior-executive-service) — as the body that functionally runs the CIA regardless of which administration is in office. Career officers at this level serve thirty to forty years, far outlasting any president, and are protected from dismissal by the Civil Service Act. Kiriakou has argued that when a president orders something the senior career layer opposes, the standard response is slow-rolling or simply waiting the president out.<Cite s="2026-05-19-ffn-jeff-dornik-swamps-holy-mess" t="20:15" />

## Alleged raid of the Director of National Intelligence's office

Kiriakou has stated that the [CIA](/wiki/cia) raided the office of the Director of National Intelligence, Tulsi Gabbard, seizing forty boxes of classified documents that were reportedly under review for declassification, including materials related to the [JFK assassination](/wiki/jfk-assassination): *"The CIA, which is not a law enforcement organization, raided the office of the director of national intelligence for whom they work."*<Cite s="2026-05-19-ffn-jeff-dornik-swamps-holy-mess" t="43:11" /> Kiriakou attributed this characterization to reporting current at the time of the interview, in May 2026.

## PDB as psychological profiling tool

According to [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), the Presidential Daily Brief served a secondary purpose in the 1990s: the president's annotations and reactions were used to psychologically profile him. Analysts referred to the sitting president as *"the first customer"* — a phrase Kiriakou said was common usage inside the [CIA](/wiki/cia) during that era.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="1:21:00" />

## Post-9/11 transformation

Kiriakou described a fundamental institutional shift that occurred after September 11: the [CIA](/wiki/cia) stopped being primarily an intelligence organization and became primarily a paramilitary one. This shift, in his account, was driven by the [enhanced interrogation program](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation), the drone program, and the expansion of covert action at the expense of collection and analysis.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="1:21:30" />

## NSA surveillance of Kiriakou

After his release from prison, [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) learned that the NSA had been intercepting his communications and sharing the contents with reporters at the *New York Times*. A reporter called and read back to Kiriakou the substance of a private conversation he had had.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="1:22:00" />

## Bob Woodward access under Tenet

Kiriakou stated that journalist Bob Woodward was given a memo by [CIA](/wiki/cia) Director [George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet) authorizing him to walk CIA headquarters freely — access Kiriakou described as extraordinary and inappropriate for a journalist.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="1:23:00" />

## Working-level officers are genuine

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) offered an unprompted assessment of [CIA](/wiki/cia) rank and file: most people at the working level are *"really, really smart,"* want nothing more than to serve the country and protect Americans, and *"God knows they don't do it for the money."* He described the workforce as genuinely motivated, contrasting this with his criticisms of senior leadership and institutional culture.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="17:30" />

## Criminality greater than imagined

Kiriakou said there is far more criminality inside the [CIA](/wiki/cia) than the public imagines — and that it goes well beyond petty misuse of government credit cards. Some officers commit *"real crimes,"* empowered, in Kiriakou's account, by convincing themselves that the authority and backing of the CIA places them beyond accountability.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="23:00" />

## Post-9/11 culture — *"everybody went nuts"*

Kiriakou described a complete post-9/11 cultural collapse at the [CIA](/wiki/cia): *"After 9/11, everybody went nuts. They just went nuts. They acted like the law, whether domestic or international, meant nothing to us. We were the good guys. We had been wronged. We were going to take revenge. And you're either with us or you're against us. And if you're against us, then get out."* He presented this as the institutional context in which the [enhanced interrogation program](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation) was born.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="30:00" /><Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="30:30" />

## Weaponized against dissidents

[Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) said he has reached the same conclusion as Tucker Carlson: the [CIA](/wiki/cia) has become weaponized against people who don't conform to what its leadership wants — whether that is a whistleblower who reports illegal activity or a political figure the agency cannot control.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="23:30" /><Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="24:00" />

## Three career-CIA directors

Of all [CIA](/wiki/cia) directors in the agency's history, only three have been career CIA officers. Every other director has been an outsider — a former FBI director, a general. Kiriakou described appointing generals as historically a mistake: people reach four-star rank by agreeing with superiors, and the director's job requires the opposite disposition — the willingness to tell a president he is wrong.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="29:30" /><Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="30:00" />

## *"Psychovac'd"* — officers who break

Kiriakou described a conversation with a group chief who asked him to travel to Beirut because the Lebanon desk officer — *"Phil"* — had been *"psychovac'd"*: evacuated for psychological breakdown and prohibited from traveling for six months while being evaluated. He used it to illustrate that the human costs of [CIA](/wiki/cia) operational work are real and ongoing.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="18:00" />

## Hollywood liaison branch

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described the [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s Office of Public Affairs as having opened, within the last decade before the interview, a dedicated branch whose sole job is to liaise with Hollywood studios — ensuring that films involving the CIA portray it favorably. The FBI has operated a similar program since the 1940s. The result, Kiriakou said, is a steady stream of films in which the CIA is the hero: he cited *Zero Dark Thirty*, *The Recruit*, *Argo*, and the TV series *The Americans*.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="15:00" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="15:30" />

## Podcast and creator strategy

Kiriakou said the [CIA](/wiki/cia) now has a strategy for podcasters and online creators, developed over recent years as it became clear that long-form digital media reaches large audiences. He described the template: identify a demographic (e.g., men aged 18–30), promote certain creators algorithmically to reach that group, and use that reach to propagate a specific, well-honed message — which could be anything from *"support the CIA"* to *"any criticism of Netanyahu is antisemitism."* The message must be repeated enough.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="15:30" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="16:00" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="16:30" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="17:00" />

## *"Not as smart as we think we are"*

Kiriakou offered a candid institutional self-assessment: the [CIA](/wiki/cia) missed nearly every major global development since its founding in 1947. The list he cited: the rise of the Berlin Wall; the fall of the Berlin Wall; the fall of the Soviet Union; the Suez crisis; the Iran hostage crisis; and 9/11. The CIA is good, he said, at day-to-day presidential updates and recruiting minor peripheral figures around terrorist groups — but not at seeing the large picture.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="42:00" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="42:30" />

## Post-9/11 domestic-surveillance expansion

Before 9/11, it was illegal — *"like in stone"* — for U.S. intelligence agencies to spy on Americans. After 9/11, Kiriakou said, billions of dollars are now spent spying on Americans across NSA, [CIA](/wiki/cia), FBI, and intelligence-community contractors. The additional mechanism is commercial: authorities can purchase Americans' [metadata](/wiki/government-metadata-purchase) directly from carriers and social-media platforms without a judge's order, eliminating the warrant requirement entirely. *"Nothing is secret. Nothing."*<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="43:00" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="43:30" />

## Steakhouse warning — day one

On his first day at [CIA](/wiki/cia), the director of security told the new-hire class never to eat at a particular steakhouse on Route 123 near headquarters — the closest restaurant — because the KGB believed all CIA employees ate there and had filled it with officers waiting for CIA people to begin talking about work. Kiriakou said he has never eaten there to that day.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="47:00" />

## Foreign spies in the U.S. — 50,000 to 60,000

Kiriakou estimated the total number of foreign intelligence officers and [CIA](/wiki/cia) personnel operating in the United States at 50,000 to 60,000. He noted that anyone working at a major U.S. defense contractor — Northrop Grumman, Lockheed Martin, Boeing — has even odds of encountering a foreign intelligence officer in the course of normal work. The three countries most actively running collection operations inside the U.S. are Russia, China, and Israel.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="57:00" />

## Directorate of Science and Technology — field-equipment process

The Directorate of Science and Technology is one of four [CIA](/wiki/cia) directorates. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described it as an entire directorate — not a room full of gadgets as depicted in James Bond films — and noted that a close friend of his eventually became the CIA's Chief Technology Officer at the Senior Intelligence Service level.<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="40:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="41:00" />

The operational process for obtaining technical equipment is as follows: an officer in the field sends a cable describing the operation and specifying what technological capability is needed. Science-and-Technology officers fly out with a sealed diplomatic pouch containing gadgets. They demonstrate options and the field officer selects whatever is most appropriate for the operation.<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="41:00" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="41:30" />

Kiriakou described one instance from his time as chief of counterterrorism operations in Pakistan after 9/11. His team was pursuing senior al-Qaeda leaders who made electronic mistakes rarely. Two technology officers flew out with a range of equipment; nothing exactly matched the need. They built something from scratch in approximately one week — a device Kiriakou and his team called *"the magic box"* — which could zero in on a cell-phone signal. The limitation was that the target would turn his phone on only long enough to check voicemail, then remove the battery. The magic box proved unhelpful.<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="41:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="42:00" />

## 90% human skills

Kiriakou characterized intelligence work as ninety percent or more human skills — relationship-building, judgment, patience — with technology as a small complement. He described surveillance as among the most tedious parts of the job: sitting at a window watching an apartment across the street for days, with periodic relief after six hours.<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="42:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="43:00" />

## Mental illness, divorce, and alcohol

Kiriakou described mental illness as common among [CIA](/wiki/cia) officers, driven by what he characterized as *"otherworldly"* pressure. He said the CIA has the highest divorce rate of any U.S. government agency — officers married four or five times is not unusual — and that Kiriakou's own two divorces were, in his words, *"the record least number."*<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="43:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="44:00" />

He described two examples of officer breakdown from his Pakistan posting: a colleague named Mark who disclosed on the day of an armed operation that he could not participate because he had beaten his wife and was therefore prohibited from carrying a firearm; and a Beirut desk officer referred to as *"Phil"* or *"Allan"* who had been placed on psychiatric no-travel status and sent for evaluation — making Kiriakou available to cover his meetings.<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="44:00" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="45:30" />

Kiriakou stated the [CIA](/wiki/cia) provides no formal training in coping with pressure — no breathing techniques, yoga, or mental-health protocols. He described alcohol as *"a problem at the agency"* as a direct result. On his own, he took Tai Chi, which he said helped immensely.<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="45:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="46:00" />
