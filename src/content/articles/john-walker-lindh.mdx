**John Walker Lindh** is the American citizen captured while fighting alongside the Taliban during the early phase of the U.S. campaign in Afghanistan. He was present at the [Qala-i-Jangi fortress](/wiki/qala-i-jangi-uprising) in northern Afghanistan at the start of the late-November 2001 prisoner uprising in which CIA officer [Mike Spann](/wiki/mike-spann) was killed.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:37:09" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:37:39" />

## Jesselyn Radack and the false rights claim

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) recounts, drawing on whistleblower attorney Jesselyn Radack's own account, that when Lindh was captured at the Qala-i-Jangi uprising, Radack — then a Justice Department ethics attorney on duty that day — was called from the Pentagon and told him, as clearly as she could, that Lindh had to be read his rights. He was not.<Cite s="2015-11-12-quakerhouse" t="1:01:21" /><Cite s="2015-11-14-robin-hensel" t="56:12" /> Radack objected to the Justice Department's own director of ethics that proceeding without doing so was unconstitutional and unethical; days later, Attorney General John Ashcroft nonetheless gave a press conference stating that Lindh had been read his rights, had waived his right to an attorney, and had confessed. Per Kiriakou, *"that was a lie."*<Cite s="2015-11-12-quakerhouse" t="1:02:23" /> In a related claim from a different telling, Ashcroft separately stated that Lindh was entitled to choose his own lawyer.<Cite s="2025-10-14-jspione-silenced-2014-the-war-on-whistleblowers-feature-documentary-" t="35:57" />
