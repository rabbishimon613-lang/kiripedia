**Robert Hanssen** was the chief of counterintelligence at the FBI and a long-running mole for the USSR and then Russia. Per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), when assets Hanssen was betraying began to be executed, Hanssen — in his counterintelligence-chief capacity — pointed the FBI at his CIA counterpart [Brian Kelly](/wiki/brian-kelly): *"It's that CIA guy, Brian Kelly."*<Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="34:44" /><Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="35:16" />

## The frame

Per Kiriakou: the FBI put Kelly on leave without pay and surrounded his house 24 hours a day with agents. Other FBI agents traveled to Oregon, where Kelly's daughter was working in a nursing home, and told her: *"You have to get your father to confess, because if we arrest him without a confession we're going to ask for the death penalty."* Kelly: *"I don't know what's happening here. It's not me."* Time elapsed before Hanssen was identified as the actual mole: a year and a half.<Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="35:16" /><Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="35:46" />

## The dead-drop tape

Per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), the operational confirmation of Hanssen as the mole came from a recording device the FBI had placed at a dead-drop site under a footbridge in Vienna, Virginia. After the drop was made and the mole did the pickup, the FBI retrieved the device and brought it back to FBI headquarters: *"They were all gathered around the table with a recorder to see who it is, and they pressed play — and it was their boss, Hanssen."*<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="29:59" />

## The plea-deal pension

Hanssen retained Kiriakou's eventual attorney. Per Kiriakou: *"They had him dead to rights. There was no chance to ever win. And he ended up taking a plea, and the only thing he got in exchange for the plea was that his wife could have his pension. That was it. He died a year ago or two years ago at the Supermax ADX Florence. He never saw the light of day."*<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="32:07" /><Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="32:38" />

## Structural lesson

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) used the Hanssen case alongside the [Aldrich Ames](/wiki/aldrich-ames) case to make a structural point: the chief-of-counterintelligence position requires an officer of exceptional personal integrity — no drinking, no drugs, no gambling, no infidelity, no speeding — because any vulnerability becomes a recruitment lever. People in that position are specifically targeted, and the position itself provides maximum access to sources and methods.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="15:00" />

## Brian Kelly framed — the threat to the daughter

FBI agents traveled to Oregon, where [Brian Kelly](/wiki/brian-kelly)'s daughter was working at a nursing home, and told her: *"If you give us information on your father, we won't execute him."* She said her father was innocent. They told her: *"Then we'll have to execute him."* This went on for approximately a year.<Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="29:00" /><Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="29:30" />

## The dead-drop tape — *"it was their own boss"*

The FBI placed a recording device at a dead-drop site in Vienna, Virginia, under a small footbridge. The drop was made and retrieved. FBI agents gathered in a conference room, pressed play, and listened to the tape to finally identify their mole. They heard the voice of their own chief mole hunter — [Robert Hanssen](/wiki/robert-hanssen).<Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="29:30" /><Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="30:00" /><Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="30:30" />

[Hanssen](/wiki/robert-hanssen) had realized he was under suspicion when he was transferred to a lesser position; he interpreted it as a sign they were onto him. His assistant — an FBI analyst named [Eric O'Neill](/wiki/eric-oneill), now connected to [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) on LinkedIn — was placed there specifically to surveil Hanssen and catch him in a slip. O'Neill is the subject of the film *Breach*. The stress of the case caused him to leave the FBI immediately after Hanssen's arrest.<Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="31:00" /><Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="31:30" /><Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="32:00" />

[Hanssen](/wiki/robert-hanssen) hired [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s own attorney — Plato Cacheris — to represent him. The government had Hanssen *"dead to rights"*; the only concession from the plea was that his wife could keep his pension. He died in custody at ADX Florence Supermax.<Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="32:00" /><Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="32:30" />

## The third mole — Singapore station chief

[Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described a postscript: a third mole, a senior officer in the Counterterrorism Center where Kiriakou worked, who had served as [CIA](/wiki/cia) station chief in Singapore. When [Ames](/wiki/aldrich-ames) was arrested, this officer concluded that the KGB probably needed a new mole and volunteered himself. He contacted the KGB station chief in Singapore and offered his services: a specific financial sum and, he specified, diamonds. His motivation was money — he was paying significant alimony. He was caught, but only after outing hundreds of undercover CIA officers who had to be extracted from Russia. He was sentenced to thirty-one years and seven months.<Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="32:30" /><Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="33:00" /><Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="33:30" /><Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="34:00" />

From prison, he called his son with instructions to contact the KGB so that information passed from prison could be deposited into a secret account he could access upon release. The son walked directly to the Russian Embassy in Washington. The FBI, which maintains continuous surveillance of the embassy, arrested him immediately.<Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="34:00" /><Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="34:30" /><Cite s="2026-05-26-covert-operations-insight-cia-secrets" t="35:00" />

## The counterintelligence mole

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) pairs [Robert Hanssen](/wiki/robert-hanssen) with [Aldrich Ames](/wiki/aldrich-ames) as traitors who had each headed counterintelligence for Russian operations, and adds that the Chinese are *"all over us"* — citing the arrest of the mayor of Arcadia as an unregistered Chinese agent.<Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="1:15:12" /><Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="1:15:43" />
