The **deep state** is a term [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) defends against the people most likely to share his politics. He notes that *"a lot of people, especially on the left, think that the deep state is just a right-wing trope to complain about government"* — and rejects that flatly, with an explicit statement of where he is speaking from: *"I can tell you, coming from the left, that the deep state is absolutely real."*<Cite s="2026-07-06-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-aaron-good-on-the" t="00:30" />

## "I was a part of it"

His claim to standing is participation rather than observation: *"It's real, and it's ugly, and I know because I was a part of it when I spent 15 years at the CIA, and frankly, another couple of years on Capitol Hill on the Senate Foreign Relations Committee staff."*<Cite s="2026-07-06-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-aaron-good-on-the" t="01:00" />

## The label doesn't matter

Kiriakou treats the terminology as a distraction from the thing being named, and offers to drop it: *"you don't have to call it the deep state. You can call it whatever you want. You can call it the state. You can call it the federal bureaucracy."*<Cite s="2026-07-06-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-aaron-good-on-the" t="01:00" />

What he will not concede is its existence or its durability: *"the truth of the matter is that it is there. It's there to stay. It's not going anywhere."*<Cite s="2026-07-06-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-podcast-aaron-good-on-the" t="01:00" />

## The moment he was convinced

Kiriakou dates his own conviction that a deep state exists to one meeting. The [National Intelligence Council](/wiki/national-intelligence-council), housed at the CIA, holds the topmost analysts of the whole intelligence community — nearly all in the senior intelligence service, nearly all with doctorates, moving back and forth between the agency and universities such as Harvard and Stanford. Among them was the national intelligence officer for warning, whose job was to look five, ten and twenty years ahead and say which countries would become problems.<Cite s="2026-06-02-covert-strategies-revealed-corpus-has-osxgmj89fwq-from-this-show" t="20:39" />

Kiriakou, then in his twenties, went into a meeting with him and found people congratulating him. The reason was an age waiver from the director allowing him to work until seventy. He was sixty-five; he had been in the same job for forty-two years; he intended to stay another five.<Cite s="2026-06-02-covert-strategies-revealed-corpus-has-osxgmj89fwq-from-this-show" t="21:41" />

Kiriakou's question was why anyone would want the same job for forty-seven years, and the answer he arrived at is the article's point. *"That's the power that you've built. That's the authority that you've built. Presidents come and go."* A president who calls and asks for a hard look at Brazil can be told, not in so many words, to go and take a jump — the response simply slow-rolled until the president is long gone. *"And I realized for the very first time there actually is a deep state."*<Cite s="2026-06-02-covert-strategies-revealed-corpus-has-osxgmj89fwq-from-this-show" t="22:15" /><Cite s="2026-06-02-covert-strategies-revealed-corpus-has-osxgmj89fwq-from-this-show" t="22:46" />

Kiriakou allows that the name is negotiable: *"We can call it the federal bureaucracy, but by God it's there. It exists."*<Cite s="2026-06-02-covert-strategies-revealed-corpus-has-osxgmj89fwq-from-this-show" t="22:46" />
