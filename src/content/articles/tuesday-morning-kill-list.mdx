The **Tuesday morning kill list** was a weekly U.S. counterterrorism-targeting meeting instituted by [John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan) in 2009 during his service as Deputy National Security Advisor for Counterterrorism in the first Barack Obama administration. Per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), the meeting produced a written list of individuals to be killed in the following week, with assignments distributed to the responsible kill teams; the next Tuesday's meeting opened with confirmation of completion and the issuance of the next list.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="45:28" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="45:59" />

## Format and mechanics

Kiriakou describes the list as produced each week at the White House, where CIA personnel met with the White House counsel's staff to name people to be killed that week. The head of the [Special Activities Division](/wiki/special-activities-division) collected the list, sent his people out, and returned the following Tuesday for the next one.<Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="1:50:57" /><Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="1:51:30" /> Elsewhere Kiriakou places the meeting at 7 a.m., after which the Special Activities Division's *"global services"* teams used post-9/11 targeting technology to carry out the killings worldwide before returning for the next list.<Cite s="2025-08-18-hope-2025-kiriakou-talk" t="39:53" /><Cite s="2025-08-18-hope-2025-kiriakou-talk" t="40:23" />

Once a target was set, the kill itself proceeded by one of two standard CIA modes: a drone strike, or a close-in action in which agency personnel were inserted, carried out the killing, and were exfiltrated by helicopter. *"You just fire a missile from the drone, or you drop a guy in that does a close-in shot, and then you get back on the helicopter and fly home."*<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="45:59" />

## Enabling technology

The program's regular weekly cadence was made possible, per Kiriakou, by the maturation of CIA targeting software during this period — *"the tech got sophisticated enough that you could just write up a list of people that you want to kill that week and you dish out the assignments."* The substitution of automated metadata processing for manual analyst work eliminated the staffing bottleneck that would otherwise have constrained a weekly kill list.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="45:28" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="45:59" />

## Legal standard and accountability

Kiriakou describes the [Tuesday-morning kill-list](/wiki/tuesday-morning-kill-list) meeting as the formalization of the Obama administration's targeted-killing program. The legal framework required only that a target meet the standard of posing *"a clear and present danger to the United States, to an American citizen, or to an American installation"* — a standard Kiriakou describes as sounding precise but being, in practice, entirely vague. Post-mission accountability consisted of taking the kill teams' word for what happened.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:14:00" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:14:30" />

Kiriakou says some names on the list were American citizens who had never been charged with any crime.<Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="1:52:00" />

## Brennan's role and continuation under later administrations

[John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan) ran the meetings during Obama's first term; in the second term, Brennan was elevated to [CIA](/wiki/cia) Director.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:13:30" /> Kiriakou is uncertain whether the program was retained under subsequent administrations: *"He started in '09 and kept it going. I have no idea if Donald Trump kept it or Joe Biden kept it or revived it, but it was something that they were very proud of in the Obama administration. They were just going out whacking everybody."* He observes that no U.S. administration has had a political incentive to publicize discontinuation of such a program: *"In their re-election campaigns: 'We need to kill fewer terrorists. If I'm reelected, we'll be a good one.'"*<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="46:31" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="47:02" />

Kiriakou situates the program within the broader scale of Obama-era drone warfare: *"Nobody dropped more missiles from drones than Obama did."*<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="47:02" />
