**Ukraine as a proxy war** is the frame John Kiriakou applied to the 2022 Russian invasion within two months of its start, and the reason he was urging negotiations at a point when almost no one in Washington was.

## The assessment

His summary was that he saw nothing good on the horizon and that this was another of the situations every generation produces: the United States perfectly happy to pour money and weapons in, run it as a proxy war, in the knowledge that the Ukrainians cannot defeat the Russians. The object is to bog the Russians down — and the tired old saying that the United States is willing to fight to the last Ukrainian, he said, *"is actually true, and that's what makes it so sad."*<Cite s="2022-04-21-lee-camp-unredacted-toni-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-joins-lee-cam" t="20:17" /><Cite s="2022-04-21-lee-camp-unredacted-toni-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-joins-lee-cam" t="20:48" />

The end state he predicted was that Russia would end up with the Donbas and with Crimea and would probably destroy Mariupol and Odesa, with terrible loss of life along the way — and that since that was where it was going, it was better to open peace talks at once, even if it meant putting your tail between your legs and giving away something you would rather not.<Cite s="2022-04-21-lee-camp-unredacted-toni-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-joins-lee-cam" t="21:20" />

## The money and the silence

On the day of the interview President Biden announced a further $800 million in weapons, a third tranche on top of the nearly $10 billion already sent. Kiriakou's question was whether that was simply what the country now did instead of spending on roads, schools, bridges and airports.<Cite s="2022-04-21-lee-camp-unredacted-toni-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-joins-lee-cam" t="21:51" /> He also noted what was absent: nobody in the administration or the government was so much as saying the words *peace talks*. There had been talks early on in Istanbul, which the United States did not attend and did not even encourage the Ukrainians to attend; they lasted three days, everyone went home, and no one raised the idea again.<Cite s="2022-04-21-lee-camp-unredacted-toni-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-joins-lee-cam" t="22:24" />

## Why Putin

His explanation for the appetite is personal to [Vladimir Putin](/wiki/vladimir-putin): Washington never liked him, because he is independent — not mouldable the way Boris Yeltsin was, not a friendly the way Mikhail Gorbachev was. For a long time the assumption was that he would have to be lived with; the war looked like an opportunity not to, with the Ukrainians doing the work.<Cite s="2022-04-21-lee-camp-unredacted-toni-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-joins-lee-cam" t="22:55" /><Cite s="2022-04-21-lee-camp-unredacted-toni-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-joins-lee-cam" t="23:27" />

## What the CIA was doing

Asked what the conversations inside the agency would be, Kiriakou's answer was training and materiel — secure communications, night vision goggles, sniper rifles, close-in equipment — running along two tracks: a great deal of paramilitary training for the Ukrainians, and a real focus on recruiting [access agents](/wiki/access-agent) to the Russian leadership.<Cite s="2022-04-21-lee-camp-unredacted-toni-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-joins-lee-cam" t="23:27" /><Cite s="2022-04-21-lee-camp-unredacted-toni-cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-joins-lee-cam" t="23:58" />
