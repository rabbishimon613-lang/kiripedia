**Abu Zubaydah capture** refers to the coordinated March 2002 raid on thirteen safe houses in Lahore and Faisalabad, Pakistan, that resulted in the apprehension of the al‑Qaeda operative known as Abu Zubaydah. The operation was overseen on the ground by [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john_kiriakou), then chief of counterterrorism operations for the [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia). An unnamed CIA targeting analyst was flown in at Kiriakou’s request to design the mission.<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:26:29" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:31:42" />

## Planning and targeting

The CIA possessed only a limited set of contact points for Abu Zubaydah—few phone numbers, email addresses, and physical locations. His disciplined use of a short‑duration battery, activating his phone for merely fifteen to thirty seconds to check voicemail, thwarted conventional geolocation methods. The targeting analyst, a peer of Andy Bustumante’s wife, drafted a butcher‑paper sheet *"about the size of the table"* and placed Zubaydah’s name at its centre, surrounding it with every known associate and then with contacts of those associates. After a week or two of work the analyst reported, *"I can't pair it down to anything less than 14."*<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:29:36" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:31:42" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:32:14" />

## Operational cover and logistics

Kiriakou requested a team of thirty‑six personnel, weapons, night‑vision equipment, secure communications, and *"X millions of dollars in cash."* A chartered Boeing 737 arrived twenty‑four hours later. Operating out of a Lahore hotel under the supervision of a sympathetic British manager, Kiriakou slipped the manager a few hundred dollars and had the troublesome lobby guard *"fired"*. The Pakistani Inter‑Services Intelligence (ISI) handler supplied a real‑estate agent, and Kiriakou purchased a ten‑bedroom, ten‑bathroom house in Lahore for cash, presenting the cover story of a textile business. When the agent asked what they did for a living, Kiriakou replied, *"I looked at my friend and he goes, 'We're textile barons.' And I said, 'Yes, we are. We're textile barons.'"*<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:32:45" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:33:48" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:34:19" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:35:21" /> A second seven‑bedroom house was acquired in Faisalabad for interrogation purposes.<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:35:21" />

## Execution of the raid

From the original fourteen targets, one was discarded during a dry run (a pay‑phone in a shish‑kebab stand), leaving thirteen locations. At 02:00 hours, Kiriakou and a colleague were on the roof of the Lahore safe house when they heard *"this sound — dink, dink, dink — metal on metal"*, followed by gunfire. Site 13, the nearest, had gone hot. A Pakistani officer opened fire with an AK‑47, killing a Syrian al‑Qaeda bomb‑maker instantly and wounding Abu Zubaydah with three shots to the thigh, groin, and stomach as he leapt between rooftops.<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:37:24" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:37:56" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:42:08" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:43:10" />

## Identification and medical evacuation

Because the most recent photograph of Zubaydah was six years old, the targeting analyst instructed Kiriakou to photograph the detainee’s ear, noting that *"no two people on earth have the same ears"*. The image was transmitted to headquarters via a wired camera‑phone connection (cell‑phone cameras were not yet available). Five minutes later, headquarters confirmed, *"It's him."*<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:41:04" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:41:37" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:42:08" />

Abu Zubaydah was rushed to Faisalabad Hospital, which Kiriakou described as *"the worst place on Earth"*. The facility’s doors and windows were open, dogs roamed the corridors, and syringes were stored in a bar of Irish Spring soap. As word of the capture spread, al‑Qaeda gunmen conducted drive‑by shootings at the hospital. Kiriakou requested the assistance of Pakistani Major Khalid, who dispatched a helicopter that landed in the parking lot twenty minutes later. While mid‑surgery, Zubaydah was loaded onto the aircraft and flown fifty miles to a Pakistani military base clinic.<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:43:41" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:44:13" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:45:15" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:45:46" />

## Post‑capture treatment, interrogation, and legal concerns

Kiriakou remained at Zubaydah’s bedside for a full twenty‑four hours, awaiting his emergence from coma. Exhausted and hungry, he received a clean red [SpongeBob SquarePants](/wiki/abu_zubaydah) shirt from the safe house. When Zubaydah finally opened his one good eye, he stared at the shirt; *"he doesn't look at me, he looks at SpongeBob"* and his pulse surged from 120 to 220 beats per minute, prompting the monitors to sound *"Code blue, code blue."*<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:49:59" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:50:30" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:51:00" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:51:33" />

In subsequent reflections, Kiriakou recounted that his orders, relayed by then‑Director George Tenet, required him to remain at Zubaydah’s bedside twenty‑four hours a day: *"my orders were to sit next to his bed 24 7. George Tenet told me 24 7 CIA eyes on. He said do not leave his bedside"*.<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:49:59" /> After Zubaydah emerged from his coma, he asked Kiriakou to kill him, requesting a pillow to smother himself; Kiriakou refused. *"the first thing he said to me was he asked me to kill him. He asked me to take a pillow and smother him and I said, 'oh no'"*.<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:49:59" />

Zubaydah also expressed personal regrets, stating he would never know the touch of a woman or the joy of fatherhood, and recited poetry he had written. He asked Kiriakou to convey a message to his mother.<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="1:50:30" />

During the debriefing period, a senior CIA Counterterrorism Center officer casually asked whether Kiriakou wanted to be certified in the use of “enhanced interrogation techniques.” The officer explained that the program had been cleared by the Justice Department and signed off by the President. Kiriakou expressed moral and legal concerns, noting the existence of the Federal Torture Act of 1946, which outlawed the very techniques being proposed. *"we have something in this country called the Federal Torture Act of 1946 which specifically outlaws exactly those techniques that we used against al‑Qaeda prisoners"*.<Cite s="2021-05-12-howie-hawkins-greensocialist-notes-23-whistleblowers-with-john-kiriak" t="17:07" />

## Later statements and controversy

Later accounts note that Zubaydah was initially believed to be al‑Qaeda’s number three, a claim later disproved; he had never formally joined al‑Qaeda but assisted the organization with logistics and founded two training camps in southern Afghanistan, as well as the “House of Martyrs” safe house in Peshawar, Pakistan.<Cite s="2021-05-12-howie-hawkins-greensocialist-notes-23-whistleblowers-with-john-kiriak" t="12:57" /><Cite s="2021-05-12-howie-hawkins-greensocialist-notes-23-whistleblowers-with-john-kiriak" t="13:28" /> On the night of 22 March 2002, a Pakistani policeman shot Zubaydah three times with an AK‑47, inflicting severe wounds to the thigh, groin, and stomach.<Cite s="2021-05-12-howie-hawkins-greensocialist-notes-23-whistleblowers-with-john-kiriak" t="13:28" />

A 2025 documentary segment summarised the operation:

- Kiriakou received a report from headquarters that Abu Zubaydah was in Pakistan.<Cite s="2025-10-14-jspione-silenced-2014-the-war-on-whistleblowers-feature-documentary-" t="24:56" />
- A mistake by Zubaydah narrowed his location to fourteen sites.<Cite s="2025-10-14-jspione-silenced-2014-the-war-on-whistleblowers-feature-documentary-" t="24:56" />
- A large team of CIA and FBI agents was assembled to capture him.<Cite s="2025-10-14-jspione-silenced-2014-the-war-on-whistleblowers-feature-documentary-" t="25:28" />
- Zubaydah attempted to escape and was shot by Pakistani forces.<Cite s="2025-10-14-jspione-silenced-2014-the-war-on-whistleblowers-feature-documentary-" t="25:28" />
- He asked for a glass of red wine and later requested to be smothered with a pillow.<Cite s="2025-10-14-jspione-silenced-2014-the-war-on-whistleblowers-feature-documentary-" t="25:59" />
- He recited poetry to Kiriakou.<Cite s="2025-10-14-jspione-silenced-2014-the-war-on-whistleblowers-feature-documentary-" t="26:29" />
- Kiriakou expressed empathy towards him.<Cite s="2025-10-14-jspione-silenced-2014-the-war-on-whistleblowers-feature-documentary-" t="26:29" />

The operation sparked ongoing debate over the legality of “enhanced interrogation” and the moral responsibilities of operatives tasked with capturing high‑value detainees. Kiriakou’s later testimony highlighted the compartmentalised nature of the program, the difficulty of reporting abuses, and the broader implications for U.S. counterterrorism policy.<Cite s="2022-05-23-danny-jones-cia-spy-betrayed" t="15:30" />
