## Kiriakou's 1989 psychological profile; 'please bomb Iran'

During his 1989 CIA application interviews, [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) was given a folder of clippings on [Benjamin Netanyahu](/wiki/benjamin-netanyahu), then Israel's UN ambassador, and asked to write a one-page psychological analysis; he concluded Netanyahu would probably become prime minister one day — and he did.<Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="43:37" /><Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="44:07" /><Cite s="2026-07-07-covert-strategies-revealed" t="41:11" /> Kiriakou calls him *"brilliant"* and *"dangerous,"* a player who runs rings around every other Israeli figure, and says that from the Reagan era every Israeli prime minister asked American presidents to bomb Iran.<Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="44:38" /><Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="45:08" />

## The Milchan visa and October 7 as a political gift (SaltCube)

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says [Benjamin Netanyahu](/wiki/benjamin-netanyahu) lobbied Secretary of State [John Kerry](/wiki/john-kerry) three times to restore the revoked U.S. visa of Israeli spy [Arnon Milchan](/wiki/arnon-milchan) — one of Netanyahu's corruption charges — and that FBI documents place Netanyahu personally in the smuggling of U.S. [nuclear secrets](/wiki/israeli-nuclear-program).<Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="33:06" /><Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="33:36" /> He calls Netanyahu arguably the most unpopular yet longest-serving figure in Israeli history, for whom the [October 7 attack](/wiki/october-7-attack) was a *"God-sent gift"* that keeps him in power and out of prison.<Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="46:38" /><Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="47:11" />

Kiriakou has given the same assessment elsewhere with a specific number: as of a mid-2024 interview, Netanyahu was, he said, the most unpopular major political figure in Israel, with a 22% approval rating even before October 7, and faced fraud, criminal, and corruption charges predating the attack.<Cite s="2024-08-12-danny-jones-loyal-officer-vs-dissident-spy" t="2:27:20" /> He has said he believes the Israeli government under Netanyahu was hoping for an attack like October 7, because it would provide the perceived legal authority to destroy the whole of Gaza, not merely [Hamas](/wiki/hamas).<Cite s="2024-08-12-danny-jones-loyal-officer-vs-dissident-spy" t="2:14:07" /> Kiriakou has gone on to characterize the resulting Israeli campaign as genocide — a term, he says, he does not use lightly — while affirming that Israel has a right to exist.<Cite s="2024-08-12-danny-jones-loyal-officer-vs-dissident-spy" t="2:15:09" />

During a Netanyahu visit to Washington, Rome ceasefire talks between Israel, [Hamas](/wiki/hamas), the United States, Egypt, and Qatar broke up after the Egyptian delegation complained the Israeli delegation had no authority to agree to anything.<Cite s="2024-08-12-danny-jones-loyal-officer-vs-dissident-spy" t="2:15:42" /> Kiriakou says National Security Council spokesman John Kirby stated that Netanyahu *"didn't even pretend to support a ceasefire"* during that visit.<Cite s="2024-08-12-danny-jones-loyal-officer-vs-dissident-spy" t="2:16:15" />

Kiriakou also traces a longer pattern of dispossession to Netanyahu's tenure: across his seven terms as prime minister, he says, Israel has seized more Palestinian land, destroyed more farms, burned more olive trees, and confiscated more water sources every year.<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="1:47:53" /> Kiriakou says pro-settlement groups have gone as far as holding "real estate seminars" at synagogues in New York, New Jersey, and California, advertising the sale of Palestinian land in Gaza that the Israeli government plans to redevelop as settlements — citing Jared Kushner's comments about Gaza "beachfront property" as evidence the redevelopment was the plan rather than an accident.<Cite s="2024-08-12-danny-jones-loyal-officer-vs-dissident-spy" t="2:14:38" />

## 'He killed the two-state solution' (The Inquiry)

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says [Benjamin Netanyahu](/wiki/benjamin-netanyahu) *"very proudly said that he killed the two-state solution,"* leaving no chance of a real peace while Israel bombs Palestinian negotiating teams abroad. He adds that Trump, Biden, Obama and Bush all simply accept that Netanyahu *"is going to run American foreign policy."*<Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="41:21" /><Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="42:53" />

## Repeated requests to bomb Iran

Kiriakou says Netanyahu has repeatedly begged the United States to attack Iran going back to the George W. Bush administration, saying the U.S. came very close to attacking Iran in the final weeks of that administration and that Netanyahu "has dedicated his life to dragging the United States into a war with Iran."<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="2:13:27" /><Cite s="2022-07-23-the-darkened-hour" t="56:05" /> He says every single time Netanyahu came to Washington going back to the early 1990s — including when he was Israel's ambassador to the UN, before he was ever prime minister — he urged whichever president was in office to bomb Iran, and that every president said no until Trump agreed.<Cite s="2025-07-14-danny-jones" t="46:17" /><Cite s="2026-07-07-covert-strategies-revealed" t="42:16" /><Cite s="2025-10-16-truth-hurts-show" t="00:00" /> He has since said the U.S. ultimately went to war with Iran because of Israel: Iran, in his assessment, posed no threat to the United States, and Israel had been pushing for the U.S. to bomb it since the 1980s, with every Israeli prime minister making the same request regardless of who was U.S. president.<Cite s="2026-06-10-zeteo-abolish-the-cia" t="27:55" /> He notes that two National Intelligence Estimates, representing all 18 members of the U.S. intelligence community, found Iran did not have a nuclear weapons program, and that Ayatollah Khamenei had issued a 2003 fatwa declaring nuclear weapons a sin.<Cite s="2026-06-10-zeteo-abolish-the-cia" t="29:29" /> Kiriakou has acknowledged he was wrong to have told Joe Rogan in October 2025 that he thought Trump would make peace with Iran in pursuit of a Nobel Peace Prize; he says Trump has since stated publicly that he no longer cares about peace since he did not receive the prize.<Cite s="2026-06-10-zeteo-abolish-the-cia" t="29:59" /> Kiriakou says Netanyahu convinced Trump that the Iranian government was a *"house of cards"* that would collapse as soon as hostilities began, and that — in a break from past practice — the U.S. intelligence community did not even attempt to manufacture public consent for the resulting war.<Cite s="2026-06-10-zeteo-abolish-the-cia" t="31:34" /> He has separately noted that CIA Director [John Ratcliffe](/wiki/john-ratcliffe) traveled to Cuba about a week before one such interview, which Kiriakou interprets not as back-channel diplomacy but as a warning of coming regime-change action, comparing it to sending the CIA director to warn [Muammar Gaddafi](/wiki/muammar-gaddafi) before Libya's overthrow.<Cite s="2026-06-10-zeteo-abolish-the-cia" t="32:36" />

Kiriakou also recounts that Barack Obama recently stated Netanyahu asked him to bomb Iran and threatened that if the U.S. refused, Israel would use nuclear weapons — a bluff Obama called by telling him to go ahead, after which Netanyahu backed down.<Cite s="2026-07-07-covert-strategies-revealed" t="42:46" /> Kiriakou says he believes a peaceful transition of power in Iran serves U.S. interests, while chaos and death serve Israel's interests, and that he believes Netanyahu is hoping for an Iranian civil war — modeled, in his view, on what Israel achieved in Libya: chaos, civil war, and as many Iranian deaths as possible.<Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-w-ted-rall" t="07:49" /><Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-w-ted-rall" t="36:52" /> He describes this chaos-focused strategy as a short-term view: Israel believes that keeping the region in a state of chaos prevents its neighbors from developing nuclear weapons, temporarily protecting Israel, rather than pursuing lasting security.<Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-w-ted-rall" t="52:28" /> During the strikes on Iran, Kiriakou reported that Netanyahu characteristically left the country — as he says Netanyahu always does whenever Israel plans a bombing campaign — with most reports placing him in Germany, and one report suggesting Cyprus.<Cite s="2026-03-01-deprogram-w-ted-rall" t="30:12" />

## The judicial takeover and the summer before October 7

Kiriakou treats the months immediately preceding [7 October](/wiki/october-7-attack) as politically decisive rather than incidental. Netanyahu seized control of the judiciary five weeks before the attack — timing Kiriakou calls *"quite interesting"* — and the response was demonstrations in Tel Aviv and Haifa *"that were unprecedented in scale."* Israel, in his account, came close to civil war that summer, and Netanyahu came *"this close to being overthrown and having to call new elections."*<Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="53:12" /><Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="53:44" />

He puts the arithmetic to a specific use. Because of Israel's party system, he says, *"Benjamin Netanyahu has never won more than 27% of the vote in Israel"* — from which he draws the reductio he aims at the charge of antisemitism: if disagreeing with Netanyahu as a person makes a critic antisemitic, then *"are we supposed to assume that 73% of Israelis are anti-Semitic?"*<Cite s="2026-07-10-kiriakou-takes-ana-cenk-behind-the-curtain" t="38:17" /> He adds that other leaders elected on comparable minorities have gone on to do great damage.<Cite s="2026-07-10-kiriakou-takes-ana-cenk-behind-the-curtain" t="38:17" />

He sets that against the arithmetic of the coalition, noting that Netanyahu wins government with roughly a quarter of the vote, and characterises the resulting cabinet — with [Bezalel Smotrich](/wiki/bezalel-smotrich) at finance and over the West Bank and [Itamar Ben-Gvir](/wiki/itamar-ben-gvir) at national security — as *"the most extreme right-wing government in the history of Israel."*<Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="27:29" /><Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="53:12" />

## The aid phase-out as messaging

Reacting to Netanyahu's eulogy for [Lindsey Graham](/wiki/lindsey-graham) — in which Netanyahu recounted telling Graham that Israel could finance its own defence and phase American military support down to zero, and Graham "went ballistic" — [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) reads the remark as a deliberate and subtle political move most Americans would miss.<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="10:03" /><Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="11:05" />

His reasoning: Netanyahu knows most Americans dislike foreign aid, and Israel is the largest recipient of it. Announcing that Israel no longer wants the money is, in Kiriakou's reading, an attempt to turn American public opinion back toward Israel rather than a genuine concession.<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="11:37" /><Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="12:40" /> Kiriakou adds that he personally favours foreign aid as cheap soft power, but that a first-world economy co-located with American military and intelligence officials — and running spies across the country to steal American secrets — does not need it.<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="12:09" /><Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="12:40" />

On Netanyahu's domestic standing, Kiriakou notes that because of Israel's party system Netanyahu has never won more than 27 per cent of the vote — a point he raises against the practice of labelling his critics antisemitic.<Cite s="2026-07-10-the-young-turks-former-cia-agent-explains-why-we-need-to-brea" t="08:22" /> Asked for a one-line assessment, Kiriakou called him "probably the foremost war criminal on the planet."<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="24:58" />

## Waters on Netanyahu and Patagonia

Interviewed by [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) in 2026, [Roger Waters](/wiki/roger-waters) said he believed — on circumstantial evidence — that senior Israeli figures were already looking at Patagonia as a refuge for the point at which they expect prices to be put on their heads, and that the prospect of Netanyahu living out his life in Argentina *"actually makes me happy so long as they're not in the Holy Land."*<Cite s="2026-07-04-the-jk-report-roger-waters-on-palestine-human-rights" t="30:18" /><Cite s="2026-07-04-the-jk-report-roger-waters-on-palestine-human-rights" t="30:50" /> He named [Itamar Ben-Gvir](/wiki/itamar-ben-gvir) and [Bezalel Smotrich](/wiki/bezalel-smotrich) in the same breath, and said retribution sat *"at the bottom of my list"* — above it, the care owed to any single surviving child in Gaza.<Cite s="2026-07-04-the-jk-report-roger-waters-on-palestine-human-rights" t="30:50" /><Cite s="2026-07-04-the-jk-report-roger-waters-on-palestine-human-rights" t="31:20" />

Waters also speculated about Netanyahu's formation, saying that whatever produced him must lie in a childhood *"growing up in Brooklyn"* about which little is heard, and that he would like to hear a specialist in childhood trauma account for it.<Cite s="2026-07-04-the-jk-report-roger-waters-on-palestine-human-rights" t="43:16" />

## The house-of-cards premise

Kiriakou identifies the operating assumption behind the 2026 [Iran war](/wiki/2026-iran-war) — that the Iranian government *"would collapse like a house of cards"* as soon as the first missile flew — as originating with Netanyahu rather than with American analysis: *"anybody with any brains at all knew that was ridiculous. And that was a Netanyahu talking point going back to the 1990s."* He says the president was convinced of it easily, by all reports.<Cite s="2026-06-27-deep-focus-report-john-kiriakou-on-iran-endless-wars-and-u-s-fo" t="1:03" /><Cite s="2026-06-27-deep-focus-report-john-kiriakou-on-iran-endless-wars-and-u-s-fo" t="1:34" />

## Seven visits, and the six-month claim

Kiriakou has quantified the frequency of the requests. Israeli prime ministers ordinarily come to Washington once or twice a year; Netanyahu came seven times in the year to March 2026. Every visit, regardless of prime minister or president, produced the same request to bomb Iran, going back to the 1980s — and every president refused.<Cite s="2026-03-24-michael-franzese-iran-israel-epstein-are-all-connected" t="23:58" />

He describes the accompanying claim as a standing joke inside the [CIA](/wiki/cia): Netanyahu is always invited to address Congress, which is itself unusual, and every time he says the Iranians are six months from a nuclear weapon. *"Well, you've been saying that since 1986, and they're still six months away from a nuclear weapon. Well, they're not. They never were."*<Cite s="2026-03-24-michael-franzese-iran-israel-epstein-are-all-connected" t="26:41" /><Cite s="2026-03-24-michael-franzese-iran-israel-epstein-are-all-connected" t="27:13" />

Kiriakou's account of how the standoff broke is [Netanyahu's threat to Trump](/wiki/netanyahu-nuclear-threat-to-trump): that Israel would use nuclear weapons against Iran if the United States did not act.<Cite s="2026-03-24-michael-franzese-iran-israel-epstein-are-all-connected" t="25:04" />

## The closed meeting and the granite wall (2026)

After [Lindsey Graham](/wiki/lindsey-graham)'s funeral, Netanyahu went to the White House for a closed-press meeting with [Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump), then gave an exclusive interview to Sean Hannity. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) treats the press exclusion as the tell: *"We're We're talking about two major world leaders who love love love to get in front of cameras. So, why were two of the most camera-happy leaders in the world afraid to answer questions? I think it's because the reality is the relationship between Donald Trump Benjamin Netanyahu has never been any more tense."*<Cite s="2026-07-30-john-kiriakou-is-it-over-for-netanyahu-poll-shows-most-amer" t="04:40" /><Cite s="2026-07-30-john-kiriakou-is-it-over-for-netanyahu-poll-shows-most-amer" t="05:11" /><Cite s="2026-07-30-john-kiriakou-is-it-over-for-netanyahu-poll-shows-most-amer" t="05:41" />

Netanyahu's public account was the opposite. The meeting was *"one one of the best meetings that we've had"*, and critics looking for cracks in the alliance would find instead *"a wall of granite, cuz the president is so clear."*<Cite s="2026-07-30-john-kiriakou-is-it-over-for-netanyahu-poll-shows-most-amer" t="06:11" /> Trump's own next-morning television appearance undercut it on both [Pickaxe Mountain](/wiki/pickaxe-mountain) and the [F-35 sale to Turkey](/wiki/f-35-sale-to-turkey).

Kiriakou is equally unimpressed by the denial issued by Netanyahu's communications director — that Israel is not pushing the United States in any direction on Iran and *"is not telling the US president what to do"*. His response: *"You have to be utterly brain dead to believe a single word of that... Does that sound like something you would say if you were not pushing your friend in a certain direction? Would you deny it multiple times in a few sentences?"* Trump's verdict afterwards — *"Excellent meeting... Bibi understands now"* — Kiriakou reads as confirmation of a rebuke: *"That tells me that the president slapped him down."*<Cite s="2026-07-30-john-kiriakou-is-it-over-for-netanyahu-poll-shows-most-amer" t="14:00" /><Cite s="2026-07-30-john-kiriakou-is-it-over-for-netanyahu-poll-shows-most-amer" t="14:30" /><Cite s="2026-07-30-john-kiriakou-is-it-over-for-netanyahu-poll-shows-most-amer" t="15:01" />

## The same speech, three wars

Kiriakou treats Netanyahu's congressional testimony as a single repeated argument. In 2002 he told Congress Saddam Hussein was an existential threat to Israel and the United States and that overthrowing him would bring peace to the Middle East — *"And then there wasn't."* In 2008 or 2009 the same case was made about Muammar Gaddafi: *"we did that, and it just made everything worse."* A year before the broadcast the argument was made again about the Iranian government. *"No, we won't."*<Cite s="2026-07-30-john-kiriakou-is-it-over-for-netanyahu-poll-shows-most-amer" t="09:52" /><Cite s="2026-07-30-john-kiriakou-is-it-over-for-netanyahu-poll-shows-most-amer" t="10:24" />

On the [International Criminal Court](/wiki/international-criminal-court), Kiriakou notes the charges Netanyahu faces are war crimes and crimes against humanity, and describes his Hannity interview about the risk of arrest abroad as *"crocodile tears"*.<Cite s="2026-07-30-john-kiriakou-is-it-over-for-netanyahu-poll-shows-most-amer" t="17:37" />
