**UK secrecy laws** are, in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s view, even tougher on whistleblowers and dissent than their American equivalents. Britain's Official Secrets Act and Terrorism Act, he says, mean that merely saying you *"support Palestinian human rights"* can bring prosecution, or being stopped at an airport and told that while not under arrest, you are *"also not free to go."*<Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="18:50" /><Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="19:23" /> He cites three friends, one a former British ambassador, stopped and threatened under those acts, calling the regime *"draconian"* — at least in the U.S., he says, the [Espionage Act](/wiki/cia-torture-program-whistleblowing) gives *"a fighting chance."*<Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="19:54" /> He pairs Britain's treatment of pro-Palestinian speech, connected to the [BDS movement](/wiki/bds-movement), with a Florida law making similar social-media posts illegal, under which Miami police visited a woman over a Facebook post.<Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="20:25" /><Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="20:57" />
