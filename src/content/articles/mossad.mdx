The **Mossad** is the primary external intelligence service of the State of Israel. In [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account — drawn from his fifteen years at the [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia) and his subsequent decades as a commentator — the Mossad is operationally formidable, organizationally distinctive in its near-total focus on operations rather than analysis, and uniformly hostile in its professional dealings with U.S. counterparts:

> My experience is universally negative. Universally negative. I've never had a positive encounter with Mossad. I have never met a CIA officer who has had a positive encounter with Mossad. Because the Mossad doesn't give two shits what you think or what you are trying to focus on in your job. They care only about Israel. And if that means shoving their fists up your ass or trying to recruit you or telling you to go fuck yourself while they try to recruit the guy sitting next to you or the guy in the defense contractor's office, they don't care what you think of them because for them it's an issue of survival.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="2:43:09" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="2:43:40" />

## Operational orientation

Mossad is described as nearly exclusively operational rather than analytical — *"It's all about ops"* — a posture conditioned by the need to manage the heterogeneous Palestinian political environment in Israel's immediate neighborhood (the Palestinian Authority, Hamas, the Quds Force, Islamic Jihad, and others). Per Kiriakou, the requirements of *"allowing a simmer"* among these factions without permitting it to boil compel constant infiltration and active management.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="1:21:29" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="1:22:00" />

## CIA prohibition on counter-spying

A categorical CIA prohibition on intelligence operations against Israel was in effect during Kiriakou's tenure: *"We're not permitted to spy on the Israelis. We're not permitted. The potential for blowback is outrageous. Can you imagine if we spied on the Israelis and got caught? Every member of Congress would be demanding the CIA director's head."* The prohibition reflects [AIPAC](/wiki/aipac)'s political reach and is treated by Kiriakou as the structural exception in the U.S. intelligence community's posture toward other states.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:12:04" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:12:35" />

## Israeli spying on the United States

In a Piers Morgan appearance in approximately 2024 Kiriakou debated a former Mossad director who denied any post-1985 Israeli espionage against the United States. Kiriakou's response was that [Jonathan Pollard](/wiki/jonathan-pollard) had been caught in 1985 — *"and you were spying on the United States in 1998, you know, or 2004."* The former director declined to comment.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:11:32" />

## Bug-laden gift practice and the off-campus meeting protocol

A documented practice of Mossad liaison delegations to the [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia) headquarters in the 1990s was to bring gifts at the start of each visit — gifts which routinely turned out, on inspection, to contain functioning listening devices. Per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou):

> Every time they would come, they'd say, 'Hey, we brought gifts. Here's a gift for you.' And it's all packed full of listening devices and batteries — every one of them. … And we'd say, 'You guys, you can't come back here every single time and try to bug our conference rooms.' What'd they say to that? 'Oh, sorry. We're not sure how that happened.'<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="2:27:53" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="2:28:25" />

The agency's response, by the 1990s, was to relocate all U.S.–Israeli liaison meetings off the CIA campus to a rented external office. *"So we rent an office where we meet the Israelis off campus. Yeah — 'cuz you just can't trust them."*<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="2:28:25" />

## The Shin Bet recruitment-attempt protocol

A separate documented pattern is Mossad's (and Shin Bet's) institutional practice of attempting recruitment of every U.S. CIA officer they encounter in a liaison setting. Kiriakou records this happening to him on his first liaison briefing — held within two and a half months of his joining the agency — when a Shin Bet officer asked him to spell his surname, noted it down, looked at him over his glasses, and said: *"You are uh Jewish."* Kiriakou's response: *"I am not recruitable. Don't even think about trying to recruit me."* On returning to the office, his furious report to his boss was met with general laughter and the observation that *"they've done that to every single one of us. It's like they can't help themselves."*<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="2:27:53" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="2:29:30" />

## Documented operational style

Two operations are routinely cited by Kiriakou as exemplifying the Mossad's operational character:

- The 2010 assassination of [Mahmoud al-Mabhouh](/wiki/mahmoud-al-mabhouh) in a Dubai hotel room — including the unresolved question of how the assassins locked the door from the inside after the killing.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:00:25" />
- The 1997 attempted assassination of [Khaled Mashal](/wiki/khaled-mashal) by poison in Jordan.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="1:31:02" />
- During the [Iran 12-Day War](/wiki/iran-12-day-war), the Farsi-language phone-call campaign to Iranian nuclear scientists by Iranian-Jewish Mossad and Shin Bet officers, followed by apartment-block strikes that killed fourteen scientists and their families.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="2:58:19" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="2:59:21" />

## Coercion and surveillance

Unlike the [CIA](/wiki/cia), which Kiriakou describes as having moved away from coercive recruitment methods in the 1970s, [Mossad](/wiki/mossad) uses coercion as a standard tool. In the context of the [Jeffrey Epstein](/wiki/jeffrey-epstein) operation: *"The Israelis do use coercion. Then you wire every room including the bathrooms for both audio and video. Just in case you need to smash somebody over the head."*<Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="54:55" />

## Morality

Kiriakou's assessment: *"I think for the most part, most intelligence agencies have zero morals. I think that most intelligence agencies also will draw the line somewhere. Like with child sex, for example. That would never happen at the CIA or at any other intelligence service that I'm aware of. And I think the Israelis were comfortable with it."*<Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:01:07" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:01:38" />

## Espionage inside the United States

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) has stated that [Mossad](/wiki/mossad) actively conducts espionage operations inside the United States, placing hundreds of intelligence officers primarily within American defense contractors. According to Kiriakou, the United States shares approximately ninety-nine percent of its defense industry technology with [Israel](/wiki/israeli-nuclear-program) through official channels; Mossad's domestic intelligence effort is directed at obtaining the remaining one percent that Washington withholds.<Cite s="2026-05-19-ffn-jeff-dornik-swamps-holy-mess" t="7:49" /> *"We give 99% of what we have in the defense industry to Israel. And they are out there stealing that last 1% that we're withholding from them."*<Cite s="2026-05-19-ffn-jeff-dornik-swamps-holy-mess" t="8:21" />

## Most impressive intelligence service

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) said that of all the intelligence services he encountered during his [CIA](/wiki/cia) career, the Israelis were the most impressive — specifically because they have no rules: *"They'll kill anybody."*<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:15:00" />

## Afghan-refugee network — Iran operation

Kiriakou described [Mossad](/wiki/mossad)'s operation against Iranian military and scientific leadership during what he called *"the 12-day war."* Iran housed more than two million Afghan refugees who, as undocumented residents, could not access medical care or the welfare system and survived by begging. The Israelis approached these refugees and offered $200 a month in exchange for information: the home addresses and cell-phone numbers of Iranian generals and nuclear scientists. The Israelis killed the top twelve generals across the entire Iranian military and nearly every nuclear scientist by geolocating their cell phones and firing missiles at the coordinates. When Iran ordered senior military officials and scientists to stop carrying cell phones, the Israelis began tracking the bodyguards instead — who had not received the same order — and killed them alongside the principals.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:09:30" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:10:00" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:10:30" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:11:00" />

## Pager operation — Hezbollah

Kiriakou described the [Mossad](/wiki/mossad) pager operation against Hezbollah in detail. Knowing Hezbollah had switched to pagers to avoid cell-phone interception, the Israelis purchased a pager-manufacturing company in Hungary, ensured Hezbollah ordered pagers from that company, inserted explosives into the devices during manufacturing, and allowed the pagers to route through Taiwan, Thailand, and Syria before reaching Lebanon. The Israelis then simultaneously activated all the explosives, killing every Hezbollah official of consequence. Those not killed in the pager explosions were killed when the Israelis bombed the apartment buildings where they lived.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:15:30" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:16:00" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:16:30" />

Kiriakou's characterization: *"totally illegal"* and *"a work of genius."*

## Gift bugging at CIA liaison meetings

The [CIA](/wiki/cia) does not allow Israeli intelligence officers inside CIA headquarters because *"they spy on us."* Every time Israelis came for liaison meetings, they brought gifts that, on X-ray inspection, contained listening devices packed with two years of batteries. The CIA would confront them — *"You have to stop doing this. Every time you come here, you try to bug our conference rooms"* — and the Israeli response was: *"We knew you'd find it. We're just kidding."* Meetings with Israeli counterparts are held miles from headquarters in rented spaces.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:17:30" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:18:00" />

Kiriakou also described his very first [CIA](/wiki/cia) liaison briefing — to the Israelis — at which the Shin Bet officer, upon hearing his name, immediately told him he was Jewish and attempted to recruit him on the spot. Kiriakou refused; his colleagues told him the Israelis do that to everyone.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:19:00" />

## 187 undeclared officers / the 5% they steal

The [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s director of security told Kiriakou's new-hire class that the FBI had identified 187 undeclared Israeli intelligence officers operating across the United States, concentrated at defense contractors. Kiriakou noted the paradox: the United States gives Israel approximately 95% of its defense secrets — F-35 designs, advanced missile systems — and [Mossad](/wiki/mossad) steals the remaining 5%.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:20:30" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:21:00" />

## Peter Mandelson and Prince Andrew — classified-document collection

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) cited the cases of [Peter Mandelson](/wiki/peter-mandelson), former British ambassador to the United States, and former Prince Andrew as evidence that [Jeffrey Epstein](/wiki/jeffrey-epstein) was collecting classified defense material on behalf of [Mossad](/wiki/mossad). Mandelson initially denied ever meeting Epstein, then said he may have met him once at a party, and was subsequently shown a photograph of himself in his underwear with a sixteen-year-old girl on Epstein's island. Kiriakou stated that Mandelson gave Epstein classified documents from the UK's Defense Ministry. He added that it was revealed shortly before the interview that Prince Andrew had also given Epstein classified Defense Ministry documents.<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="17:00" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="17:30" />

## Ehud Barak and Yoni Corin

Kiriakou cited reporting that former Israeli Prime Minister Ehud Barak had a monthly check-in with [Epstein](/wiki/jeffrey-epstein) at Epstein's Manhattan property, and that an Israeli intelligence figure named Yoni Corin also paid regular visits. Kiriakou further noted that Epstein introduced Benjamin Netanyahu to Chase Bank for financial relations.<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="17:00" />

## CIA married couple in Jerusalem — harassment campaign

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described a harassment campaign conducted by [Mossad](/wiki/mossad) against a married couple, both [CIA](/wiki/cia) officers, who were assigned to the U.S. consulate in Jerusalem. At a welcome reception hosted by the U.S. ambassador, the couple was greeted appropriately. When they returned home afterward, all of their living-room furniture had been rearranged. Kiriakou's interpretation: *"the message being we can do whatever we want and there's nothing you can do about it."*<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="1:05:00" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="1:05:30" />

On a second occasion, the couple attended the ambassador's Christmas party. When they returned home, people had defecated in all four of their bathrooms and left the toilets unflushed.<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="1:06:00" />

As they were preparing to leave their posting, the ambassador hosted a farewell party for them. When they returned home after the party, someone had broken into the house and cut off the dog's tail, wrapping the wound in gauze and medical tape. The dog was found whimpering under the dining-room table. Kiriakou's characterization: *"We're supposed to be allies. God knows the Israelis ask us for everything under the sun — and then that's how you treat our people."*<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="1:06:00" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="1:06:30" />

## Christmas gifts stolen during intelligence exchange

A colleague of Kiriakou's who was excited to visit Israel went to [Mossad](/wiki/mossad) for a standard intelligence exchange — sharing what the [CIA](/wiki/cia) was doing on a particular issue, receiving an update on what Mossad was doing. It was the Christmas season and the officer had spent approximately $1,000 on Christmas presents. When he returned to his hotel room, all of the presents had been stolen. His suitcase was empty.<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="1:07:00" />

## First and only meeting with Mossad

Kiriakou described his one personal interaction with [Mossad](/wiki/mossad), which occurred six weeks into his [CIA](/wiki/cia) career in 1990. He said the Mossad officers were *"so offensive and so aggressive"* that he returned to CIA headquarters and told his boss: *"Never again. I will never work with the Israelis ever again."* He kept that resolution throughout his career. During the meeting, one Mossad officer — peering at him over the top of his glasses — asked how to spell Kiriakou's name and then declared: *"You are Jewish."* Kiriakou's response: *"Oh, don't you dare. I am not recruitable. Don't even think about it."*<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="1:03:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="1:04:00" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="1:04:30" />

## Cultural assessment

Kiriakou's characterization of [Mossad](/wiki/mossad) and Israeli intelligence culture: *"The Israelis really believe that they're alone in the world, even though they're not. And because they've convinced themselves that they're alone in the world, they treat everybody else with disrespect."*<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="1:07:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="1:08:00" />
