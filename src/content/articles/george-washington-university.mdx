**George Washington University** in Washington, D.C., is where [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s intelligence career began. He left western Pennsylvania at eighteen to attend it, took a bachelor's degree in Middle Eastern studies, became fluent in Greek there, and stayed on for a master's programme in legislative affairs, expecting to end up on Capitol Hill, in the Foreign Service, or as a lobbyist.<Cite s="2016-04-25-fort-collins-community-act-john-kiriakou-cia-whistle-blower-part-1" t="00:32" /><Cite s="2019-03-04-a-good-place-with-ella-a-a-good-place-with-ella-a" t="56:01" /><Cite s="2023-02-18-savage-joy-marie-mann-savage-joy-and-john-kiriakou" t="04:16" />

## The psychology of leadership

The recruitment ran through a graduate class called the Psychology of Leadership, which Kiriakou describes as the best class he had in graduate school. It was taught by [Jerome Post](/wiki/jerome-post), an eminent psychiatrist who held a doctorate in political science, a doctorate in psychology and a medical degree, and who called himself a political psychiatrist.<Cite s="2019-06-19-soundwaves-2000" t="02:51" /><Cite s="2019-10-03-kiriakou-on-the-cia-obama-vs-trump" t="01:02" /><Cite s="2022-09-13-hr-news-channel-hr-news-channel" t="04:15" /> Post was working undercover as a graduate adviser, looking for students who might fit the [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s culture; he liked Kiriakou's writing style and asked whether he had ever thought of applying.<Cite s="2019-08-08-david-gornoski-archives-david-gornoski-archives" t="03:10" /><Cite s="2019-03-14-salem-access-tv-public" t="25:31" />

Kiriakou is emphatic that the arrangement could not happen today — the practice was made illegal by equal-employment law — but that in the late 1980s there really was an old-boy network, and one could not simply apply to the CIA: one had to be spotted and recruited.<Cite s="2022-04-07-david-gornoski-explores-life-as-a-spy" t="03:08" /><Cite s="2019-08-08-david-gornoski-archives-david-gornoski-archives" t="03:10" />

## The tests on campus

The agency's screening began on the university's own premises. Sent to meet a CIA personnel officer he knew only as Bob — a man he describes as six foot five and 350 pounds — Kiriakou was told to be at the George Washington University medical centre auditorium at eight o'clock on a Saturday morning to take some tests.<Cite s="2015-04-17-the-real-news-network-the-real-news-network" t="04:00" /> Written examinations, medical examinations, financial and security vetting and a [polygraph](/wiki/polygraph) followed over roughly nine months before he entered on duty.<Cite s="2019-06-19-soundwaves-2000" t="04:33" /><Cite s="2015-07-25-splendour-in-the-grass" t="01:37" />

## Afterwards

The university recurs on the far side of his career. When Kiriakou submitted *The Reluctant Spy* to the [Publications Review Board](/wiki/publications-review-board), the fact that he had attended George Washington University was among the details initially redacted; he appealed on the ground that it was not classified, and the chapter was cleared.<Cite s="2019-06-19-soundwaves-2000" t="26:31" /> He later taught a class at the university on surveillance and surveillance detection — and noticed, in the course of it, that the FBI was following him.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="1:11:38" />

The institution also supplies him with a recurring measure of political weightlessness and of political energy. Of Juan Guaidó he observes that a graduate student from George Washington University *"right down the street"* was a nobody whom even Venezuelans had never heard of until Washington decided he would be president.<Cite s="2019-05-01-potkaars-podcast-potkaars-podcast" t="37:28" /> Of Mike Gravel's 2020 presidential campaign, for which Kiriakou served as foreign policy adviser, he notes that the campaign manager was a twenty-year-old from George Washington University and that the students running its social media built one of the biggest crazes in political Twitter.<Cite s="2023-06-26-kevin-gosztola" t="17:39" />
