**The AIPAC "Holy Land" trip offer** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account of being approached by [AIPAC](/wiki/aipac) shortly after taking up the post of chief investigator on the [Senate Foreign Relations Committee](/wiki/senate-foreign-relations-committee), a job he held from 2009 to 2011.<Cite s="2026-04-03-megyn-kelly-megyn-kelly" t="04:41" />

A couple of days after he started, two men *"clearly, obviously from AIPAC"* came in and welcomed him to Capitol Hill. He replied that he had worked there before. They then offered him an all-expenses-paid trip *"to the Holy Land"* — and Kiriakou's emphasis falls on the phrasing: *"They didn't say Israel."*<Cite s="2026-04-03-megyn-kelly-megyn-kelly" t="04:41" />

He declined, saying he could pay for his own vacations. The offer was pressed — it was all expenses paid, and they would take him to all the Christian holy sites — and he declined again: *"Guys, I'm not taking AIPAC's money. I'm not interested."*<Cite s="2026-04-03-megyn-kelly-megyn-kelly" t="05:13" />

## A permanent presence

Kiriakou's point in telling the story is that the approach was not a one-off but a fixture of the building. *"I don't think a single day went by where I didn't see those two. They practically had offices up there."*<Cite s="2026-04-03-megyn-kelly-megyn-kelly" t="05:13" />

He reports the same arrangement inside individual members' offices, on the account of House members themselves: AIPAC representatives would come to the office every day and sit on the couch, and when constituents arrived to meet staff or the member, *"these guys will get up like it's their office and say, 'Remember Israel. Israel, Israel, Israel. Remember.'"* His verdict: *"That's not the American way."*<Cite s="2026-04-03-megyn-kelly-megyn-kelly" t="05:43" />
