**Sleep deprivation** was among the techniques approved by the U.S. Department of Justice and applied by the [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia) to high-value detainees as part of the post-September 11 [enhanced interrogation](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation) program. It is considered, in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s assessment, *"clearly worse than waterboarding."*<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="00:00" /><Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="03:38" />

## Physiology

Research by the American Psychological Association on sleep deprivation found that humans *"begin to go insane at seven days with no sleep"* and begin to die of organ failure around day nine.<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="03:38" /> Kiriakou has cited this same seven-day/nine-day timeline repeatedly and consistently across interviews, sometimes giving the CIA's authorized ceiling as 12 days and elsewhere as 14 — in one account, *"14 days beyond the point of death"* some prisoners were already suffering.<Cite s="2023-12-07-truthovercomfort" t="08:54" /><Cite s="2022-05-19-scheerpost" t="27:57" /><Cite s="2017-05-23-podcast-ufo-live-shows" t="24:44" /><Cite s="2016-07-11-adventures-in-the-free-sta" t="18:59" />

## CIA authorization and practice

The CIA was authorized to keep prisoners awake for up to **12 days** — well past the 7-day onset of clinical insanity established by the APA literature. *"People died from sleep deprivation."*<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="04:09" /> Kiriakou says the technique killed at least one prisoner outright.<Cite s="2017-05-23-podcast-ufo-live-shows" t="24:44" /><Cite s="2016-07-11-adventures-in-the-free-sta" t="19:31" />

The 2014 [Senate Torture Report](/wiki/senate-torture-report) documented sleep deprivation of up to **180 hours** (approximately 7.5 days) with prisoners *"hands shackled above their heads"* in stress positions throughout.<Cite s="2015-02-09-democracy-now-torture-report" t="00:13" />

## Documented permanent psychiatric injury

[Ramzi bin al-Shibh](/wiki/ramzi-bin-al-shibh), a September 11 co-conspirator, was rendered **permanently clinically insane** by CIA sleep deprivation. The diagnosis is the Pentagon's own — *"the Pentagon's psychiatrist who's saying he's insane, not his defense attorneys"* — and bin al-Shibh has been declared by the Department of Defense as unable to be tried.<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="05:43" /><Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="06:16" />

## APA timeline and CIA authorization

According to [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), the American Psychological Association's research on sleep deprivation established the following timeline: subjects begin to lose their minds by day seven; by day nine they begin to die; by day eleven, organ systems begin shutting down. The [CIA](/wiki/cia) authorized sleep deprivation for up to twelve days.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="27:10" />

## Mechanics

Kiriakou described the sleep-deprivation setup used in the [enhanced interrogation program](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation): the prisoner is chained to an eyebolt in the ceiling, unable to sit, lie down, or otherwise get comfortable, while strong industrial-strength lights are kept on continuously and music — Kiriakou has variously described it as death metal, hard rock, or loops alternated with children's jingles — plays at high volume twenty-four hours a day.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="27:30" /><Cite s="2023-12-07-truthovercomfort" t="09:27" /><Cite s="2022-05-19-scheerpost" t="26:56" /><Cite s="2017-05-23-podcast-ufo-live-shows" t="25:46" /> In a separate description of the same setup, Kiriakou said the explicit purpose was to drive the prisoner insane: *"That's exactly what happened. We made them crazy."*<Cite s="2026-04-20-dead-drop-s1e24-moment-of-truth" t="08:30" /> In some cases the insanity became permanent, leaving prisoners unable to participate in their own legal defense.<Cite s="2015-11-14-robin-hensel" t="08:54" />

## Rumsfeld's standup desk

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says [Donald Rumsfeld](/wiki/donald-rumsfeld) — who worked at a standup desk and would go 24 to 36 hours without sitting down — famously claimed he didn't believe sleep deprivation was even a real form of punishment, missing what the program actually did: keeping prisoners chained upright and awake for up to 12 days, past the point — per American Psychological Association studies — where people begin losing their minds at day seven and dying of organ failure by day nine.<Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="1:24:29" /><Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="1:24:59" /><Cite s="2026-02-13-o-keefe-media-group" t="31:30" />

## "You might as well just kill the guy"

A May 2026 account gives the physiological threshold consistently with his long-standing figure, and draws the conclusion more bluntly than usual. Sleep deprivation, he says, takes far longer to break a prisoner than the [cold cell](/wiki/cold-cell) does: *"you need to go 8, 9 days without sleep. Then he goes into organ failure."*<Cite s="2026-05-17-the-matan-show-matan-confronts-kiriakou-israel" t="19:37" />

His gloss on holding someone to that point was that it collapses the distinction between interrogation and killing — *"you might as well just kill the guy at that point"* — and when the interviewer took that as hyperbole, Kiriakou did not soften it: that was *"kind of the end result half the time."*<Cite s="2026-05-17-the-matan-show-matan-confronts-kiriakou-israel" t="19:37" />
