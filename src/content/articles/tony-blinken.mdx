**Tony Blinken** served as United States secretary of state under [Joe Biden](/wiki/joe-biden). [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) worked with him on the [Senate Foreign Relations Committee](/wiki/senate-foreign-relations-committee) and offers him as an illustration of how senior American foreign-policy appointments are made.<Cite s="2025-08-07-militaire-news-cia" t="26:31" />

## Kiriakou's assessment

Kiriakou is careful to separate the man from the appointment: *"I know Tony Blinken. I worked with Tony Blinken on the Senate Foreign Relations Committee. He's a very nice man."*<Cite s="2025-08-07-militaire-news-cia" t="26:31" />

The objection is to how he got there. *"He owes his entire adult life to Joe Biden. Every job he's ever had in his life has been working for Joe Biden."* On Kiriakou's reading, Blinken was not appointed *"because he's the smartest, most forward-thinking strategist that the United States could identify"* but because he had been Biden's friend and employee for thirty years. *"That's it."*<Cite s="2025-08-07-militaire-news-cia" t="26:31" /><Cite s="2025-08-07-militaire-news-cia" t="27:03" />

## The context of the criticism

Kiriakou raises Blinken as the third element of a three-part diagnosis of American decline abroad. The first is the reliance on the military; the second is the domestic hostility to foreign aid that leaves the United States with nothing to offer countries whose votes and cooperation it wants. The third is *"in many cases poor leadership."*<Cite s="2025-08-07-militaire-news-cia" t="26:31" />

He immediately pairs the point with his admiration for [Sergey Lavrov](/wiki/sergey-lavrov), whom he describes as having outperformed far better-resourced Western ambassadors at the United Nations — the comparison that gives the criticism its force.<Cite s="2025-08-07-militaire-news-cia" t="27:33" /><Cite s="2025-08-07-militaire-news-cia" t="28:37" />
