**Eric Holder** served as Attorney General of the United States under [Barack Obama](/wiki/barack-obama). The Justice Department he ran reopened the closed investigation of [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) and brought the January 2012 indictment that ended in a twenty-three-month prison sentence.

## The reopened case

The [FBI](/wiki/fbi) investigated Kiriakou from December 2007 to December 2008 over his [ABC News interview](/wiki/brian-ross), and the Bush administration's Justice Department then sent his attorneys a declination letter, reasoning that [Human Rights Watch](/wiki/human-rights-watch), [Amnesty International](/wiki/amnesty-international) and the International Committee of the Red Cross had all already published findings that the CIA was torturing prisoners, and that it was the worst-kept secret in Washington.<Cite s="2025-04-16-the-dr-phil-podcast-top-secrets-the-cia-torture-whistleblower-john-ki" t="50:15" /> Three weeks later Obama was inaugurated, and the case was reopened in secret.<Cite s="2025-04-16-the-dr-phil-podcast-top-secrets-the-cia-torture-whistleblower-john-ki" t="50:46" />

Who asked for it reopened is told three different ways across the corpus. In one account [John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan), newly installed as deputy national security adviser for counterterrorism, asked the Justice Department directly.<Cite s="2025-04-16-the-dr-phil-podcast-top-secrets-the-cia-torture-whistleblower-john-ki" t="50:46" /> In another it was the CIA that asked Holder.<Cite s="2017-03-07-flyingovertr0ut-re-upload-pt-1" t="4:46" /> In a third it was Obama himself who asked Holder to reopen the investigation, after which Holder's Justice Department brought three counts of espionage and one of violating the [Intelligence Identities Protection Act](/wiki/intelligence-identities-protection-act).<Cite s="2015-12-16-human-rights-defense-cente-human-rights-defense-cente" t="9:34" /> The variance has never been resolved in Kiriakou's telling.

## The memo

Fifteen thousand pages of discovery followed the indictment. Among them was a memo from Brennan to Holder instructing him to charge Kiriakou with espionage. Holder wrote back that his people did not think Kiriakou had committed espionage. Brennan wrote back again: charge him anyway, and make him defend himself.<Cite s="2025-04-16-the-dr-phil-podcast-top-secrets-the-cia-torture-whistleblower-john-ki" t="51:48" /> Kiriakou was charged in January 2012 with five felonies including three counts of espionage, a charge that can carry the death penalty; the espionage counts were dropped once he had gone bankrupt under $1.15 million in legal fees.<Cite s="2025-04-16-the-dr-phil-podcast-top-secrets-the-cia-torture-whistleblower-john-ki" t="51:16" /><Cite s="2025-04-16-the-dr-phil-podcast-top-secrets-the-cia-torture-whistleblower-john-ki" t="51:48" />

## The leak prosecutions

Between 1917 and 2009 three Americans were charged under the [Espionage Act](/wiki/espionage-act) for providing information to the press. Obama and Holder brought eight such prosecutions in a single administration.<Cite s="2017-03-07-flyingovertr0ut-re-upload-pt-1" t="9:01" /> Holder's Justice Department also threatened reporters with criminal prosecution as a matter of routine, and the reporters involved in Kiriakou's own case were actively threatened.<Cite s="2017-03-17-the-zero-hour-rj-eskow-the-zero-hour-rj-eskow" t="2:39" />
