The **2026 American operation against Venezuela** — in which special operations forces seized the sitting president and rendered him to New York for trial — is one [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) analysed primarily as a legal question, and answered in two directions at once.

## Illegal internationally

His objection to the act itself is plain: *"you can't just send a team of special operations forces into a country, snatch a sitting president out of his bed, and render him to New York City to face trial for his life on charges that may or may not be real charges."*<Cite s="2026-01-13-newsnation-whistleblower-details-uap-encounter-what-is-the" t="03:15" />

He then sets out the test. International law recognises three grounds on which one country may attack another: that the target *"presents a clear and present danger to the safety and security of Americans or American installations"*; that the target government invited the United States into its territory; or that the UN Security Council authorised it.<Cite s="2026-01-13-newsnation-whistleblower-details-uap-encounter-what-is-the" t="03:46" /><Cite s="2026-01-13-newsnation-whistleblower-details-uap-encounter-what-is-the" t="04:17" />

His conclusion: *"The US had none of those three things. So it's a violation of international law for this operation to have taken place in the first place."*<Cite s="2026-01-13-newsnation-whistleblower-details-uap-encounter-what-is-the" t="04:17" />

## Legal domestically

The distinction he draws immediately afterwards is the one that makes the case unusual: *"With that said, it's not a violation of US law."* He points to *"a very specific US law dating to 1985 that does allow US special forces to cross borders, snatch people, and bring them back to the United States to face trial."*<Cite s="2026-01-13-newsnation-whistleblower-details-uap-encounter-what-is-the" t="04:17" />

## "It seems to be working"

Kiriakou does not claim the operation failed. He judged the window for a Chinese or Russian response to have closed, leaving *"Trump got what he wanted to get, in addition to access to 300 billion barrels of oil, the largest proven oil reserves on the planet."*<Cite s="2026-01-13-newsnation-whistleblower-details-uap-encounter-what-is-the" t="04:49" />

Pressed on it, he conceded the point against his own preference: *"as much as I hate admitting it, this regime change seems to be working right now."*<Cite s="2026-01-13-newsnation-whistleblower-details-uap-encounter-what-is-the" t="05:20" />

The absence he finds most telling is Chinese: *"The Chinese have one aircraft carrier. They could have diverted that aircraft carrier at any point over the previous six months to the Caribbean. They didn't do that."*<Cite s="2026-01-13-newsnation-whistleblower-details-uap-encounter-what-is-the" t="05:51" />

## The sanctions that turned out to help

Kiriakou revises an earlier judgement in the same breath. In 2017 Trump's blanket sanctioning of the Venezuelan oil industry *"appeared to make a mistake, a strategic mistake."*<Cite s="2026-01-13-newsnation-whistleblower-details-uap-encounter-what-is-the" t="05:51" />

The reason it looked like one is the oil itself: Venezuelan crude is *"famously dirty… famously full of sulfur,"* requiring vast chemical injection, generally convertible only into home heating oil rather than gasoline, because refining it further is *"just too expensive."*<Cite s="2026-01-13-newsnation-whistleblower-details-uap-encounter-what-is-the" t="05:51" /><Cite s="2026-01-13-newsnation-whistleblower-details-uap-encounter-what-is-the" t="06:22" />

## Who he expects to be next

Asked in February 2026 about remarks on Cuba coming from the administration, Kiriakou gave a forecast he twice flagged as unwelcome to him: *"I hate to say this, but I think the Cuban government is the next one that falls."*<Cite s="2026-02-05-u-cast-studios-john-kiriakou-on-venezuela-the-maduro-trial-a" t="19:02" />

The reasons he gives are drawn from a visit rather than from analysis — his first trip to [Cuba](/wiki/kiriakou-cuba-trip), two years earlier, which left him describing *"an absolutely beautiful place, lovely people"* who *"want very much to get along well with the United States."* He notes the density of the family connection as the thing that ought to have settled the question long ago: *"literally not a single Cuban doesn't have family members living in the United States."* Against that, he traces the diplomatic record — relations restored under Barack Obama, ended and the embassies closed under Trump, and then, under Joe Biden, *"nothing. Biden did nothing."*<Cite s="2026-02-05-u-cast-studios-john-kiriakou-on-venezuela-the-maduro-trial-a" t="19:02" /><Cite s="2026-02-05-u-cast-studios-john-kiriakou-on-venezuela-the-maduro-trial-a" t="19:33" />
