**Thomas Massie** is a Republican member of Congress from Kentucky. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) has described him as a personal acquaintance — they have attended Washington Nationals baseball games together, introduced through attorney [Bruce Fein](/wiki/bruce-fine) — and as *"absolutely lovely"* with *"not a malicious hair on his head."*<Cite s="2026-05-19-ffn-jeff-dornik-swamps-holy-mess" t="47:54" />

Kiriakou has said Massie votes with the president more than ninety percent of the time, and that the minority of votes where he diverges represent positions most conservatives would agree with on reflection. In Kiriakou's analysis, the only plausible explanation for the [Israel lobby](/wiki/aipac) spending approximately $30 million to remove Massie from Congress is his opposition to U.S. funding of [Israel](/wiki/israeli-nuclear-program) and his opposition to foreign interventionist wars: *"America first is exactly the opposite of what the Israel lobby wants. They want Israel first."*<Cite s="2026-05-19-ffn-jeff-dornik-swamps-holy-mess" t="48:26" />

Massie's primary opponent at the time of Kiriakou's remarks was Ed Gallerin, a retired special forces veteran. Kiriakou described Gallerin as an attractive candidate in other contexts but expressed hope that he would lose Massie's specific district.<Cite s="2026-05-19-ffn-jeff-dornik-swamps-holy-mess" t="48:57" />

Kiriakou cites Massie's re-election itself as proof that [AIPAC](/wiki/aipac)'s strategy of primarying insufficiently pro-Israel incumbents does not always work as intended — *"Massie's one,"* he says, naming him as the exception to the pattern.<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="1:23:43" /> On policy, Kiriakou says Massie is a frequent and vocal advocate of the position that the Constitution is clear only Congress can declare war.<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="1:46:38" />

## The $35 million seat

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) cites a [Thomas Massie](/wiki/thomas-massie) primary in which two sides spent 35 million dollars — almost entirely outside money — on a single House seat paying 180,000 dollars a year. He says [AIPAC](/wiki/aipac) spent the money because Massie does not support Israel's agenda, part of a pattern of primarying any member who is not fully pro-Israel.<Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="17:18" /><Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="17:49" /><Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="18:20" />
