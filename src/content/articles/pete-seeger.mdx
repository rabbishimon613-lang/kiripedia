**Pete Seeger** was an American folk singer and, per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), *"besides my dad and my grandfather, Pete was the most important man in my life."*<Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="51:56" />

## Relationship with Kiriakou

Kiriakou and Seeger exchanged Christmas cards for years. When Kiriakou was arrested in 2012, Seeger rallied to his defense. Per Kiriakou, Seeger once asked him: *"You and I have the greatest differences in our backgrounds — like, what is it about me that is attracting you?"* Kiriakou's answer:<Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="52:26" />

> Pete, you stood up to them. In 1958, you stood up to them. All you had to say is, "On the advice of counsel, I decline to answer your question, Senator." And you didn't. You said, "Based on the First Amendment, I don't have to tell you with whom I associate." Everybody's pleading the Fifth — he pleaded the First, and they sent him to prison for it. It was overturned on appeal, but he wasn't afraid of them. And so I wanted to be like Pete.<Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="52:58" />

The biography of Seeger, *How Can I Keep From Singing?*, is Kiriakou's favorite book.<Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="51:56" />

## The Phil Ochs story

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) recounted a story [Pete Seeger](/wiki/pete-seeger) told him personally about the folk singer Phil Ochs. Ochs was a major figure in 1960s and 1970s folk music. On a concert tour of Africa, he was mugged; the attacker cut his throat. He survived, but was left unable to sing high notes. He fell into a deep depression.<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="1:05:41" />

One night, Seeger was performing at the Café Wha in Greenwich Village and had to run immediately after the show to catch the last train from Grand Central Station to Beacon, New York, where he lived. Just as he was packing up his banjo, a waitress called out that Phil Ochs was on the phone. Seeger grabbed the receiver, said *"Phil, I can't talk, I've got to run and catch the last train to Beacon — call me tomorrow,"* and hung up. Phil Ochs killed himself that night.<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="1:06:14" />

Seeger's reaction, as Kiriakou recounted it: *"That selfish prick. I'll never forgive him. He had no idea how much he was hurting the people who meant the most to him."*<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="1:06:46" />

Kiriakou used the story in the context of discussing suicide and resilience — as a direct address to people at their lowest point: *"You have no idea how loved you are and how much you would hurt the people that you care about the most."*<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="1:06:46" />

## Relationship with Kiriakou

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described [Pete Seeger](/wiki/pete-seeger) as the most important man in his life besides his father and grandfather. Kiriakou and Seeger exchanged Christmas cards for years. When Kiriakou came under federal prosecution, Seeger rallied publicly to his support.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="32:30" />

Seeger once asked Kiriakou why he was drawn to him — observing that their backgrounds were vastly different. Kiriakou's answer: *"You stood up to them. In 1958, you stood up to them."*<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="33:00" />

## 1958 HUAC appearance

In 1958, Seeger appeared before the House Un-American Activities Committee. Where other witnesses invoked the Fifth Amendment's protection against self-incrimination, Seeger took a different path. He invoked the First Amendment directly: he stated that the Constitution did not require him to tell the government with whom he associated. He was convicted and sent to prison for his refusal. The conviction was overturned on appeal.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="33:00" /><Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="33:30" />

Kiriakou described this as the act that made Seeger his model: *"He wasn't afraid of them. And so I wanted to be like Pete."*<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="33:30" />
