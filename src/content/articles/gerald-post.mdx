**Dr. Gerald "Jerry" Post** was an American psychiatrist and [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia) officer who founded the agency's [Political Psychology Division](/wiki/cia-political-psychology-division). For decades he worked under cover as a professor at George Washington University, where he taught a class on the psychology of leadership and identified students likely to fit CIA culture. He recruited [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) in this manner in the late 1980s.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="9:02" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="14:18" />

Post described himself as a *"political psychiatrist."* He held doctorates in both political science and psychology and a medical degree.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="9:02" />

## Teaching and recruitment

Post's GW course examined how governments manipulate other world leaders into doing what they want. A signature lecture used the Yalta Conference: the conference was held at Yalta — rather than in Cuba or Tehran as originally proposed — because Stalin's intelligence services had reported that Roosevelt was sick and dying. Forcing Roosevelt to travel from Washington to Morocco, then to sub-Saharan Africa, then to Tehran, and finally north to Yalta exhausted him; Stalin insisted on beginning talks immediately, and Roosevelt — too tired to negotiate — conceded Poland to the Soviet sphere simply to be allowed to sleep. *"He said that is how you manipulate somebody's psychology."*<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="10:07" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="11:09" />

A final-week assignment in the course required students to shadow their employers and produce a psychological evaluation. Post used the assignment as a screening instrument; students whose evaluations demonstrated the requisite insight were invited to his office, where he disclosed his CIA cover and offered them the opportunity to join the agency. *"I'm not really a professor here. I'm a CIA officer undercover as a professor here, and I'm here to find people who would fit into the CIA's culture."*<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="14:18" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="14:49" />

When a recruit accepted, Post placed a telephone call from a Rolodex on his desk — among the cards visible was one reading *"Oliver North, home"* — and dispatched the candidate to an address in Rosslyn, Virginia to begin the formal application process.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="15:50" />

## CIA career

Post's initial role at the CIA was as a staff psychiatrist treating case officers who suffered psychological breakdown. He observed that *"there aren't that many that go crazy, really"* and pitched the agency on a more expansive function: long-distance psychiatric evaluation of foreign leaders.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="36:47" />

This evolved into the [Political Psychology Division](/wiki/cia-political-psychology-division), which Post founded and led. The division produced short psychiatric profiles of foreign leaders — typically 500 words — embedded in the finished intelligence products that went to the President, Vice President, Secretary of State, Secretary of Defense, and National Security Advisor.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="38:54" /> Profiled leaders included Saddam Hussein and Deng Xiaoping.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="38:54" />

The division also produced exploitation plans for visiting foreign leaders. One documented case concerned an Allied leader who *"loved to play chess and loved, loved, loved to eat sausage."* Post's analysts proposed that during the leader's state visit he be served sausage to the point of satiation; the resulting drowsiness was expected to make him chatty as he attempted to stay awake, at which point Bill Clinton — already invited to play chess with him — would ask a list of sensitive prepared questions.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="37:19" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="37:50" />

The practice of issuing psychiatric determinations on foreign leaders without an in-person examination is regarded as unethical under standard psychiatric practice. Post and the division proceeded on the basis that *"this isn't a perfect world."*<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="38:21" />

The Political Psychology Division continues to operate. *"It's a lasting legacy at the CIA, it's still there."*<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="39:27" />

## Continued mentorship

Post remained available to Kiriakou throughout his CIA career and offered specific tradecraft advice — including the technique of focusing on a fixed point on the wall during the agency's polygraph examination and answering yes-or-no without reflection — that Kiriakou credits with helping him pass his entry polygraph.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="24:14" /> *"Jerry was good to me, really good to me, even later in life he was even better to me."*<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="28:27" />

## Death

Post died of COVID-19, approximately one year before [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s November 2023 appearance on the *Dalton Fischer Podcast*.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="28:58" />

## The A paper

Kiriakou's profile — of a boss he concluded was a sociopath, whom he called out directly in the paper — earned an A. Post wrote in the margin: *'Please see me after class.'* Kiriakou went to Post's office, closed the door at Post's request, and received the recruitment offer.<Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-cia-marriages-relationships" t="0:32" /><Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-cia-marriages-relationships" t="1:02" />

## The shadow-the-boss assignment

The assignment that triggered [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s recruitment was a week-long shadow exercise: students were to follow their boss for a week and write a psychological profile. Kiriakou was working at the United Food and Commercial Workers Union. His profile concluded his boss was *"a sociopath with psychopathic and possibly violent tendencies,"* supported by citations from psychology texts. Post gave him an A and a note to see him after class.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="18:30" />

In his office, [Post](/wiki/gerald-post) closed the door and said: *"I'm not really a professor here. I'm a [CIA](/wiki/cia) officer undercover as a professor here, and I'm looking for people who would fit into the CIA's culture. I think you would fit into the CIA's culture. Would you like to be a spy?"* Kiriakou said yes. Post immediately called headquarters, said *"Bob, this is Jerry. I've got a good one for you. Do you have some time?"* and wrote an address on a scrap of paper. Kiriakou had to be there in twenty minutes.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="19:30" />

The address was one subway stop away in Rosslyn, Virginia. Kiriakou was met by *"Bob"* — described as 6'6" and 350 pounds — who scheduled him for testing at the GW Medical School auditorium the following Saturday with approximately two hundred other candidates.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="20:00" />

## The undercover professor (Scott Michael Nathan)

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says he was recruited by [Gerald Post](/wiki/gerald-post), an eminent psychiatrist who taught a George Washington University course called the psychology of leadership. For an assignment, Kiriakou shadowed his abusive union boss and profiled him as a sociopath; Post gave the heavily footnoted paper an A, wrote *"please see me after class,"* then closed his office door and revealed, *"I'm not really a professor here. I'm a CIA officer undercover."*<Cite s="2026-01-21-scott-michael-nathan-prison-for-truth" t="02:05" /><Cite s="2026-01-21-scott-michael-nathan-prison-for-truth" t="03:06" /><Cite s="2026-01-21-scott-michael-nathan-prison-for-truth" t="03:38" /> Kiriakou notes such secret recruiting became illegal in 1993 under equal-employment rules.<Cite s="2026-01-21-scott-michael-nathan-prison-for-truth" t="04:08" />
