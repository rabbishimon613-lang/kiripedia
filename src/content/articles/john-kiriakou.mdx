<Hatnote>This article is about the former CIA officer and whistleblower. For the broader case against him, see United States v. Kiriakou.</Hatnote>

**John Chris Kiriakou** (born August 9, 1964) is an American former [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia) analyst and case officer who served from 1990 to 2004. He led the team in Pakistan that captured [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah), refused training in the agency's [enhanced interrogation techniques](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation), and in December 2007 became the first U.S. government official to publicly confirm that the [CIA](/wiki/cia) waterboarded detainees. He was later prosecuted under the Espionage Act for confirming the name of a covert officer to a journalist and served 23 months in federal prison.

Since his release, Kiriakou has written nine books<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="2:02:30" /> and works as a podcast host and commentator on intelligence, foreign policy, and the prosecution of whistleblowers. He has traveled to 72 countries and lives approximately three miles from the White House in Washington, D.C.<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="36:25" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="10:52" /> At the start of 2026 he delivered a UK speaking tour comprising fifteen engagements — five with a professor of English literature, five with a retired actor, and five with a retired MI6 officer.<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="03:39" />

In early 2026 — following the viral spread of a clip in which he recounted his unsuccessful auction bid for the artifact known as [Lincoln's last turd](/wiki/lincolns-last-turd) — Kiriakou achieved a degree of internet meme celebrity, with a single clip drawing approximately fifteen million views. He is widely referred to in this period as *"the hummus guy"* or *"the slow-motion hummus guy,"* in reference to his repeated public commentary on the CIA's use of rectal feeding with [hummus](/wiki/hummus).<Cite s="2026-02-28-julian-dorey-meme-reaction" t="00:00" /><Cite s="2026-02-28-julian-dorey-meme-reaction" t="03:07" />

## Early life

Kiriakou was born and raised in New Castle, Pennsylvania, a town about an hour north of Pittsburgh in Amish Country.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="3:41" /> Both of his parents were public school teachers; his father was an elementary school principal. He is the oldest of three children, with a brother and a sister.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="4:15" />

The town had 50,000 residents during his childhood and has since declined to roughly 18,000; Kiriakou cites the arrival of DEA and ATF field offices as evidence of the change.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="3:41" /> As a child he was preoccupied with baseball and football, and spent time exploring nearby cemeteries — initially hunting for salamanders, later studying the names on the gravestones of the town's industrial founders.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="4:15" />

At age nine, his father bought a box of junk at an auction for fifty cents that contained a broken shortwave radio. Once repaired, the radio became a formative obsession: Kiriakou listened to BBC London, Radio Moscow, and stations from Cuba, Ecuador, China, and Mongolia.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="5:50" /> By age fourteen his father had built a forty-foot tower with a shortwave antenna behind the house and run the wires into Kiriakou's bedroom; that year, with money saved from his paper route, Kiriakou bought a $1,000 shortwave base unit — "a lot of money in 1978, like buying a car."<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="6:22" /> He credits the shortwave radio with giving him the travel bug and the determination to leave New Castle for Washington.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="6:54" />

## Education

Kiriakou attended George Washington University in Washington, D.C., the only school to which he applied. He chose it because it sat two blocks from the White House and because it was one of only three schools in the country at the time with a degree program in Middle Eastern studies.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="7:26" />

His interest in the region had been sparked at age fifteen by the Iranian hostage crisis: "we're on the brink of war with Iran and I was coming up to draft age."<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="7:26" /> At GW he majored in Middle Eastern studies with a focus on Islamic theology and studied Arabic and oil economics.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="7:57" />

He stayed at GW for a Master's in Legislative Affairs with a focus on foreign policy analysis, explaining that after undergrad he "wasn't adult enough to start [his] life."<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="8:32" /> During his master's program he took a class on the psychology of leadership taught by [Dr. Gerald Post](/wiki/gerald-post), an "eminent psychiatrist" with doctorates in political science, psychology, and medicine.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="9:02" /> Post — unknown to his students — was a [CIA](/wiki/cia) officer working undercover as a professor to identify candidates who would fit the agency's culture.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="14:18" />

## CIA recruitment

A final assignment in Post's class required students to shadow their bosses for a week and write a psychological analysis. Kiriakou was working at the time for the United Food and Commercial Workers Union in Washington under a "mean, tough, old-school union organizer" who had had his back broken by strike-breakers in 1970s New York.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="11:40" /> Midway through the shadowing week the two argued; Kiriakou called him a racist, and the man — instead of striking him as Kiriakou expected — responded with the line "my penis is bigger than yours."<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="12:15" /> Kiriakou quit on the spot and wrote his evaluation diagnosing the man as "a sociopath with psychopathic and possibly violent tendencies." Post returned the paper with an A and a note: *please see me after class.*<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="13:16" />

In his office Post revealed his CIA cover and asked whether Kiriakou would like to join the agency. Six weeks from his wedding and with no job prospects, Kiriakou agreed. Post picked up the phone, called a number from a Rolodex — Kiriakou recalls noticing the card *Oliver North home* in the wheel — and sent him to an address in Rosslyn, Virginia to ask for "Bob."<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="15:50" />

### "Bob"

[Bob](/wiki/bob-cia-hr) — a name Kiriakou never confirmed was real — was a "6'6", 320-pound, loud, smiling" man who later turned out to be the director of human resources for the entire CIA.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="17:57" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="27:55" /> Bob's opening question was *"Have you ever betrayed a friendship?"* He then sent Kiriakou to an unmarked, one-story building in Vienna, Virginia, for an interview with three people who identified themselves only as a psychologist, a psychiatrist, and an anthropologist.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="19:33" />

The interview was fifteen minutes long and consisted of three questions: *describe your relationship with your mother*, *describe your relationship with your father*, and a repetition of *have you ever betrayed a friendship?* When Kiriakou answered no the second time, the anthropologist responded, *"that's the answer we were looking for."*<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="20:36" /> Kiriakou was then asked to provide urine, blood, and hair samples and sent home.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="20:36" />

### The polygraph

The CIA polygraph is administered before hiring, after a three-year probationary period, and every five years thereafter for the rest of an officer's career.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="32:05" /> During the pre-hire and three-year polygraphs, examiners ask "lifestyle questions" — about sexuality, deviance, and criminal history — that are dropped from subsequent re-investigations.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="32:35" />

Acting on advice from Dr. Post, Kiriakou focused on a single speck on the wall throughout the examination and answered yes-or-no without reflection.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="24:14" /> During his exam the polygrapher abruptly asked whether he had ever had sex with a man; Kiriakou later learned this was a "turd thrown into the middle of the room" to test reactions.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="26:51" /> He passed; Bob later told him, *"they're fighting over you at headquarters."*<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="28:27" />

## CIA career

Kiriakou joined the agency in 1990 and spent his first seven years as an analyst in the Directorate of Intelligence, including service as the agency's chief historian on Saddam Hussein in the period leading into the First Iraq War.<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="06:14" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="2:02:30" /> In 1997 — *"halfway through my career"* — he transferred to operations and attended case-officer training at [The Farm](/wiki/the-farm), the CIA's clandestine training facility in Virginia, where his strongest skills were counterterrorist driving and marksmanship — despite never having touched a real gun before training began.<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="06:14" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="52:06" /> He was sent for additional advanced driving training in the Nevada desert.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="53:43" />

### Greece (1998–2000)

Kiriakou's first case-officer assignment, beginning in 1998, was Athens, where he was specifically tasked with disrupting [Revolutionary Organization 17 November](/wiki/revolutionary-organization-17-november), the deadliest terrorist group in Greek history.<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="06:45" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="1:28:58" /> Athens at the time was rated "critical threat" for terrorism — the highest level — and the embassy spent more on security than its counterpart in Beirut.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="1:30:01" />

In Greece he carried a 9mm Glock in a fanny pack on his waist, a snub-nose .38 revolver on his ankle, a buck knife in his back pocket, and two spare magazines.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="1:32:39" /> He drove a fully armored BMW 540 — the first 540 in Greece, requiring its 5-liter engine to carry the weight of the armor; it could not be insured locally and had to be covered through a German company.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="1:34:13" />

His first recruitment was an elderly former defense attaché of an unnamed enemy country who had witnessed the 1975 [assassination of CIA Athens station chief Richard Welch](/wiki/richard-welch) and was never interviewed at the time because of his country of origin. Twenty-five years later, Kiriakou tracked him to his bank night-watchman job, knocked on the door, and introduced himself with his real name: *"I'm John Kiriakou from the CIA and I'm here to change your life."*<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="1:58:50" /> The man replied, *"My friend, I've waited thirty years for this day."*<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="1:59:20" />

In March 2000, Kiriakou's neighbor [Stephen Saunders](/wiki/stephen-saunders), the British defense attaché, was assassinated by 17 November. A subsequent 17 November manifesto identified Kiriakou by name as the originally intended target. Within hours, the embassy collected his wife and children from school and the family flew home on the noon Delta flight to New York; embassy staff packed and shipped their belongings.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="1:43:45" />

### Pakistan and Abu Zubaydah

Kiriakou later led the [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s counterterrorism efforts in Pakistan, where in March 2002 his team captured [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah), then believed to be a senior al-Qaeda figure.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="1:01" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="56:05" /> In the first week of May 2002, two months after the capture, Kiriakou was personally offered certification in the [enhanced interrogation techniques](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation) and refused. The offer took the form of a hallway conversation in the [CIA](/wiki/cia) cafeteria, in which a friend from the [Counterterrorism Center](/wiki/cia) listed ten techniques and represented that *"the President approved it and the Justice Department approved it."* After consulting a senior officer on the seventh floor — who advised him *"this is a torture program … somebody's going to go overboard, and they're going to kill a prisoner … you want to go to prison?"* — Kiriakou declined. In total, **fourteen** CIA officers were offered EIT certification; two initially declined, one subsequently changed his mind, and Kiriakou was the only one in the end who refused.<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="00:31" /><Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="01:32" /><Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="02:03" />

He was leaving Pakistan for a vacation in Santa Fe, New Mexico when a cable from headquarters redirected him to break into an apartment in a third country and plant a bug; the vacation was rescheduled by a month.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="56:53" />

During his Pakistan posting Kiriakou conducted near-daily liaison meetings with foreign intelligence services. In one notable instance an officer of the [French foreign intelligence service](/wiki/dgse) attempted to conduct a covert-recruitment pickup on Kiriakou himself — arranging a residential-corner meet rather than a public venue and instructing Kiriakou to *"get in"* a car pulling up to him. Kiriakou refused the maneuver on the spot: *"You're not using tradecraft on me."*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="01:38" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="02:08" />

### Guantanamo Bay (interim assignment, summer 2002)

Following his Pakistan tour and the capture of Abu Zubaydah, Kiriakou — *"a big star after Abu Zubaydah"* — was approached by a senior agency official with an offer to fill in as interim Chief of the CIA station at [Guantanamo Bay](/wiki/guantanamo-bay) until a permanent Chief was named. Kiriakou had never previously been to Cuba and accepted on that basis. The posting took place during the summer of 2002, between his Pakistan and subsequent assignments.<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="13:40" />

### Bahrain

Kiriakou served at the U.S. embassy in Bahrain under Ambassador [David Ransom](/wiki/david-ransom). One documented incident from that posting involved a former U.S. ambassador to Bahrain working as a fundraiser for the Bob Dole presidential campaign who solicited and received $50,000 from the Bahraini Foreign Minister and another $50,000 from the Amir personally — both contributions illegal under U.S. campaign-finance law restricting foreign-national donations. On Ambassador Ransom's instruction, Kiriakou omitted the second contribution from his cable reporting the meeting.<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="58:07" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="58:39" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="59:11" />

He left the CIA in 2004.

## Post-CIA career

### Private intelligence consulting (2004–2009)

Following his CIA departure, Kiriakou worked in private intelligence — applying the same operational and analytical skills he had used at the agency, with the constraint that he could not use classified information. Three engagements from this period are publicly documented:

- A Cypriot billionaire engagement: the subject's only daughter was engaged to a London-based Greek-Cypriot man Kiriakou identified, over six weeks of work, as a drug-smuggling embezzler.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="53:17" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="54:49" />
- A Romanian silver-mine engagement: Kiriakou predicted a Romanian municipal election outcome on behalf of a mining company that needed the village atop the mine to be relocated rather than blocked. Data source: connection requests to every Orthodox Christian in Romania on LinkedIn.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="56:22" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="57:24" />
- An Omani oil-field engagement: brokered with the Omani government a military operation to clear al-Qaeda from a 99-year-lease oil field on the Yemen border.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="57:55" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="58:58" />

The work was facilitated by office space and a part-time secretary provided to Kiriakou by [Mack McLarty](/wiki/mack-mclarty), the former White House Chief of Staff to Bill Clinton, at his firm McLarty Associates (then Kissinger McLarty).<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:45:23" />

Kiriakou also took a 2008 contract from professional propagandist [John Rendon](/wiki/john-rendon) — at $25,000 for four op-eds — to rehabilitate the U.S. press reputation of Indonesian general [Joko Widodo](/wiki/joko-widodo), banned from the United States for personally killing six pro-democracy student protesters. No newspaper published the op-eds.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="1:52:56" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="1:56:03" />

### Senate Foreign Relations Committee (2009)

Kiriakou served in 2009 as the **senior investigator on the Senate Foreign Relations Committee** under chairman [John Kerry](/wiki/john-kerry). Kerry had personally recruited him to reactivate the committee's long-dormant investigative function, last operative in 1972.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:52:41" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:53:11" />

In the SFRC role, Kiriakou:

- Traveled to [Yemen](/wiki/yemen) as part of the embassy-closure process, encountering Deputy CIA Director [Steve Kappes](/wiki/steve-kappes) in the Sanaa Marriott lobby.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:21:25" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:22:57" />
- Toured the Dubai government's underground CCTV operations facility, built by Siemens, with full-country coverage.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:02:00" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:03:35" />
- Attempted the reopening of the [Dasht-i-Leili massacre](/wiki/dasht-i-leili-massacre) investigation promised by Barack Obama during the 2008 campaign — an effort that resulted in a top-secret-classified *"go fuck yourself"* non-response from the CIA and Kerry's subsequent refusal to take the story to the *Washington Post*.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:42:50" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:43:50" />

### The 2011 Japanese-diplomat sting that became the prosecution

In approximately 2011, Kiriakou was invited to lunch by a Japanese diplomat with whom he had previously been on a casual professional basis. The lunch — conducted in Arabic because of the diplomat's limited English — ended with the diplomat saying: *"If you give me information, I can give you money."* Kiriakou refused, walked directly to the Senate Security Officer, and cooperated with the FBI on a five-meeting sting attempt against the diplomat (the diplomat was subsequently promoted to deputy ambassador in Cairo).<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="43:49" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="44:52" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="46:25" />

In January 2012 — nine months after Kiriakou had left the Senate — two FBI agents (one of whom Kiriakou subsequently identified as Peter Strzok) summoned Kiriakou to the Washington Field Office under the pretext that they needed his help on a similar matter. The phone words *"anything for the FBI"* — which he gave before learning the meeting was an interrogation — were deployed by the prosecution as evidence of cooperation. The agents revealed mid-meeting: *"We know you've been giving information to the Guantanamo defense attorneys. … And we're raiding your house right now as we speak."* Kiriakou's request to speak to his attorney prevented his arrest; the case was thereafter conducted as an Espionage Act prosecution under the personal direction of [John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan).<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="47:27" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="48:30" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="49:00" />

### The defense team and the 45-year offer

Kiriakou retained an eleven-attorney defense team led by [Plato Cacheris](/wiki/plato-cacheris), with [Mark McDougall](/wiki/mark-mcdougall) and Bob Trout as principal co-counsel. The initial Justice Department plea offer was **45 years**; the prosecutor (subsequently elevated to Deputy Attorney General for the criminal division under Biden) accompanied the offer with: *"Take this deal, Mr. Kiriakou, and you may live to meet your grandchildren."* Subsequent offers descended unusually — 10 years, then 8, then 5, then 3.5 — a pattern Cacheris characterized as unprecedented in his 52 years at the D.C. criminal bar and as definitive evidence that the government's case was weak. He was advised that going to trial in the Eastern District of Virginia (the "espionage court") would mean facing a jury drawn from CIA, FBI, DoD, and IC-contractor personnel — by OJ Simpson's pro-bono jury consultant. The final accepted deal was **30 months** at [FCI Loretto](/wiki/fci-loretto); every actual prison day was served.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="51:39" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="52:10" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="52:42" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="59:29" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="58:58" />

### Prison and the anonymous mortgage

During his 2013–2015 federal prison sentence at [FCI Loretto](/wiki/fci-loretto), Kiriakou's second mortgage was anonymously paid off — a development he learned of through a letter from his bank to his wife. The benefactor was identified only years later as Pink Floyd co-founder [Roger Waters](/wiki/roger-waters), who has subsequently become a personal friend.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="4:12:06" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="4:12:38" />

Kiriakou's CIA pension was confiscated upon conviction, an action [John McCain](/wiki/john-mccain) subsequently attempted but failed to reverse via a 2016 NDAA amendment that died with his brain-tumor diagnosis.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:06:10" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:07:10" />

### Greek citizenship and the EU whistleblower law

The Greek government granted Kiriakou Greek citizenship within twenty-four hours of his arrest, on the request of the Greek ambassador in Washington. On his release from prison the Greek government hired him to draft a new whistleblower-protection law; the Greek Parliament passed it, the European Union adopted it, Kiriakou testified in Brussels, and the law is now in effect across the EU.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:09:14" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:09:47" />

### Post-prison career

Kiriakou's career rebuilding combined his journalism, a syndicated column distributed through Consortium News to approximately 200 small-town newspapers, paid speaking engagements, and an adjunct professor position at the University of Salamanca in Spain, where he teaches a graduate-level history of terrorism course. He has stated he plans to work until the day he dies because he has *"literally nothing saved — it all went to the attorneys."*<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:09:47" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:11:22" />

## The Oval Office briefing, August 2, 1990

Eight months into his CIA career, Kiriakou arrived at the office on the morning of August 2, 1990 to find that Iraq had invaded Kuwait. His boss told him: *"Don't take your jacket off. We're going to the White House."* In the Oval Office he found President George H.W. Bush, the Vice President, the National Security Advisor, the CIA Director, his boss, and himself — the most junior person in the room at age 25. When Bush asked *"Well, now what do we do?"* everyone turned to look at Kiriakou. *"I was 25 years old. And I'm looking at him like — and then it took me a second like, 'Oh. Oh. Uh yes. Well, Mr. President, as you know, Iraqi troops crossed the border at 2:00 this morning. They've taken the whole of Kuwait. The royal family has fled to Saudi Arabia...'"* He describes it as the most intimidated he has ever felt: *"Not only am I meeting the President and the Vice President and the CIA Director and the National Security Advisor, but they're asking for my advice on issues of war and peace. I'm 25 years old."*<Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:13:00" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:13:31" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:14:02" />

## Post-CIA career (interim period)

After leaving the CIA in 2004, Kiriakou spent approximately four years in the private sector before joining the Senate Foreign Relations Committee as chief investigator, a role he held for about two and a half years. It was during the period after he left the committee and went into business for himself that the Obama administration moved against him.<Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="02:36" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="03:08" />

## 'Dead Drop' podcast

As of May 2026, Kiriakou hosts three monetized podcasts. In addition to *Deprogram* (Monday through Friday on YouTube and Rumble) and *Deep Focus*, he hosts *John Kiriakou's Dead Drop* on Apple Podcasts — *"all the stories from my career"* — which at the time of this interview was ranked seventh in the world on the platform.<Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:22:16" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:22:49" />

## On fear

Kiriakou describes a near-total absence of fear throughout his career: *"I'm not afraid of anybody. And I don't really know why that is."* He attributes it partly to tempo — *"I was so busy I just didn't have time to really process the emotion of fear"* — but also to something physiological: *"Maybe there's just some switch in my brain that never activated a sense of fear."* He notes that CIA psychiatrists would characterize this as evidence of sociopathic tendencies, and credits it as the specific personality trait that made him an effective case officer while also making him constitutionally unable to participate in the torture program: *"That's why I blew the whistle — cuz I'm not a sociopath."*<Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:11:57" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:12:28" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="51:18" />

## Recruitment motivation

Kiriakou was getting married in six weeks and had no job when [Gerald Post](/wiki/gerald-post) recruited him. *'The truth was, I was getting married in 6 weeks, and I didn't have a job. So, I said, Yeah, sure. I'd love to join the CIA. And the next thing I knew, I was in the CIA.'*<Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-cia-marriages-relationships" t="1:02" />

## First marriage and breakdown

Kiriakou's first wife was a first-generation Greek-American whose parents were from Greece; she had inherited her grandmother's house on one of the Greek islands. While he was posted to Athens running the [Revolutionary Organization 17 November](/wiki/revolutionary-organization-17-november) case, she began taking the children to the island on weekends. The trips gradually extended — leaving Thursday, returning Monday, with the children missing school. The marriage ended after his then-six-year-old son told him: *'I told Mommy not to kiss Uncle Stelios on the lips. I told her to kiss only you on the lips, and she told me to mind my own business.'*<Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-cia-marriages-relationships" t="5:07" /><Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-cia-marriages-relationships" t="5:40" />

Kiriakou left immediately without finishing shaving, to prevent himself from 'doing something that's going to dog me for the rest of my life.' When 17 November named him in their manifesto after assassinating [Stephen Saunders](/wiki/stephen-saunders) and the CIA ordered the family evacuated, their plane landed in New York; Kiriakou connected to Washington and his wife connected to Cleveland. *'That was the end of it.'*<Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-cia-marriages-relationships" t="7:44" />

## Pakistan posting and CIA marriage culture

Approximately two years after the Athens evacuation, Kiriakou was posted to Pakistan, where he used the embassy's trunk line — a Washington 202 area code phone — to call a CIA officer he was dating each morning, reaching her in the previous evening in Washington. When a colleague overheard him say goodbye and asked about wedding bells, Kiriakou demurred: *'I don't know. I just got divorced, and I really don't want to be a two-time loser.'*

The colleague — a former chief of Near Eastern operations — polled the branch: *'I've been married four times. Jim, how many times have you been married? Five for me. Bill — four. Dave — three. Two-time loser? Welcome to the CIA, kid.'*<Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-cia-marriages-relationships" t="8:46" /><Cite s="2026-04-02-megyn-kelly-cia-marriages-relationships" t="9:16" />

## Decision to go public

Kiriakou has stated that he kept silent for approximately five and a half years after learning of the [CIA](/wiki/cia) torture program, having resigned from the CIA two years after discovering it. He believed that enough people more directly involved were objecting, and that the program would be stopped from within.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="7:50" /> CIA physicians from the Office of Medical Services had reportedly invoked the Hippocratic oath in protest.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="7:50" /> When the program was finally shut down in 2006, Kiriakou attributed the decision to internal CIA leadership under Director Porter Goss, who concluded the program was producing no actionable value.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="8:21" />

The immediate trigger for Kiriakou's decision to speak publicly was a press conference in which President Bush looked directly into the camera and stated, *"We do not torture."* Kiriakou, watching with his wife — also a senior CIA officer — said: *"He is just lying to the American people."*<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="37:31" /> Days later, Bush implied that any torture would be the fault of a rogue CIA officer, which Kiriakou interpreted as preparation to scapegoat him specifically, as he had been the only officer to object internally and had already been passed over for promotion for doing so. He then called ABC News journalist Brian Ross and agreed to an interview, deciding to answer every question truthfully.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="38:03" />

## Regrets about the disclosure

Looking back, Kiriakou has said the one thing he would have done differently was to retain an attorney before speaking publicly. Without legal counsel, he was forced to respond reactively to a rapidly escalating media story. He gave approximately fifty follow-up interviews, each pushing closer to classified material, which generated criticism when he declined to add new disclosures. He has said the original disclosure was correct and necessary — *"somebody had to say something. We were committing a war crime, a crime against humanity"* — but that managing the aftermath without professional guidance was a mistake.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="33:20" />

## Post-prison work

Following his release, Kiriakou has lectured at multiple universities, including an intelligence studies course at the graduate school of the University of Salamanca in Spain and a journalism school course at the University of Southern California. He has written columns for *Consortium News* and *Covert Action Magazine*, hosted two podcasts — *Deep Focus with John Kiriakou* and *Deprogram* — and produced an internet television program called *CIA Declassified* on Unified Television, in which CIA documents are used to explain historical international events.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="52:09" />

## Insider-threat designation removed

A CIA friend informed Kiriakou that he had appeared on a slide in a security briefing under the heading *"the insider threat."* When the briefing audience began booing, the instructor asked why, and attendees responded that Kiriakou *"was a legitimate whistleblower"* and *"in the end, he was the one who was right."* His image was subsequently removed from the slide.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="53:44" />

## Two-day sequence that pushed him to go public

The final trigger for [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s decision to go public was a two-day sequence of statements from President George W. Bush. On a Wednesday, Bush said there was no torture program. On Friday, Bush said that if there was torture, it was the work of a rogue [CIA](/wiki/cia) officer. Kiriakou understood this as the agency preparing to blame him specifically: *"I decided to defend myself."*<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="5:40" />

He had expected his ABC News interview in December 2007 to be a one-day story. The CIA's response — continuing to leak to the *Washington Post*, forcing Kiriakou to respond publicly again and again — extended the story. When the agency also escorted a female friend of his out of the White House, suspended her clearance, and told her never to come back, Kiriakou concluded they were attempting to ruin people around him and resolved to keep speaking.<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="7:13" />

## Charges and plea

Kiriakou was initially charged with five felonies: three counts of espionage, one count of making a false statement, and one count of violating the [Intelligence Identities Protection Act](/wiki/intelligence-identities-protection-act). He had not committed espionage and the government knew it. The standard tactic followed: prosecutors waited until he had filed for bankruptcy, then dropped the espionage charges, then threatened to add a conspiracy charge and a second false-statement count. The choice was twelve to eighteen years at trial or twenty-three months on a plea. He took the plea.<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="8:14" /><Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="8:45" />

## Career overview

Kiriakou's fifteen years at the [CIA](/wiki/cia) divided into two equal periods. The first seven and a half years he spent as an analyst on the Middle East. The second seven and a half he spent as a counterterrorism operations officer, rising to chief of counterterrorism operations in Pakistan after September 11. In that capacity his primary mission was to identify the locations of al-Qaeda senior leadership and to lead or support raids to kill or capture them. He led the raid that resulted in the capture of [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah), the first high-value capture of the post-9/11 period.<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="17:35" />

## Family

Kiriakou has four sons and one daughter. The toll of his case, prosecution, and imprisonment on his family was total: he described himself as no longer having a family. One son remains close — they speak nearly every day. The others are estranged, partly from embarrassment, partly from what Kiriakou described as lies spread during the period of media attention.<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="25:23" />

## Night of arrest and suicidal ideation

On the night of his arrest, after the FBI raid and his processing at headquarters, Kiriakou and his wife were watching television. She said they should go to bed; Kiriakou said he would stay up and watch more TV. He was not planning to watch television. He intended to go down to the garage, start the car, and lie across the back seat. He believes his wife suspected this. She stood at the television and waited until he turned it off and came to bed.<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="1:11:27" />

His brother told him the day of his arrest: *"I know you can't see it, but this is going to turn out to be the best thing that ever happened to you."* Kiriakou said his brother was right.<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="1:12:29" />

## *"Never lie to medical, security, or finance"*

Kiriakou's first station chief told him early in his career: *"Never lie to medical, security, or finance — not only will they ruin your career, they can put you in prison. And never lie to me."* Kiriakou described these as words he has always tried to live by.<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="48:58" />

## Post-prison career explosion

Approximately eight weeks before this interview, Kiriakou gave an interview to Steven Bartlett on *Diary of a CEO*. A twenty-year-old sophomore at the University of Texas at Austin took the interview, edited it into short-form clips with a chipmunk voice effect and laser beams shooting from Kiriakou's eyes, and posted them to TikTok. The videos went massively viral. As of the interview date, Kiriakou said he could not walk twenty-five feet down the street without being stopped for a selfie, had been stopped five times in the Miami airport and twice on the plane to Washington the previous night, and always said yes. The viral attention led to representation by a major talent agency, speaking-engagement bookings, new publishing deals, and Hollywood representation. He had previously sold eight television pilots over twenty years, and described his current career as podcasting, writing, and speaking.<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="1:03:04" /><Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="1:04:06" />

## FBI arrest — January 12, 2012

On January 12, 2012, twenty-two FBI agents raided [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s home. His wife, Heather, was present with their two-month-old son. Kiriakou himself was not home; he had been lured to FBI headquarters under the pretext of a routine interview. Agents ransacked the house while Heather — who had worked for the [CIA](/wiki/cia) herself — managed the scene alone with an infant.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="44:00" />

Kiriakou was interviewed at FBI headquarters. At the conclusion of the interview, [Peter Strzok](/wiki/peter-strzok) — whose identity Kiriakou would not learn for five more years — asked the lead interviewing agent: *"Did he implicate himself?"* Strzok then physically placed handcuffs on Kiriakou.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="52:44" /><Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="57:21" />

That night, Kiriakou told his wife he was going to kill himself. She talked him out of it.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="53:20" />

## Prison and post-release surveillance

Kiriakou served twenty-three months of a thirty-month sentence. He received no halfway-house placement upon release.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="1:00:00" />

After his release, Kiriakou discovered that the NSA had been intercepting his communications and leaking information from those intercepts to reporters at the *New York Times*. He learned this when a *Times* reporter called him and read back to him the contents of a private conversation.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="1:01:00" />

Kiriakou also received what he described as apology emails from FBI agents who had worked his case — agents who said they had been ordered to build a case against him and felt it was unjust.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="1:02:30" />

## Strzok's identity revealed (2017)

Kiriakou learned [Peter Strzok](/wiki/peter-strzok)'s name in 2017, when a *Washington Post* reporter called to ask for comment on Strzok's firing from the FBI. *"I said, 'I don't know anything about Peter Strzok other than what I've read.' He said, 'No, Peter Strzok arrested you in January of 2012.'"* Kiriakou's published statement: *"Karma's a [expletive] and now it's his turn."*<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="57:53" />

## Forgiveness

Kiriakou described arriving at forgiveness through three figures: Nelson Mandela, who emerged from twenty-seven years in prison without bitterness; Martin Luther King Jr.'s writings on loving one's enemies; and the Orthodox saint Nectarios of Aegina. He said he reached a point where he no longer carried hatred for the people who prosecuted him.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="1:42:00" />

## Athens — the morning his first marriage ended

In April 2000, [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) was stationed in Athens, Greece, which he described as a critical-threat terrorism posting. One morning while shaving, his six-year-old son — sitting on the bathroom floor watching him — volunteered: *"I told Mommy she shouldn't kiss Uncle Stelios on the lips. She should only kiss you on the lips, and she told me to mind my own business."* Kiriakou described the sensation as being electrocuted. He wiped off the shaving cream, went to the bedroom where his wife was sleeping, and kicked the bed. When he asked who Stelios was, she said: *"Don't believe everything a six-year-old says."* He told her he was leaving before he did something he would regret for the rest of his life, got in his fully armored BMW 540 with two firearms, and drove to work.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="2:30" /><Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="3:00" /><Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="3:30" />

That afternoon, his wife began calling repeatedly. He let it go to voicemail. Eventually his secretary came in: his wife had been in a car accident and was injured. A driver five cars behind her, impatient in the Greek manner, had T-boned her as she made a left turn, pushing the car onto the sidewalk and demolishing it. She broke her wrist. Kiriakou went to the scene.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="4:00" /><Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="4:30" />

At the accident scene, the driver became agitated and called his wife a whore in Greek. Kiriakou said the man was in a coma for three days. He noted: *"Any other day I would have let it go."* He was arrested at the scene, invoked diplomatic immunity, and was held briefly — the cell door left open, his guns returned. The Greek police captain, upon establishing that the driver had in fact called Kiriakou's wife a whore, charged the driver — it being a crime in Greece to call a man's wife that name. Kiriakou asked that the charges be dropped given the circumstances. The driver later filed a civil lawsuit; it was thrown out due to Kiriakou's diplomatic immunity. He described it as the only fight of his entire life.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="5:00" /><Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="5:30" /><Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="6:00" /><Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="6:30" /><Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="7:00" />

## First operational boss — rules on lying

Kiriakou recounted his first operational boss's rules: *"Never lie to medical, finance, or security. They'll put you in prison. Never lie to me. I'll put you in the hospital."* The security concern specifically was that a vulnerability — a drinking problem, a drug habit, a gambling debt — could become a lever for a foreign intelligence service: *"I need 50 grand right now, and I can make it right for you."*<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="8:00" />

## Career timeline — State rotation, Arabic, Bahrain, Yemen

Kiriakou remained in the Directorate of Intelligence (analytic directorate) until August 1993. To qualify for the Senior Intelligence Service, [CIA](/wiki/cia) officers are required to complete a rotation in the policy community (the White House, State Department, or Defense Department) and a rotation in another directorate (operations, science and technology, or administration). Kiriakou did his policy rotation at the State Department and used the time to complete a year of Arabic-language training, which he described as mastering the language completely.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="26:00" /><Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="26:30" />

In Bahrain, [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) worked under Ambassador [David Ransom](/wiki/david-ransom), whom he described as a wonderful boss. On one occasion, Ransom asked Kiriakou to accompany him as interpreter to a courtesy call on the Bahraini Minister of Al-Awqaf and Islamic Affairs, who spoke no English. After the meeting, the minister told Kiriakou directly: *"Your Arabic is excellent, young man."* The ambassador echoed the compliment in the car.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="27:00" /><Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="27:30" />

In another instance, Kiriakou returned from Yemen to [CIA](/wiki/cia) headquarters planning only to do accounting paperwork and go home to sleep. His boss saw him and asked if he could fly back to Sudan by 3:00 p.m. — there was a walk-in and no one at post spoke Arabic. Kiriakou went home, did a load of laundry, and flew back.<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="28:00" /><Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="28:30" />

## On becoming [CIA](/wiki/cia) Director

On the question of whether he was on track to become CIA Director, Kiriakou said no — it is a highly political position and only three career [CIA](/wiki/cia) officers in history have held it. Everyone else has been an outsider: former FBI directors, generals. He described the appointment of generals as a historical mistake, because people become four-star generals by saying *"Yes, sir"* — the opposite disposition from what the job requires: *"You want somebody in that position who can say, 'Mr. President, let me tell you why that's a terrible idea.'"*<Cite s="2026-03-02-carlos-watson-conversations-cia-deep-state" t="29:00" />

## Early life and CIA recruitment

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) said he decided at age nine that he wanted to be a spy. When it came time for college, he applied only to George Washington University in Washington, D.C., because it was two blocks from the White House and one of only three schools in the country offering a Middle Eastern studies program. He was one of four students in the inaugural cohort of that program. He stayed for a master's degree in legislative affairs with a focus on foreign-policy analysis.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="18:00" />

He was recruited into the [CIA](/wiki/cia) by [Gerald Post](/wiki/gerald-post), a CIA officer operating undercover as a GWU psychiatry professor.

## Telling his wife

Upon joining the [CIA](/wiki/cia), Kiriakou was instructed not to tell anyone — including his wife — because he might go undercover or deep cover. He told her anyway. When asked on a polygraph whether he had told her, he admitted it: *"She's my wife. What am I going to do?"* The operational reality of the secret life created ongoing strain: he would arrive home smelling of a garbage dumpster from a surveillance operation and she would ask *"What was her name?"* They divorced.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="21:30" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="22:00" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="22:30" />

## The Powell call and the janitor (1993)

In 1993, [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) was in the morning briefing when his secretary came in to tell him General Colin Powell was on the phone and had asked for him by name. Powell asked a single question: if the Iraqis were going to kill President Bush, who would run that operation and where would they be located? Kiriakou told him it would be Iraqi-intelligence's Basra station chief, Sabre Abdul-Aziz al-Douri, operating from Baghdad, and gave him the address. Eight hours later, the United States fired forty-seven cruise missiles into Iraqi-intelligence-service headquarters. The attack was in the middle of the night in Baghdad; the only person killed was a janitor. The next morning Kiriakou told his boss: *"I killed that janitor."* His boss replied: *"You had no idea what Powell was going to do with the information."* Kiriakou said: *"I know, but I still feel guilty about it."*<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:11:30" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:12:00" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:12:30" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:13:00" />

## Bahrain human-rights officer

Kiriakou served as the human-rights officer at the [CIA](/wiki/cia) station in Bahrain. In that role, he was required to report human-rights violations to Congress — including cases where security services beat demonstrators to death in custody and called the family to collect the body. He would confront the Bahraini Minister of Interior. Simultaneously, he said, the CIA station chief would meet with the same minister an hour later and offer $10 million in exchange for establishing a secret prison where the CIA could send detainees to be tortured. The minister listened to the station chief.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:38:00" />

## McCain-Feinstein amendment credit — December 2014

When the McCain-Feinstein anti-torture amendment was passed into law in December 2014, Senator John McCain rose on the Senate floor and credited [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) by name: *"If I had not told the American people that the [CIA](/wiki/cia) was torturing prisoners in their name, we would never have known."* Kiriakou cited this as the reason he says it was worth going to prison.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:39:00" />

## Pardon application

Kiriakou has applied for a pardon from President Trump. His name is in the system. He described having *"very high level supporters"* who have approached Trump personally on the matter, and said he is hopeful.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:40:00" />

## Depression and turning point

Kiriakou described struggling with depression his entire life. After his second divorce, he reached a point where he could not get out of bed. He described the self-assessment in his fifties: unemployable, a convicted felon, barely able to pay rent. He said to himself: *"What's wrong with you? You don't have to answer to anybody."* He decided to stop feeling sorry for himself, identify what he was genuinely good at — writing and storytelling — and build from there. He described this single decision as what turned his life around.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:41:00" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:41:30" />

## Current career

After leaving the [CIA](/wiki/cia), Kiriakou was head of the competitive-intelligence practice at Deloitte & Touche — *"spying on Ernst & Young and PricewaterhouseCoopers and IBM"* — which he described as great fun. He knows he will never work for government or in corporate America again. ## Shortwave radio and numbers stations — origin story

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described his introduction to intelligence as occurring at age nine. ## Recruitment by Dr. Gerald Post — full account

Kiriakou described the full story of his [CIA](/wiki/cia) recruitment by [Dr. Gerald Post](/wiki/gerald-post), his graduate school professor. Post held a PhD in political science, a PhD in psychology, and an MD — and referred to himself as a *"political psychiatrist."* His class, *Psychology of Leadership*, taught how governments manipulate world leaders. The example that stayed with Kiriakou: Stalin learned from spies that Roosevelt was gravely ill, insisted the Yalta Conference be moved from Tehran to Yalta — forcing Roosevelt to fly days around the war zone. By the time Roosevelt arrived he was exhausted and desperately wanted sleep. Stalin insisted they begin immediately. *"Roosevelt gave up Poland just to get to bed. Forty-five years of communism in Poland resulted."*<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="09:30" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="10:00" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="10:30" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="11:00" />

Post assigned students to shadow their supervisors for a week and write a psychological profile. Kiriakou's supervisor was a mean old union organizer whose back had been broken by scabs. Mid-week Kiriakou called him a racist to his face; the man balled his fists, face turned red, and then said: *"My penis is bigger than yours."* Kiriakou quit. His subsequent psychological evaluation earned an A and a *"please see me after class."*<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="11:30" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="12:00" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="12:30" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="13:00" />

He said sure.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="14:00" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="14:30" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="15:00" />

## The 9/11 White House meeting that never happened

The meeting at the White House scheduled for 9:00 a.m. on September 11th was with Condoleezza Rice and was about an obscure Government Printing Office publication: *Foreign Policy of the United States, Greece, Turkey, Cyprus, 1949–1967*. The thousand-page volume contained the names of three surviving former [CIA](/wiki/cia) sources; rather than relocate three hundred-year-old men, the plan was to ask Rice simply to pull three pages from the volume.<Cite s="2024-11-07-julian-dorey-249" t="22:00" /><Cite s="2024-11-07-julian-dorey-249" t="22:30" /><Cite s="2024-11-07-julian-dorey-249" t="23:00" />

The secretary said: *"Did you see that or did I imagine it?"* Kiriakou ran to his office: *"Guys — two planes just hit both towers of the World Trade Center. ## The shower radio — hearing the Kuwait invasion

## Deloitte ethics class — *"Let me go get $200 in singles"*

## Jordanian intelligence — 50% Palestinian; deer stew and auto-flush toilets
