[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) referenced *The CIA Insider's Guide to Lying and Lie Detection* when asked how an ordinary person can tell if they are being lied to. He described the book as covering the primary physical tells: a person looking down when answering is concealing something; a person looking up is constructing a script in real time. Fidgeting and throat-clearing serve as micro-delays. Deeper in a conversation, a liar loses the thread or produces internal contradictions because maintaining a false account across an extended exchange exceeds working memory.<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="42:06" />

The book's practical framework overlaps with what Kiriakou describes as [CIA](/wiki/cia) field technique: in a professional intelligence context, a source suspected of lying can be placed on the polygraph; in civilian life, the equivalent tool is patient, extended conversation that gives inconsistencies time to surface.<Cite s="2026-04-23-doug-bopst-escape-system-stop-being-manipulated" t="42:06" />
