Asked to characterise the [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s internal culture in a single word, [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) answered *"incestuous,"* and said there was no other word that would do.<Cite s="2025-09-02-yung-flamingo-club-yung-flamingo-club-john-kiriakou" t="05:41" />

## A club for everything

The first evidence he gives is the completeness of the agency's internal provision for its officers' non-working lives. *"You want to play softball? Great, there's a CIA softball league. You want to make a quilt? Not a problem, there's a CIA quilting club. You're LGBTQ? Not a problem, we have the gigantic LGBTQ group. You want to sing hymns? No problem — Tuesday at seven o'clock, everybody meets in the auditorium to sing hymns."*<Cite s="2025-09-02-yung-flamingo-club-yung-flamingo-club-john-kiriakou" t="06:16" />

He does not present this as a perk but as a design. The purpose, in his reading, is that *"the CIA wants you to devote your entire life to it, and to not have outside friends, or outside interests, outside activities. You can do everything internally."*<Cite s="2025-09-02-yung-flamingo-club-yung-flamingo-club-john-kiriakou" t="07:19" />

## Encouraged romances, and the cleared spouse

The same logic extends to marriage. *"The CIA actively encourages CIA romances, because you're both cleared. And you can talk about work and not get in trouble."*<Cite s="2025-09-02-yung-flamingo-club-yung-flamingo-club-john-kiriakou" t="06:47" />

Kiriakou offers his own two marriages as the demonstration, and the contrast is the argument. His first wife was a ballet teacher, and the marriage *"didn't work out, because she had no understanding whatsoever what I was doing all day and all night."* His second wife was a CIA officer, and *"I could tell her exactly what I was doing."*<Cite s="2025-09-02-yung-flamingo-club-yung-flamingo-club-john-kiriakou" t="06:47" />

He notes the specific counter-intelligence anxiety the arrangement removes: with an uncleared spouse there is always the question of whether there was *"any pillow talk when I revealed a secret"* — a question that does not arise when both parties hold clearances.<Cite s="2025-09-02-yung-flamingo-club-yung-flamingo-club-john-kiriakou" t="07:19" />

## "Decidedly more The Office"

Put the choice between the two television versions of the agency — the thriller and the workplace comedy — Kiriakou did not hesitate: *"Oh, it''s decidedly more The Office."* The illustration he reached for was a standing joke among officers: *"we used to have this ongoing joke that when you went into a meeting, don''t touch the table — because you don''t know who was having sex on it last night."*<Cite s="2026-02-27-one-america-news-network-one-america-news-network" t="12:38" /><Cite s="2026-02-27-one-america-news-network-one-america-news-network" t="13:10" />

His serious gloss on the same question separates the workforce from the institution. John F. Kennedy, he noted, once called the agency *"the best and the brightest"* — *"and oh, how I wish that were true."* There are *"some really brilliant people in there,"* and *"for the most part these are whatever it is now, 30-, 40,000 people who want nothing more than to serve the American people."* The failure he locates in supervision rather than personnel: *"without appropriate oversight, it''s human nature just to push and push and push the envelope until somebody or something pushes back. And as a result you have a CIA that, in a cycle of every twenty or thirty years, goes out of control."*<Cite s="2026-02-27-one-america-news-network-one-america-news-network" t="13:10" /><Cite s="2026-02-27-one-america-news-network-one-america-news-network" t="13:40" />
