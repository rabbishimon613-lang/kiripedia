The **Saudi nuclear program** predates [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s career: when he arrived at the [CIA](/wiki/cia) in 1990, Saudi Arabia's pursuit of the ability to enrich uranium was already old news, the kingdom having sought nuclear power since the 1980s.<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="54:36" />

Kiriakou treats the stated rationale as implausible on its own terms. Saudi Arabia is the sunniest country in the world and has little solar generation; it is windy in the desert and there is little wind power; it has large bodies of water on two sides and little tidal power. "Why nuclear?"<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="55:07" /> The conventional wisdom, he says, has always been that the Saudis want a nuclear weapon — and senior former CIA officers have claimed over the years that they already have one, purchased from the Pakistanis.<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="55:38" />

He raised the subject in July 2026 on reporting that the United States and Saudi Arabia had agreed a nuclear power deal, describing it as a story breaking very slowly that most mainstream outlets were not really examining.<Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="54:36" /><Cite s="2026-07-27-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-middle-east-on-the-brink-of-a-m" t="55:38" />
