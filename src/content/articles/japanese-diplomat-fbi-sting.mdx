The **Japanese diplomat FBI sting** was, in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s telling, an entrapment operation run against him while he was chief investigator on the Senate Foreign Relations Committee. A supposed Japanese diplomat took him to lunch and, at the end, cold-pitched him: *"if you give me information, I can give you money."* Offended, Kiriakou walked out and reported it to the Senate security officer, who sent it to the FBI.<Cite s="2025-08-04-tegan-broadwater-from-the-inside-part-2" t="13:06" /><Cite s="2025-08-04-tegan-broadwater-from-the-inside-part-2" t="14:09" /><Cite s="2025-08-04-tegan-broadwater-from-the-inside-part-2" t="14:39" /> The FBI then had him meet the man five times to draw out what he wanted — but Kiriakou kept reporting each contact back to the FBI, unaware there was no Japanese diplomat: the man was an FBI agent, revealed only in the 15,000 pages of discovery in Kiriakou's later case.<Cite s="2025-08-04-tegan-broadwater-from-the-inside-part-2" t="15:40" /><Cite s="2025-08-04-tegan-broadwater-from-the-inside-part-2" t="16:42" /><Cite s="2025-08-04-tegan-broadwater-from-the-inside-part-2" t="17:14" /> They were trying, he says, to get him to commit *"actual espionage"* so they could charge him.
