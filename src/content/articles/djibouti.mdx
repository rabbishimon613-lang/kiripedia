**Djibouti** sits on the African side of the [Bab el-Mandeb](/wiki/bab-el-mandeb), about sixteen miles across the water from Yemen, and in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account it is the physical basing point of the undeclared American war in [Yemen](/wiki/yemen). He calls the drone base there *"the worst kept secret in the world."*<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="2:10:18" />

## How he worked it out

Kiriakou dates his own realisation to a taxi ride from the airport in Yemen in 2009. He asked the driver how things were, and the driver said they were not bad — except that drones were firing on them, which he attributed to the Saudis. Kiriakou chuckled and said the Saudis did not have drones; then thought again. *"Oh, but we do."* The Americans were in an undeclared war in Yemen, and the drones were based sixteen miles away in Djibouti. He reported it to John Kerry, then chairman of the [Senate Foreign Relations Committee](/wiki/senate-foreign-relations-committee), where Kiriakou was an investigator.<Cite s="2026-05-07-unfiltered-with-s-a-m-unfiltered-with-s-a-m" t="11:26" /><Cite s="2023-04-12-danny-jones" t="1:10:36" />

## Sharing the ground

The base's other feature is who else is on it. Kiriakou notes, with evident relish at the absurdity, that the Americans share the facility with the Chinese — *"there's like a fence down the middle and this side is American"* and the other is not.<Cite s="2025-02-28-epstein-bin-laden-china-israel" t="2:30:33" />

Djibouti also anchors his account of the wider scramble for the Red Sea approaches: Russian ambitions for a base at Port Sudan, and the Israeli offer of recognition to [Somaliland](/wiki/somali-land) — which borders Djibouti — in exchange for a base of its own.<Cite s="2023-04-26-katie-halper-kiriakou-gerald-horne-on-the-indictment-of-ru" t="07:19" /><Cite s="2026-01-06-deep-focus-maduro-capture-cia-deadliest-day" t="25:30" />
