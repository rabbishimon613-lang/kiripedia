**China as economic threat** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s framing that China endangers the West economically rather than militarily. Flush with cash, it has *"essentially been able to buy friends all around the world"* while the U.S. spends a trillion dollars a year on weapons and cannot build nice roads, bridges or bullet trains.<Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="30:13" /><Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="30:46" /> He quotes a Congolese diplomat: *"You promise us democracy. The Chinese promise us food. You can't eat democracy."*<Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="31:19" /> Militarily, he notes, China has one aircraft carrier to America's 24 and one foreign base (in Djibouti), and does not invade its neighbors as the U.S. does — echoing an Onion headline he found true: *"China content to sit back and watch US destroy itself."*<Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="31:52" /><Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="32:23" />

Kiriakou points to Greece as a European example of the same pattern: desperate for money after its 2008 economic collapse, Greece sold the ports of Piraeus — the biggest cargo port in the world — Thessaloniki, and Iraklio to China.<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="2:15:29" />

Kiriakou traces this soft-power reach to specific deals: China built a refinery in the Caribbean — one of only two places in the world, alongside Texas, able to process Venezuela's unusually high-sulfur crude — buying influence with an oil-rich but sanctioned state.<Cite s="2024-08-12-danny-jones-loyal-officer-vs-dissident-spy" t="1:20:52" /> He argues China's history of expansionism is thin — the annexation of Tibet, a brief 1970s border skirmish with Vietnam, occasional friction with India — and that it maintains only one overseas military base, in Djibouti, which it shares with the United States; that a smaller Chinese population would actually raise per-capita GDP, since roughly 10% of the population already generates most of it.<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="1:37:18" /><Cite s="2025-12-03-honesty-box-24-tough-questions" t="23:58" /><Cite s="2024-08-12-danny-jones-loyal-officer-vs-dissident-spy" t="2:53:26" />

On Taiwan, Kiriakou says the U.S. interest is less about the island itself than pressuring the mainland and protecting [Taiwan Semiconductor](/wiki/china-economic-threat), which supplies most of the world's microchips.<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="1:36:48" /> Under the one-China policy adopted during the Carter administration, the U.S. keeps an "interest section" rather than an embassy in Taipei, and currently sails two aircraft carrier battle groups through the South China Sea — a move Kiriakou calls a provocation against China rather than a response to any Chinese threat.<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="1:38:56" /><Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="1:40:01" /> A U.S.-China war, he says, would be "highly technological" — dominated by unprecedented cyberattacks disabling banking and internet access on both sides, with nuclear exchange as the ultimate danger.<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="1:43:10" />

Kiriakou connects this to the credibility of U.S. sanctions policy more broadly: after the U.S. unilaterally withdrew from the [JCPOA](/wiki/iran-nuclear-assessment) Iran nuclear deal in 2017, its own sanctions law required the Treasury to sanction Britain, France, Germany, and Japan for continuing to trade with Iran — which the U.S. did not enforce, a failure he says made America look weak.<Cite s="2024-08-12-danny-jones-loyal-officer-vs-dissident-spy" t="2:56:00" />
