**Congressional intelligence oversight** — the work of the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence and the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence — is something [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) argues does not meaningfully happen. He raises it as the answer to an obvious question: if abuses inside the [CIA](/wiki/cia) are classified, and disclosing them is prosecutable, then who is supposed to be checking?<Cite s="2026-07-21-tucker-carlson-network-ex-cia-agent-john-kiriakou-on-the" t="13:36" /><Cite s="2026-07-21-tucker-carlson-network-ex-cia-agent-john-kiriakou-on-the" t="14:07" />

## "A beef that I have"

Kiriakou introduces the subject as a personal grievance rather than an abstraction — *"That's a beef that I have. I feel very strongly about that."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-tucker-carlson-network-ex-cia-agent-john-kiriakou-on-the" t="14:07" />

His comparison is with committees he regards as functional. Real oversight, in his account, happens with the Armed Services Committee, and to a slightly lesser extent with the Foreign Affairs and Foreign Relations Committees — bodies he knows from the inside, having served as a senior investigator on the Senate Foreign Relations Committee. Set against those, his assessment of the intelligence committees is dismissive: *"but the intelligence committees, forget it. They're just cheerleaders."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-tucker-carlson-network-ex-cia-agent-john-kiriakou-on-the" t="14:38" />

## The chairman who wanted a proxy

The anecdote Kiriakou uses to demonstrate the problem is recent. A sitting senator — a committee chairman — called him a few weeks earlier and asked him to come up for a private meeting. Kiriakou agreed without hesitation: *"of course. I'm a patriot. I want to serve my country."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-tucker-carlson-network-ex-cia-agent-john-kiriakou-on-the" t="15:09" />

What the meeting turned out to be about was a fight between two committee chairmen. Kiriakou's reaction was to point out how little standing he had in it: *"Well, what the heck do you want me to do about it? I mean, seriously, who am I? I'm nobody."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-tucker-carlson-network-ex-cia-agent-john-kiriakou-on-the" t="15:39" />

The senator's request was that Kiriakou write something publicly — for a newspaper — making the argument the chairman would not make himself.<Cite s="2026-07-21-tucker-carlson-network-ex-cia-agent-john-kiriakou-on-the" t="15:39" />

Asked directly whether he thought the senator was afraid, Kiriakou does not hedge: *"Yes, I do... I do."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-tucker-carlson-network-ex-cia-agent-john-kiriakou-on-the" t="16:10" />
