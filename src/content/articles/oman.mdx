**Oman** is the Gulf state [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) names as America's steadiest friend in the region, and the channel he argues Washington should be using to reach Iran.

## "Friends of ours for generations"

Kiriakou's assessment is unusually warm and explicitly non-contingent: *"The Omanis have been friends of ours for generations. It doesn't matter who the president is. It doesn't matter who the Sultan is. The Omanis are friends of ours."*<Cite s="2026-01-29-deprogram-show-with-target-tehran-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and" t="06:14" />

What distinguishes them, in his account, is restraint toward their neighbours — they *"never"* interfere with them. *"They're extraordinarily peaceful people. They're thoughtful. I like the Omanis very much."*<Cite s="2026-01-29-deprogram-show-with-target-tehran-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and" t="06:14" />

## The back channel

The strategic value follows from the same quality: Oman is also close to Iran, and *"many, many times over the years, the Omanis have acted as the go-between between the United States and Iran."*<Cite s="2026-01-29-deprogram-show-with-target-tehran-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and" t="06:45" />

His recommendation is to use it — *"I think we should approach the Omanis again, just like we should approach the Algerians."* The Algerian precedent he cites is the release of the American hostages in 1981: *"It was the Algerians of all people who got our hostages out."* He notes wryly that popular memory has replaced them — *"I always thought it was Ben Affleck."*<Cite s="2026-01-29-deprogram-show-with-target-tehran-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and" t="06:45" />

The contrast he draws is with what was done instead: *"we should engage rather than just send a carrier battle group off the coast of southern Iran and then just make threats. We should be engaging in diplomacy even if that diplomacy is not public."*<Cite s="2026-01-29-deprogram-show-with-target-tehran-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and" t="07:17" />

His view of what such engagement could achieve is expansive: *"I have little doubt that if an American administration reached out to Iran, we could settle our outstanding issues and end up with diplomatic relations and trade relations."* The obstacle he identifies in the envoy's conduct is where the reporting goes — a troubleshooter should do the job, *"and I don't mean then run back to Jerusalem every couple of days to report back."*<Cite s="2026-01-29-deprogram-show-with-target-tehran-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and" t="07:17" /><Cite s="2026-01-29-deprogram-show-with-target-tehran-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and" t="07:47" />

Underneath the diplomacy he places a claim about temperament: *"Americans and Iranians are so much more similar than Americans would bet."* And personally — *"I've never met an Iranian that I didn't like. I mean that with complete sincerity."* The one group he excepts is the Persian exile community in Los Angeles, whom he calls "a different breed."<Cite s="2026-01-29-deprogram-show-with-target-tehran-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and" t="07:47" /><Cite s="2026-01-29-deprogram-show-with-target-tehran-deprogram-with-ted-rall-and" t="08:18" />
