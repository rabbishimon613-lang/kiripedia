The **Publications Review Board** is the CIA office that must clear anything a current or former officer publishes. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) describes the obligation as total: *"Anytime I write something, whether it's a book or an article or a letter to the editor of Home and Garden magazine, I have to send that to the CIA's Publications Review Board, and they have to clear it."*<Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="22:29" />

## Turnaround as a measure of standing

In the ordinary case the process is fast — *"90% of the time, they'll clear it in a couple of hours,"* and a longer article might take 24 hours.<Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="22:29" />

Books are where the difference showed. Of his first: *"when I was on the outs and everybody at the CIA in the leadership hated me, it took me nine months to write my first book and 22 months to get it cleared."* By contrast, his most recent six books *"cleared in a matter of days."*<Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="23:01" />

## Why they cannot stop him now

Kiriakou gives two reasons the board has little purchase on his current work. The first is age and access: much of what he discusses *"is either ancient history, because I left the CIA 21 years ago, or it is based on open-source information."* He is entitled to his own positions and analysis, and *"I don't have access to classified information. And so they can't stop me from saying something."*<Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="23:01" /><Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="23:32" />

The second is statutory. There is a mandatory declassification rule — *"it's 35 years that something has to be declassified after 35 years"* — unless a compelling national security reason applies. The exceptions he lists are material revealing sources and methods, material identifying a source, or anything touching the NSA. *"Otherwise, we can do what we want."*<Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="23:32" /><Cite s="2026-02-03-the-deep-focus-epstein-files-cia-asset-recruitments-worst-spies" t="24:04" />

## Why the books are the licence

Asked what actually restricts him when audiences want stories, Kiriakou gave the clearance process as the answer rather than his own discretion. He does not want to go back to prison, he said — *"but over the years because I've written so many books, I've written now nine books, I've gotten all my stories cleared."*<Cite s="2026-06-11-eb-flow-mehdi-hasan-presses-john-kiriakou-about-his-p" t="06:44" /> The published record, in other words, is the permission slip: material that has been through the [Publications Review Board](/wiki/publications-review-board) once can be told again from a stage.

He names two further releases beside it. The first is time — the [CIA](/wiki/cia) is subject to *"something called the mandatory declassification"*, under which information has to be declassified when it reaches the thirty- or thirty-five-year mark, though as a rule someone must file a Freedom of Information Act request to force it.<Cite s="2026-06-11-eb-flow-mehdi-hasan-presses-john-kiriakou-about-his-p" t="06:44" /><Cite s="2026-06-10-zeteo-abolish-the-cia" t="12:54" /> The second is the category distinction he relies on most in commentary: *"my opinion is not classified so long as it's not based on specifically classified information."*<Cite s="2026-06-10-zeteo-abolish-the-cia" t="12:54" />

None of which, he concedes, empties the vault. Asked directly whether there are things he knows and still cannot say, he agreed there are, and how bad they are drew four words: *"it's it's pretty ugly."*<Cite s="2026-06-10-zeteo-abolish-the-cia" t="12:54" />

## The Devlin evasion

Kiriakou cites one route around the board that he says worked, and that he admires without having attempted. The former chief of station in the Congo, [Larry Devlin](/wiki/larry-devlin), wrote a full career memoir and instructed his daughter to publish it the day after he died. *"And that's what she did. And they never went to the Publications Review Board."* Kiriakou's verdict is *"well played"* — with the observation that the secrecy agreements have presumably been tightened since, *"at least if the lawyers are worth anything."*<Cite s="2026-05-04-dead-drop-s1e26-did-john-le-carre-invent-the-modern-spy" t="1:11:21" /><Cite s="2026-05-04-dead-drop-s1e26-did-john-le-carre-invent-the-modern-spy" t="1:11:55" />

## The NSA hold on the surveillance book

Kiriakou describes a late objection he treats as possible retaliation. The board had cleared all three of the *CIA Insider's Guide* volumes due out together, and then he received an email saying that although the surveillance and surveillance-detection title had been cleared, *"there's another governmental entity that wants to review it, because they may have an objection."*<Cite s="2026-01-15-the-opperman-report-john-kiriakou-on-bob-costello-pardon-4-sale-h" t="28:08" /><Cite s="2026-01-15-the-opperman-report-john-kiriakou-on-bob-costello-pardon-4-sale-h" t="28:39" />

His written answer was that he had *"never signed any secrecy agreements with any other federal government entity. If you guys cleared it, then it's cleared, and it's going to be published."* The board then telephoned him — *"which they're not supposed to do, because everything is supposed to be in writing so we can prove our side in court."*<Cite s="2026-01-15-the-opperman-report-john-kiriakou-on-bob-costello-pardon-4-sale-h" t="29:10" /><Cite s="2026-01-15-the-opperman-report-john-kiriakou-on-bob-costello-pardon-4-sale-h" t="29:42" />

On the call he asked directly: *"look, at least be honest and tell me — was this the [FBI](/wiki/fbi) harassing me? And the guy said, no, it was [NSA](/wiki/national-security-agency). I said, why in the world would NSA care about a book that talks about conducting surveillance or surveillance detection — you know, where you're just driving around in a car following somebody, or driving around making sure that nobody is following you? And he said, I don't know, but they said they need it until April 22nd."* Kiriakou's own verdict is undecided: *"I don't know if that's harassment or not."*<Cite s="2026-01-15-the-opperman-report-john-kiriakou-on-bob-costello-pardon-4-sale-h" t="29:42" /><Cite s="2026-01-15-the-opperman-report-john-kiriakou-on-bob-costello-pardon-4-sale-h" t="30:12" />
