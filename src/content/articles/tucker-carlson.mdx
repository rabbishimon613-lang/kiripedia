**Tucker Carlson** is an American broadcaster and former Fox News host. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) describes him as *"a great friend"* of years' standing, having appeared on Carlson's Fox show roughly a dozen times before the two ever recorded a podcast together, and having had dinner at Carlson's house the night before one such taping.<Cite s="2025-06-05-austin-and-matt" t="00:35" /> Kiriakou says Carlson told him in 2025 that the two now agree on close to 100% of issues — a shift Kiriakou attributes to Carlson becoming more libertarian over time.<Cite s="2025-06-05-austin-and-matt" t="01:41" />

## Lobbying Trump for a pardon

Kiriakou says that after appearing on Carlson's Fox show roughly a dozen times — Carlson, he says, *"loves when I criticize the agency"* — the two became friends, and Carlson personally went to Trump on Kiriakou's behalf to lobby for a pardon, telling him Trump had agreed to do it.<Cite s="2025-02-19-katie-halper" t="1:13:49" /> On the last day of Trump's first term, Kiriakou says Carlson called him to report that Senate Majority Leader Mitch McConnell had killed the pardon: Trump's original plan had been to pardon [Julian Assange](/wiki/julian-assange), Edward Snowden, and Kiriakou together, but McConnell told Carlson that pardoning Assange and Snowden would cost Trump control of the Republican Senate caucus, who would then vote to convict him on his pending impeachment charges — so Trump dropped all three pardons.<Cite s="2025-02-19-katie-halper" t="1:13:49" />

## Presidential ambitions Carlson denies

Kiriakou relays that Glenn Greenwald believes Carlson could win the presidency, and that Kiriakou himself has told Carlson he would support a run. But per Kiriakou, Carlson privately dismisses the idea whenever it comes up, brushing it off and saying flatly that he is not interested.<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="45:51" />

In July 2026 Kiriakou put his own position more strongly than support: *"I've begged him. I've begged him to run for president."* He says Greenwald has done the same, and that Carlson's answer has been consistent — *"I'm not interested at all,"* and, as recently as a week before, *"I'm not going to run. It's not going to happen."* Kiriakou's reply was an open offer: *"If you change your mind, I'm all in and I will do whatever you want me to do to help get the word out."*<Cite s="2026-07-20-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-reveals-the-biggest-unanswered-questions" t="06:15" /><Cite s="2026-07-20-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-reveals-the-biggest-unanswered-questions" t="06:45" />

His assessment of the hypothetical is unqualified: *"I think that he would be an absolutely brilliant presidential candidate and president."*<Cite s="2026-07-20-the-jk-report-john-kiriakou-reveals-the-biggest-unanswered-questions" t="06:45" />

## Life in Maine

Kiriakou describes visiting Carlson at his home in rural Maine, where Carlson lives without security despite Kiriakou's suggestion that he needs it. On one visit, Carlson was a half hour late to record a podcast with Kiriakou because he had been fly fishing — an avid, accomplished angler — and had a stand-off with a 1,000-pound moose on his way back to the car.<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="46:21" />

## The Nick Fuentes interview

Kiriakou intervened in the conservative split over Carlson's interview with Nick Fuentes on the side of the interview, while making clear he would not have conducted it himself. His objection is to the premise of the boycott: *"Why wouldn't you interview Nick Fuentes? If you think he's a turd with repulsive positions, then expose him as a turd with repulsive positions."* Refusing to speak to someone, in his account, is worse than the conversation — *"talk to him and let him destroy himself with his repulsive positions."*<Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="1:00:27" /> The subsequent demand that Carlson be cut off, and that the head of the Heritage Foundation resign for saying something kind about him, drew his sharpest line: *"What's wrong with you people?"*<Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="1:00:57" />

He attributes the booking itself to Carlson's changed circumstances rather than to sympathy: since leaving Fox News, Carlson *"feels liberated… unencumbered by corporate structures and policies"* and now books anyone he thinks will give him two or three hours of interesting conversation. Kiriakou notes the interview was, by his interviewer's account, a normal Tucker conversation in which Carlson freely disagreed.<Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="1:05:40" /><Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="1:07:14" />

Kiriakou's own reason for declining such a booking is not principle but futility, and he states it as bias: *"I see him as just some kid. He's just some loudmouth kid."* Many of Fuentes's positions have been definitively debunked by historians — *"there are still people alive who liberated Dachau"* — and his support, in Kiriakou's estimate, cannot grow beyond *"a bunch of angry white guys in their 20s."* He concedes Fuentes is a good speaker, bright, funny, and disarming at first glance.<Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="1:10:23" /><Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="1:10:54" /><Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="1:11:25" />

He places the episode within a broader argument that Americans have lost the ability to listen to people they disagree with, illustrated from his own family — a royalist grandmother and a republican grandfather who both became Democrats, uncles who voted for George Wallace in 1968 — and from his high-school friendships, split between Republicans and Democrats, in which *"it never mattered to any of us."* It is the same reason he gives for going on Fox News: *"maybe I can educate a Fox News viewer."*<Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="57:52" /><Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="59:25" /><Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="59:56" />

## Kiriakou's assessment

Kiriakou calls Carlson *"utterly fearless"* and possibly the most brilliant, best-informed person he has ever encountered, adding that what audiences see on camera is genuinely who Carlson is: a nice, devout, family-oriented person who loves the country.<Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="47:22" />
