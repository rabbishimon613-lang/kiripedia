**John O. Brennan** is the former Director of the [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia), serving under the Obama administration. In KiriPedia's source corpus, [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) characterizes Brennan as the principal architect of the agency's [enhanced interrogation techniques](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation) program and as the individual responsible — through the Espionage Act prosecution that followed Kiriakou's 2007 public confirmation of CIA waterboarding — for the ruination of Kiriakou's own life. *"He ruined your life. He, I believe, committed crimes, by the way, to ruin your life."*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="27:34" /> Kiriakou describes Brennan as the public face of the moral case against torture while privately having been *"the godfather of the torture program."*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="27:34" />

Brennan has been known to Kiriakou for approximately thirty-five years, dating from the start of Kiriakou's CIA career. Their mutual antipathy is, per Kiriakou, unbroken across that span: *"John and I have always hated each other. I never liked him. I never trusted him. I believe that he is the archetypal sociopath."* Brennan's institutional advancement, in Kiriakou's account, was structurally dependent on the patronage of [George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet) — the *"rabbi"* relationship the agency's promotion culture turns on: *"He had a rabbi in George Tenet. And the next thing you know, he's at the top of the heap."*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="20:41" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="47:36" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="48:07" />

## Early career: the Kesler firing and the Tenet rabbi origin

Brennan's institutional trajectory at the [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia) was shaped decisively by a single sequence of events in late 1993 or early 1994. As deputy to senior CIA Syria analyst [Martha Kesler](/wiki/martha-kesler), Brennan held a GS-15 staff position with no realistic promotion path. He approached Kesler asking for her endorsement to the [Senior Intelligence Service](/wiki/cia) (SIS). Her reply: *"Not only will you never be a member of the senior intelligence service, I don't even want you working for me anymore. You're fired."*<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="36:30" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="37:01" />

CIA firings of this type provide a six-week window to find an alternative position. Brennan was given his six weeks the week before Christmas. The only position available was on the President's Daily Brief staff as a morning briefer — and Brennan was assigned to brief the lowest-ranking PDB recipient: [George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet), then the National Security Council Director for Intelligence Programs. The two men — both *"alpha dogs, cigar-smoking, hard-drinking"* — *"immediately hit it off,"* customarily walking after each morning briefing to a kiosk at the corner of 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue adjacent to the White House, where Tenet (who had already had a heart attack and whose wife had forbidden cigars at home) would buy them and stand there laughing.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="37:32" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="38:05" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="38:35" />

## Riyadh station chief and the 9/11 visa approvals

On Tenet's elevation to Deputy DCIA, Brennan was made Tenet's morning briefer and then — despite having no operational experience and never having recruited a spy — appointed Chief of Station in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. *"He's an analyst. He's never served overseas before. Never recruited a spy ever. It wasn't his job. Now all of a sudden, he's the the station chief in one of the most important stations in the world."* During Brennan's tenure as Riyadh chief, the U.S. embassy under his oversight approved the visa applications of the September 11 hijackers.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="39:36" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="40:10" />

## The 2007 Obama bet

The 2007 wave of senior CIA retirements created a forced political-affiliation choice among the retirees for the following year's presidential election. Per Kiriakou, *"half of them went to the McCain campaign and half of them went to the Hillary Clinton campaign — and John Brennan was literally the only one who went to the Obama campaign."* This single choice, in Kiriakou's account, is what *"saved his career"* — the Obama victory positioned Brennan for the Deputy National Security Adviser appointment from which he would subsequently shape the Espionage Act prosecution of Kiriakou.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="42:14" />

## The Espionage Act prosecution

Brennan is, in Kiriakou's account, the individual personally responsible for Kiriakou's Espionage Act prosecution. The Bush administration had closed the case against Kiriakou after his December 2007 ABC News interview confirming CIA waterboarding; *"the Bush administration said I had not committed a crime. They closed the case."* On Brennan's arrival at the National Security Council in 2009 as Deputy National Security Adviser for Counterterrorism, Brennan wrote a memo to U.S. Attorney General [Eric Holder](#) asking the Department of Justice to reopen the case under espionage charges. Holder declined: *"My people don't think he committed espionage."* Brennan's reply: *"Charge him anyway and make him defend himself."*<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="48:37" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="49:10" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="49:40" />

The result was a five-count indictment — three counts of espionage among them. The Justice Department subsequently waited until Kiriakou was bankrupt and then dropped the three espionage counts; Kiriakou took a plea to a lesser charge and served 23 months in federal prison.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="49:40" />

## The Tuesday morning kill list

In his 2009 NSC role, Brennan instituted the [Tuesday morning kill list](/wiki/tuesday-morning-kill-list) — a weekly meeting at which a written list of targets to be killed in the following week was produced and the assignments distributed. The program was, per Kiriakou, sustained through the Obama administration and is the institutional source of the often-noted observation that *"nobody dropped more missiles from drones than Obama."*<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="45:28" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="47:02" />

## Private-sector role (2005)

In 2005, between his CIA service and his appointment as Deputy National Security Adviser, Brennan was appointed CEO of [The Analysis Corporation](/wiki/analysis-corporation), an Arlington, Virginia private intelligence firm originally configured as an LLC pass-through for retired Senior Intelligence Service analysts. He has also sat on the boards of various Beltway defense contractors including BAE Systems.<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="50:10" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="52:14" />

## The 2025–2026 investigation

As of January 2026, Brennan is the subject of an active U.S. Department of Justice investigation under the second Trump administration. The investigation centers on three allegations:

1. Coordination of the October 2020 public letter by intelligence-community veterans casting doubt on the authenticity of the Hunter Biden laptop
2. Coordination of the post-2016-election CIA analysis attributing Donald Trump's electoral victory to Russian interference
3. An ongoing conspiracy — under this theory the statute of limitations does not run — to *"deny American voters their duly-elected president"*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="29:38" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="30:10" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="30:41" />

The U.S. Attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia — the district where the alleged conduct would have occurred — declined to bring charges, was subsequently fired by the Trump administration, and the case was transferred to the Southern District of Florida on the theory that the relevant memos were *"read on the MSNBC website"* in Miami.<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="23:57" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="24:29" />

## Public behavior under pressure

In late 2025 and early 2026 Brennan exhibited public behavior that Kiriakou — who has known him for thirty-five years — characterizes as out of character and indicative of fear. Three incidents:

- At George Mason University in Arlington, Virginia, Brennan was publicly challenged by a Republican congressional candidate over his role in the Hunter Biden laptop letter and the 2016 Russia analysis. *"Brennan actually pokes the guy in the chest several times. Besides that being assault, that was the first time in the 35 years that I've known John Brennan that I ever saw him lose his composure ever."*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="20:41" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="21:13" />
- On MSNBC the same night, Brennan appeared *"exhausted and old beyond his years"* and said he did not understand why the Trump administration was investigating him — a denial that *"even by MSNBC standards, was not believable or credible."*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="21:46" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="22:18" />
- A few days later, in a Metro station, Brennan confronted an individual who had shouted a question at him — *"went after him, went up to him with his finger and raised voice."*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="22:18" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="22:51" />

Kiriakou's assessment of the pattern: *"He's scared."*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="22:51" />

## Kiriakou's distinction between Brennan and other senior CIA figures

Kiriakou identifies Brennan as distinct from other senior CIA officers of the post-9/11 period — including [George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet), John McLaughlin, Michael Morell, [Jose Rodriguez](/wiki/jose-rodriguez), and Rick Pavitt — on the following ground:

> There were a lot of crimes that were committed in the name of national security. Brennan's different. Brennan plotted against an elected president of the United States, and he tried to use the intelligence community and this lawfare against him. And we can't risk that happening again.<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="33:50" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="34:21" />

## Kiriakou's view of the prosecution

Kiriakou's position on Brennan's prosecution is bounded by his commitment to the prosecutorial principle articulated by Robert H. Jackson: see ["Pick the man, then find the crime"](/wiki/pick-the-man-principle). Kiriakou supports prosecution of Brennan for any crime that can be demonstrated by evidence, including specifically the *"ongoing conspiracy"* theory advanced by the Justice Department. He opposes a prosecution conducted by *"scouring the law books looking for a crime"* against a pre-selected defendant — *"even a defendant who ruined my life."*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="28:35" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="29:07" />

## The "charge him anyway" Discovery

Per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), classified discovery during his own prosecution revealed Brennan's direct hand in the case. After three years of investigation the Department of Justice's professional staff had concluded Kiriakou had not committed espionage. Brennan, per the discovered correspondence, wrote back to Attorney General Eric Holder: *"Charge him anyway and make him defend himself."*<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="3:02:11" /><Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="19:34" />

## *"One of the fathers of the torture program"*

Per Kiriakou: *"John Brennan was literally the only former senior CIA officer to endorse Obama. All the others were pretty evenly split. Brennan was one of the fathers of the torture program — which he later said in that 2015 documentary CIA Spymasters … he tried to paint himself, because he was the current head of the CIA at that point, as someone who's like 'the CIA should be above torture' — I'm like, mala you fuck."*<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="2:55:28" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="2:55:59" />

The institutional position Kiriakou is referring to: Brennan was Executive Director of the CIA — the number-three position — when the [enhanced interrogation](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation) program was designed and implemented.<Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="19:04" />

## "Brennan plotted against an elected president"

Per Kiriakou on the Dorey Vault 7 episode, Brennan stands apart from other CIA wrongdoers Kiriakou criticizes ([George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet), John McLaughlin, Mike Morell, [Jose Rodriguez](/wiki/jose-rodriguez), Rick Prado): *"Brennan plotted against an elected president of the United States, and he tried to use the intelligence community and this lawfare against him — and we can't risk that happening again."*<Cite s="2025-11-19-julian-dorey-vault-7" t="04:48" />

## Role in Kiriakou prosecution

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) has identified [Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan) as the primary driver of his prosecution. When Kiriakou was arrested, the Justice Department turned over 15,000 pages of classified discovery, within which Kiriakou's lawyers found three memos. The first was a memo from Brennan to Attorney General Eric Holder demanding that Kiriakou be charged with espionage. Holder responded that his lawyers did not believe Kiriakou had committed espionage. Brennan then wrote back: *"Charge him anyway and make him defend himself."*<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="11:00" />

Kiriakou described Brennan as *"one enemy in the [CIA](/wiki/cia) in particular"* and attributed to Brennan his selective prosecution — at a time when CIA Director David Petraeus had himself confirmed the names of ten covert CIA officers to his biographer and girlfriend without being charged.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="10:29" /> The espionage charges were eventually dropped when Kiriakou went bankrupt from attorney's fees.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="11:00" />

## Professional unqualification

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described [Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan) as professionally unqualified to serve as [CIA](/wiki/cia) Director. Brennan was an analyst, not a case officer; he never recruited an agent in his career. The CIA nonetheless made him station chief in Riyadh — a position that, in Kiriakou's account, required skills Brennan had never developed.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="18:00" />

## Vindictiveness

Kiriakou described Brennan as vindictive by temperament. He recounted that early in Brennan's career, a female supervisor had let him go — the equivalent of a poor performance review. Years later, after Brennan had accumulated institutional power, he had the woman fired from the [CIA](/wiki/cia). Kiriakou cited this as illustrative of how Brennan used authority: not to serve institutional interests but to settle personal scores.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="18:30" />

## Disregard for law

Kiriakou characterized Brennan as not merely amoral but actively contemptuous of legal constraints. He described Brennan as dangerous specifically because he did not feel bound by rules that constrained others — including rules governing the treatment of whistleblowers and the use of [enhanced interrogation](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation).<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="19:00" />

## Security-clearance revocation

President Trump revoked Brennan's security clearance. Kiriakou noted this without editorial characterization, presenting it as a factual endpoint to Brennan's post-government intelligence access.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="19:30" />

## *"Charge him anyway and make him defend himself"*

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) repeated his account of [John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan)'s role in his prosecution: Brennan wrote to Attorney General Eric Holder demanding Kiriakou be charged with espionage. Holder wrote back saying his people did not believe Kiriakou had committed espionage. Brennan replied: *"Charge him anyway and make him defend himself."* Kiriakou was arrested and charged with five felonies including three counts of espionage. The government waited until he had spent $2 million in legal fees and gone bankrupt before dropping the espionage charges ten months later.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="44:30" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="45:00" />

## Full career arc — GS-14 to CIA Director

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described having known [John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan) since 1990 and provided an account of his career that he described as having never told publicly before.

In 1990, Brennan was a GS-14 and the deputy director of the Arab-Israeli analysis group. He worked for a woman named Martha Kessler, whom Kiriakou described as one of the great minds and great thinkers on Syria. They did not get along; Brennan had been imposed on her.<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:53:30" />

## Firing by Kessler — and revenge

Brennan approached Kessler and said he was ready for a promotion to the Senior Intelligence Service and wanted her blessing. Her response: *"Not only are you not ready for the senior service, I don't even want you to work for me anymore. You're fired."*<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:54:00" />

At the [CIA](/wiki/cia), a firing is not final dismissal: the officer has six weeks to walk the halls and find any other job. If no job is found within six weeks, the badge is taken and the officer is actually terminated. The firing occurred around Christmas, when no positions were open — the CIA's annual job turnover happens in the summer. Brennan struggled to find a position.<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:54:00" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:54:30" />

## Tenet origin — PDB briefer

Brennan eventually found an opening on the President's Daily Brief staff — specifically, briefing the lowest-ranking official on the National Security Council entitled to a PDB: the Director of Intelligence Programs, who at the time was [George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet). Brennan and Tenet immediately hit it off. Kiriakou's description: both were *"alpha dogs — cigar smoking, hard drinking, pockmark-faced guys."*<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:54:30" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:55:00" />

When Tenet was promoted to Deputy Director of the [CIA](/wiki/cia), he named Brennan director of the Office of Near Eastern and South Asian Analysis — Martha Kessler's boss. Brennan called Kessler into the office and told her: *"Now you're fired."* She retired.<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:55:00" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:55:30" />

The office was subsequently reorganized and Brennan was squeezed out, but Tenet then made him station chief in Riyadh — despite Brennan having had, in Kiriakou's words, *"not five seconds of operational experience in his life."*<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:55:30" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:56:00" />

## 9/11 hijacker visas

Kiriakou stated: *"It was [John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan) who oversaw the granting of the visas to the 9/11 hijackers, for God's sake."*<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:56:00" />

## Executive Director — torture advocacy

Brennan returned from Riyadh and became Executive Director of the [CIA](/wiki/cia) — the number-three ranking official — under Director Tenet and Deputy Director John McLaughlin. He was present in Kiriakou's briefings every day in this period. Kiriakou's characterization: *"John was just torture, torture, torture. We got to torture these guys. We got to do this. We got to do that. We need to start killing more people. We need to get out there and start shooting."*<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:56:30" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:57:00" />

The Deputy Director for Operations could not stand Brennan and went to Tenet: *"Come on, man. You got to get him out."* Tenet made Brennan the head of the TTIC — the Transnational Terrorist Information Center, which later became the National Counterterrorism Center (NCTC). Brennan had been telling everyone he wanted to be Secretary of Defense; the TTIC was not all he had hoped for. He retired a year before George W. Bush finished his second term.<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:57:00" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:57:30" />

## Obama — kill list — CIA Director

In the wave of retirements following 2008, roughly half of [CIA](/wiki/cia) senior-level retirees joined the McCain campaign and the other half joined the Clinton campaign. Brennan was the only one who went to Obama. When Obama won, Brennan wanted the CIA directorship. Liberal groups objected — he was one of the architects of the torture program. Obama instead made him Deputy National Security Adviser for Counterterrorism, from which position he ran the [Tuesday-morning kill list](/wiki/tuesday-morning-kill-list).<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:57:30" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:58:00" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="1:58:30" />

## The rise of a swamp creature (American Conservative)

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) traces [John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan)'s improbable rise. In the mid-1990s Brennan was a GS-15 nobody, deputy chief of the Arab-Israeli analysis group under Syria expert [Martha Kesler](/wiki/martha-kesler); when he asked her blessing for promotion, she fired him — *"you will never be promoted into the senior intelligence service."*<Cite s="2026-02-19-american-conservative-cia-propaganda-going-viral" t="09:24" /><Cite s="2026-02-19-american-conservative-cia-propaganda-going-viral" t="09:54" /> Pounding the pavement, he landed as the PDB briefer for the lowest-ranking official entitled to the president's daily brief — who happened to be [George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet). The two *"ugly, pockfaced alpha dogs"* bonded over cigars at a kiosk on 17th Street; when Tenet became deputy CIA director, he brought Brennan back and made him Kesler's boss — and Brennan's first act was to fire Kesler.<Cite s="2026-02-19-american-conservative-cia-propaganda-going-viral" t="10:57" /><Cite s="2026-02-19-american-conservative-cia-propaganda-going-viral" t="11:29" /><Cite s="2026-02-19-american-conservative-cia-propaganda-going-viral" t="12:31" /> Squeezed out months later, Brennan — with *"never five seconds of operational experience"* — was made station chief in Riyadh, where Kiriakou believes he ordered visas for the 9/11 hijackers to try to recruit them.<Cite s="2026-02-19-american-conservative-cia-propaganda-going-viral" t="13:03" /><Cite s="2026-02-19-american-conservative-cia-propaganda-going-viral" t="14:06" /> He rose to the agency's number-three, founded the TTIC (now the National Counterterrorism Center), and in the 2007 retirement wave was the only one of some 180 departing officers to join the Obama campaign — which made him a shoo-in until the progressive left blocked his CIA nomination; Obama parked him as deputy national security adviser (no Senate confirmation), and by 2012 he was CIA director after all.<Cite s="2026-02-19-american-conservative-cia-propaganda-going-viral" t="13:34" /><Cite s="2026-02-19-american-conservative-cia-propaganda-going-viral" t="16:42" /><Cite s="2026-02-19-american-conservative-cia-propaganda-going-viral" t="17:43" />
