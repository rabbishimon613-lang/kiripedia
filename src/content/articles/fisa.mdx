**FISA** — the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act — is the U.S. legal framework that governs surveillance warrants for foreign-intelligence purposes. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) describes it as one of the most easily abused instruments in the surveillance state.

## Carter Page

[Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) cites the case of Carter Page, against whom the Steele dossier — which he characterizes as the *"dirty dossier"* — was used to support *"not one, but four FISA applications."* His framing: *"You know how easy it is to ruin a person's life with FISA?"* He notes Page was driven into bankruptcy, and that one of Page's lawyers was the same attorney Kiriakou had used.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="45:00" />

## Part of the surveillance architecture

[Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) groups FISA with the [Patriot Act](/wiki/patriot-act) and [Section 702](/wiki/section-702) as the legal machinery that erodes Americans' constitutional protections. He names [Thomas Massie](/wiki/thomas-massie), Rand Paul, and Ro Khanna as among the very few legislators willing to oppose it, concluding that three votes out of 535 cannot turn the tide — and that he is not optimistic about the future of American privacy.<Cite s="2026-02-10-deep-focus-favorite-cia-work-nato-espionage-privacy" t="21:00" /> He cites Mike Johnson as a case study in how the security state co-opts even its critics: Johnson once led a congressional Weaponization Committee investigating FBI mass-surveillance abuses, but became the tie-breaking vote to approve Section 702 surveillance without reforms once he became Speaker of the House.<Cite s="2025-06-25-harrison-berger" t="14:29" />
