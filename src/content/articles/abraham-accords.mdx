The **Abraham Accords** are the normalization agreements between Israel and a run of Arab states brokered under [Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump) — and one of [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s standing examples of dealmaking mistaken for peacemaking.

## "A punch in the throat to the Palestinians"

Kiriakou grants the Accords were *"a great victory for Israel and for the Gulf Arabs"* — and calls them *"a punch in the throat to the Palestinians."*<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="1:55:03" /> When Trump promised in 2016 to fix the Israeli-Palestinian problem in a day, Kiriakou says, this is what he meant: encourage Bahrain, the Emirates, Sudan, Morocco — eventually the Saudis — to normalize with Israel, *"and you just screw the Palestinians. And that's just not going to work, no matter how many times he tries."*<Cite s="2025-02-07-american-exception-exceptional-american-failures-john-kiriakou-d" t="12:02" /><Cite s="2025-02-07-american-exception-exceptional-american-failures-john-kiriakou-d" t="12:34" /> His summary judgment: *"a terrible idea in the first place."*<Cite s="2025-02-07-american-exception-exceptional-american-failures-john-kiriakou-d" t="05:14" />

## October 7

In Kiriakou's analysis, Hamas attacked in part because the world was moving away from the Palestinian cause — the Accords had passed, the Saudis were preparing to normalize, and the Palestinians had been thrown under the bus. *"Hamas was right"* about the abandonment.<Cite s="2025-09-22-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="30:22" /> Netanyahu had put his legacy on the Accords, which *"fell apart on October 7."*<Cite s="2025-06-30-jamarl-thomas-how-mossad-cia-recruit-in-iran" t="04:13" /> The Saudis had watched a diplomatic agreement with Israel *"slipping through their hands."*<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="1:55:03" />

## The revival project

Kiriakou reads Trump's second-term Middle East policy as an Accords-revival campaign: the *"big overarching all-settling deal"* — bring in the Saudis eventually, declare victory, *"and get your [Nobel Peace Prize](/wiki/nobel-peace-prize)."*<Cite s="2025-07-14-danny-jones" t="52:33" /> His own prescription for Israel runs through the same door: declare Hamas destroyed, hand Gaza to the UN, then ask the Saudis to join the Accords and negotiate later.<Cite s="2025-07-28-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra-deprogram-show-with-ted-ra" t="13:40" /> Even Israel's non-response to the strike on Qatar, he argues, was shaped by the Accords calculus — preserving Doha's potential membership while *"all of the Arabs are looking at us."*<Cite s="2025-09-12-dialogue-works-john-kiriakou-israel-humiliates-itself-middle" t="06:55" />

## Why they keep expanding

By late 2025 Kiriakou was reading the Accords' durability as evidence for an uncomfortable proposition he shares with critics of Israel and defenders alike: that much of the Arab world does not greatly care about the Palestinians, who function as *"a useful tool of propaganda"* rather than a constituency. Asked directly whether the Israelis are right about that, his answer was *"I think so too"* — and he offers it as one of the reasons the Accords have been *"so popular and so well received around the Middle East."*<Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="39:37" />

He notes that Kazakhstan joined in the week before the interview — *"of all places"* — alongside Bahrain, the United Arab Emirates, Tunisia, Morocco and Sudan, and repeats that Saudi Arabia remains the object: *"the crown jewel is going to be Saudi Arabia. And I think eventually Saudi Arabia joins as well, because the bottom line is it'll be good for the Saudi economy."*<Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="40:08" /> He qualifies the Saudi position by pointing to the king's full title — not merely Malik but Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques — which requires pristine Islamic credentials that, in his assessment, none of the family actually possess.<Cite s="2025-11-18-nuclear-war-vault-7-mossad-in-iran" t="40:39" />

## The crown jewel

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) traces the accords to a 2016 campaign promise to end the Arab-Israeli dispute in twenty-four hours, which he reads as a plan to sideline the Palestinians and get Arab governments to open relations with Israel directly — an effort he judges largely successful, bringing in Morocco, Sudan, the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain alongside Egypt and Jordan.<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="25:09" /><Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="25:40" />

Saudi Arabia is the prize still outstanding, and not merely because it is the largest and most populous state on the Arabian Peninsula: it is the land of the two holy mosques, "the very beating heart of Islam."<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="26:11" />

Kiriakou argues the accords work because Gulf governments no longer feel obliged to the Palestinians, a rupture he dates to the [expulsion of the Palestinian professional class from the Gulf](/wiki/palestinians-in-the-gulf) after [Yasser Arafat](/wiki/yasser-arafat) backed Iraq in 1990.<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="28:50" /><Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="29:23" /> [Lindsey Graham](/wiki/lindsey-graham) saw Saudi-Israeli normalisation as the defining prize of a broader post-war settlement, had worked the issue for years including with the Biden administration, and believed Iran's weakening created a rare opening.<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="30:27" />
