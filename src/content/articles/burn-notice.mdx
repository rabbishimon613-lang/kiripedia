***Burn Notice*** is the spy series for which [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) served as script adviser in its sixth season, having been a fan of it first.

## The flight

Kiriakou's account begins with an exception to his own habit. Flying from Washington to Los Angeles, he found himself beside a man he did not know — *"I never talk to anybody on planes, but I was sitting next to this guy"* — and struck up a conversation anyway. The man was Matt Nix, whom Kiriakou describes as *"an absolutely brilliant and gifted writer and showrunner."*<Cite s="2026-05-19-gq-ex-cia-officer-john-kiriakou-breaks-down-spy" t="20:22" />

Asked what he did for a living, Nix said he had created a show. Asked whether it was one Kiriakou might have heard of, he said: *"Well, maybe. It's the number one show on basic cable. It's called Burn Notice."*<Cite s="2026-05-19-gq-ex-cia-officer-john-kiriakou-breaks-down-spy" t="20:22" />

Kiriakou's reply gave away how closely he had been watching. He had seen every episode, and — wanting to know who was keeping the tradecraft straight — *"I ran the credits in slow motion just to see who your script advisor was."* Nix's answer was that there wasn't one: he had been obsessed with the [CIA](/wiki/cia) since he was a child and had read every book on it.<Cite s="2026-05-19-gq-ex-cia-officer-john-kiriakou-breaks-down-spy" t="20:52" />

*"By the time we landed in LA, I was the script advisor for season 6. And Matt and I are still friends."*<Cite s="2026-05-19-gq-ex-cia-officer-john-kiriakou-breaks-down-spy" t="20:52" />

## The point of the story

Kiriakou uses the episode as his counterexample to the general complaint that screen spycraft is nonsense: *"sometimes writers take this so seriously that they make it 100% accurate."*<Cite s="2026-05-19-gq-ex-cia-officer-john-kiriakou-breaks-down-spy" t="20:52" />
