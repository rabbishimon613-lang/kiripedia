**Afghan heroin policy** is the term [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john_kiriakou) uses for the post‑2001 United States arrangement in [Afghanistan](/wiki/afghanistan) under which heroin‑poppy cultivation was, according to Kiriakou’s 2009 investigation for the [Senate Foreign Relations Committee](/wiki/senate_foreign_relations_committee), protected and actively encouraged by U.S. military and [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia) personnel.<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="14:43" /><Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="22:10" />

The policy emerged in the immediate aftermath of the 2001 invasion, when senior U.S. officials concluded that a flourishing opium economy could be leveraged as a geopolitical instrument. It was framed as a means to weaken regional adversaries by flooding their societies with narcotics, a rationale that shaped operational decisions across the Department of Defense and the CIA throughout the 2000s.

## Strategic rationale and origins

U.S. planners viewed the Afghan poppy field as a lever in a larger contest with regional powers. A senior [Drug Enforcement Administration](/wiki/drug_enforcement_administration) officer, speaking at a secret DEA facility in rural Virginia, summed up the strategic calculus: *"Buddy, you know you're never going to get this published, right? Afghanistan produces 93% of the world's heroin. All of that heroin goes to Iran and Russia, and we want them to be addicted to heroin. It weakens their societies. It weakens their cultures. It makes them easier to beat if we have to fight them."*<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="21:36" /><Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="22:10" />

The same officer noted that the United States also benefited from a domestic heroin supply that largely bypassed Afghanistan, stating that *"it's just too hard to get it from Afghanistan all the way to the United States when it can just come right up through Mexico."*<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="22:41" />

## Implementation and field evidence

Kiriakou notes that *"The Taliban banned the cultivation of heroin poppy. So in 2000, Afghanistan accounted for 0% of the world's heroin."* By the time he travelled to [Kandahar](/wiki/kandahar) and [Lashkar Gah](/wiki/lashkar_gah) as senior investigator for Senator Kerry’s committee, Afghanistan was producing *"93% of the world's heroin."*<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="15:14" /><Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="16:21" /> In a separate retelling he adds that as recently as the 1970s Afghanistan was a net food exporter, feeding itself and exporting rice to Pakistan and Iran, before farmers converted their fields to opium poppy after the Taliban's overthrow.<Cite s="2026-06-16-julian-dorey-daily-mkultra-twin-experiment" t="02:04" /> DEA charts he was shown corroborate the reversal: the Taliban produced almost no poppy while in power, and none at all in 2001.<Cite s="2026-06-16-julian-dorey-daily-mkultra-twin-experiment" t="09:54" />

When the military balked at flying him to Kandahar and Helmand provinces, Kiriakou says he invoked his Senate staff rank — equivalent to a Brigadier General — to force the flight.<Cite s="2026-06-16-julian-dorey-daily-mkultra-twin-experiment" t="03:35" /> He flew into Lashkar Gah, where a State Department-led Provincial Reconstruction Team worked alongside military and USAID staff on projects like electrical grids and water wells, and where, approaching the town, poppy fields stretched for miles in every direction with rivers diverted to irrigate them.<Cite s="2026-06-16-julian-dorey-daily-mkultra-twin-experiment" t="05:09" /><Cite s="2026-06-16-julian-dorey-daily-mkultra-twin-experiment" t="05:40" />

Through a State Department contact who ran the Provincial Reconstruction Team in Lashkar Gah, Kiriakou persuaded a reluctant U.S. military escort to take him into the poppy fields. Speaking through a Pashtu translator, he asked a farmer why he cultivated poppy instead of vegetables that could be harvested twice a year and exported. The farmer replied, *"The Americans told me in 2001 that if I told them where the Arabs were, I could grow all the poppy I wanted."* Kiriakou pressed, *"What American told you you could grow poppy?"* At that moment the U.S. security officer escorting Kiriakou said *"meeting over"* and physically pulled him back to the jeep.<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="19:00" /><Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="19:30" /><Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="20:03" /><Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="20:34" />

The farmer’s testimony illustrates how the policy was communicated at the grassroots level: promises of impunity in exchange for cooperation with U.S. intelligence objectives. The abrupt termination of the interview by the escort underscores the sensitivity of the subject within the chain of command. A DEA contact separately warned Kiriakou his paper would never be published, since the heroin flowing to Russia and Iran was seen as deliberately weakening those societies — reinforced, he says, by a military contact who showed him a nighttime satellite heat-image of southern Afghanistan, where pinpricks of light turned out to be heroin processors cooking poppy in small huts across the region.<Cite s="2026-06-16-julian-dorey-daily-mkultra-twin-experiment" t="07:47" /><Cite s="2026-06-16-julian-dorey-daily-mkultra-twin-experiment" t="10:24" />

## CIA counter‑terrorism operations in Afghanistan

In a 2021 briefing, Kiriakou recounted that *"Zubaydah created and ran the two al‑Qaeda training camps in southern Afghanistan. He formed the… house of martyrs safe house in Peshawar, Pakistan…"*<Cite s="2021-05-12-howie-hawkins-greensocialist-notes-23-whistleblowers-with-john-kiriak" t="13:28" />

Kiriakou further described his direct orders during the operation: *"my orders were to sit next to his bed 24/7. George Tenet told me 24/7 CIA eyes on. He said do not leave his bedside and so I sat there 24 hours. After we caught him Abu Zubaydah came out of his coma and the first thing he said to me was he asked me to kill him, he asked me to take a pillow and smother him and I said I said oh no Abu Zubaydah."*<Cite s="2021-05-12-howie-hawkins-greensocialist-notes-23-whistleblowers-with-john-kiriak" t="14:00" />

Later, Kiriakou noted the CIA’s handling of the detainee: *"we've been looking for you for a long time. Nobody's going to kill you. In fact I said you're going to get the best medical care that the American government could provide. He cried a lot, he said that he would never know the touch of a woman, he would never know the joy of fatherhood… he recited poetry, he asked if I would pass a message to his mother."*<Cite s="2021-05-12-howie-hawkins-greensocialist-notes-23-whistleblowers-with-john-kiriak" t="14:33" />

Kiriakou also highlighted the broader dilemma of detainee rights: *"Washington Post saying that the last minor prisoner is being released from Guantanamo. He's an Afghan that leaves only one Afghan left at Guantanamo. He's one of 70 people and at its height it had something like 789 people… but my god even Khalid Muhammad has constitutional rights so either charge him with a crime or let him go. Same with Abu Zubaydah, same with Ramsay Ben and Shib, same with all these guys. If you're not going to charge them with a crime you have to let them go. They have the same right that you and I have to face our accusers in a court of law…"*<Cite s="2021-05-12-howie-hawkins-greensocialist-notes-23-whistleblowers-with-john-kiriak" t="1:00:23" />

*"or let him go same with Abu Zubaydah same with Ramsay Ben and Shib same with all these guys if you're not going to charge them the crime you have to let them go they have the same right that you and i have to face our accusers in a court of law to face a jury of their peers and if we're not going to allow them to do that then they have to be released with that said i don't see guantanamo closing down until either two things"*<Cite s="2021-05-12-howie-hawkins-greensocialist-notes-23-whistleblowers-with-john-kiriak" t="1:00:54" />

*"happen these prisoners eventually die and you know that that happens with some frequency usually by suicide uh or somehow the government is able to charge them in a civilian court but the reason that hasn't happened yet is because the cia tortured them all and because they were tortured literally nothing that they said can be used against them and without their own admissions then there's just nothing to hold them on"*<Cite s="2021-05-12-howie-hawkins-greensocialist-notes-23-whistleblowers-with-john-kiriak" t="1:01:24" />

Operational insights from a 2022 debrief describe a shift in tactics along the Afghan‑Pakistan border: *"the rest of them are gonna run into Pakistan right because we can't bomb in Pakistan that it was an allied country so I said instead of having people all the way up and down the Afghan‑Pakistan border and we're grabbing them one at a time, let's let them into Pakistan, let them get situated so that they feel like they're safe again, they're going to make a mistake they always do and then we can hit the safe houses. Instead of getting one at a time we get 20 at a time or 50 at a time…"*<Cite s="2022-05-23-danny-jones-cia-spy-betrayed" t="29:30" />

A senior operative expressed frustration with personnel assignments: *"I volunteered to go to Afghanistan, everybody volunteered right and they wouldn't take me… I said to a buddy of mine I don't understand this, I volunteered three times, they won't take me. How long had you been in the CIA at that point? Almost 12 years, okay, and my Arabic was fluent… they said we're not interrogating anybody."*<Cite s="2022-05-23-danny-jones-cia-spy-betrayed" t="30:30" />

## Political reaction and legal debate

When Senator Kerry received Kiriakou’s paper, his response was recorded as: *"You know, on second thought, I don't think it really does any good for us to publish this paper. It's just it's angry and it points the finger and I think we should probably just kill it."*<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="22:41" />

Kiriakou draws a comparison to the flow of Chinese fentanyl into the United States, asserting: *"Why won't the Chinese stop the flow of fentanyl to the United States? I'll give you one guess. Because it weakens our society and it makes us easier to beat if we have to fight them in a war."*<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="23:12" />

These remarks reflect internal tension between whistleblowers exposing the policy and senior officials seeking to contain its political fallout. The reluctance to publicize the findings suggests an awareness of the policy’s controversial nature and its potential to damage U.S. credibility.

## Legacy and ongoing debate

The term “Afghan heroin policy” remains a point of contention in discussions of U.S. foreign policy and intelligence ethics. Kiriakou’s 2009 Senate‑commissioned report, bolstered by on‑the‑ground interviews and agency assessments, provides a detailed account of how a covert narcotics strategy was embedded within the broader war on terror.

Critics argue that the policy violated international drug‑control conventions and contributed to regional instability, while proponents contend that it served a calculated, albeit morally ambiguous, objective of weakening hostile regimes. The policy’s legacy continues to inform contemporary debates over the use of illicit economies as instruments of geopolitical influence.

## The poppy investigation Kerry buried

As senior investigator for the [Senate Foreign Relations Committee](/wiki/senate-foreign-relations-committee), [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) flew to Helmand Province and interviewed a poppy farmer who said, *"The Americans told me in 2001 that if I told them where the Arabs were, I could grow all the poppy I wanted."*<Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="58:53" /><Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="59:26" /> A DEA contact told him he would never be allowed to publish, because Afghanistan produces 93% of the world's heroin, most of it flowing to Russia and Iran — *"we want them to be addicted… it weakens their societies,"* the same thing Kiriakou says China now does with fentanyl. [John Kerry](/wiki/john-kerry) told him to drop it.<Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="1:00:27" /><Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="1:00:59" />

Discussing why mineral wealth has become a new justification for foreign entanglement generally, Kiriakou's co-guest notes Afghanistan has an estimated $23 trillion worth of rare earth metals, discovered around 2015-2016 — raised as context alongside competing claims about Ukraine's own mineral wealth.<Cite s="2024-08-12-danny-jones-loyal-officer-vs-dissident-spy" t="2:47:45" />
