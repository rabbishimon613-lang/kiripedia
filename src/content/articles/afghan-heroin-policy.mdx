**Afghan heroin policy** is the term [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john_kiriakou) uses for the post‑2001 United States arrangement in [Afghanistan](/wiki/afghanistan) under which heroin‑poppy cultivation was, according to Kiriakou’s 2009 investigation for the [Senate Foreign Relations Committee](/wiki/senate_foreign_relations_committee), protected and actively encouraged by U.S. military and [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia) personnel.<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="14:43" /><Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="22:10" />

## Scale of the opium trade

Kiriakou notes that *"The Taliban banned the cultivation of heroin poppy. So in 2000, Afghanistan accounted for 0% of the world's heroin."* By the time he travelled to [Kandahar](/wiki/kandahar) and [Lashkar Gah](/wiki/lashkar_gah) as senior investigator for [Senator John Kerry](/wiki/john_kerry)’s committee, Afghanistan was producing *"93% of the world's heroin."*<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="15:14" /><Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="16:21" />

## Interaction with local poppy farmers

Through a State Department contact who ran the Provincial Reconstruction Team in Lashkar Gah, Kiriakou persuaded a reluctant U.S. military escort to take him into the poppy fields. Speaking through a Pashtu translator, he asked a farmer why he cultivated poppy instead of vegetables that could be harvested twice a year and exported. The farmer replied, *"The Americans told me in 2001 that if I told them where the Arabs were, I could grow all the poppy I wanted."* Kiriakou pressed, *"What American told you you could grow poppy?"* At that moment the U.S. security officer escorting Kiriakou said *"meeting over"* and physically pulled him back to the jeep.<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="19:00" /><Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="19:30" /><Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="20:03" /><Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="20:34" />

## DEA perspective on the heroin pipeline

Before finalising his report, Kiriakou consulted a senior [Drug Enforcement Administration](/wiki/drug_enforcement_administration) officer at a secret DEA facility in rural Virginia. The officer’s candid assessment was: *"Buddy, you know you're never going to get this published, right? Afghanistan produces 93% of the world's heroin. All of that heroin goes to Iran and Russia, and we want them to be addicted to heroin. It weakens their societies. It weakens their cultures. It makes them easier to beat if we have to fight them."*<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="21:36" /><Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="22:10" />

Kiriakou adds that domestic U.S. heroin primarily originates from [Latin America](/wiki/latin_america) via [Mexico](/wiki/mexico), noting *"it's just too hard to get it from Afghanistan all the way to the United States when it can just come right up through Mexico."*<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="22:41" />

## Political reaction within the U.S. government

When [John Kerry](/wiki/john_kerry) received Kiriakou’s paper, his response was recorded as: *"You know, on second thought, I don't think it really does any good for us to publish this paper. It's just it's angry and it points the finger and I think we should probably just kill it."*<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="22:41" />

## Parallel with Chinese fentanyl trafficking

Kiriakou draws a comparison to the flow of Chinese fentanyl into the United States, asserting: *"Why won't the Chinese stop the flow of fentanyl to the United States? I'll give you one guess. Because it weakens our society and it makes us easier to beat if we have to fight them in a war."*<Cite s="2026-05-25-covert-operations-insight-chills" t="23:12" />
