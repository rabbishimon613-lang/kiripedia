**CIA sexpionage** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s term for the agency's use of sex in intelligence, which he says it has practiced since its inception — a documented part of [MK-Ultra](/wiki/mk-ultra) and of honey-pot operations that ran routinely into the early 1980s.<Cite s="2026-05-08-jay-dyer-real-cia-history" t="03:35" /><Cite s="2026-05-08-jay-dyer-real-cia-history" t="04:05" /> His mentor [Gus Avrakotos](/wiki/gust-avrakotos) told him the CIA *"used sex all the time until Ronald Reagan became president"* — and stopped not for morality but because it did not work: a source who commits espionage for years must be held by trust, not coercion.<Cite s="2026-05-08-jay-dyer-real-cia-history" t="04:35" /><Cite s="2026-05-08-jay-dyer-real-cia-history" t="05:06" /> Kiriakou relays a possibly apocryphal story of an Ayatollah, targeted before the Iranian Revolution, who laughed at the compromising photos and asked for one *"in an 8-by-10 and give me two 5-by-7s"* — the episode around which, he says, the agency decided the tactic *"just isn't working."*<Cite s="2026-05-08-jay-dyer-real-cia-history" t="06:08" /><Cite s="2026-05-08-jay-dyer-real-cia-history" t="06:39" />

## The tennis-club line (Austin and Matt)

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) cites a scenario from James Olson's ethics book *Fair Play*: a CIA officer befriends a suspected Russian sleeper at a tennis club, assesses him as lonely and recruitable, and asks her boss whether she may sleep with him to double him back. Everyone in Kiriakou's class said yes — but Olson's answer, and Kiriakou's, is *"absolutely not,"* the line the agency draws in [sexpionage](/wiki/cia-sexpionage).<Cite s="2026-02-12-austin-and-matt-us-destroying-itself" t="52:03" /><Cite s="2026-02-12-austin-and-matt-us-destroying-itself" t="53:34" /><Cite s="2026-02-12-austin-and-matt-us-destroying-itself" t="54:04" />
