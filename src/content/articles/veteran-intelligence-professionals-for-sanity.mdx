**Veteran Intelligence Professionals for Sanity** (VIPS) is a group of former U.S. intelligence officials — retired CIA, FBI, NSA and some DOD personnel — of which [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) is a member.<Cite s="2026-05-07-secret-intelligence-report-attack-iran" t="26:26" /><Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="20:30" /> It confers the Sam Adams Award for Integrity in Intelligence. Kiriakou recounts that when [Joe Kent](/wiki/joe-kent) went public on intelligence matters, he tracked down Kent's contact information and arranged for VIPS to give him the Sam Adams Award — though, he notes, the two had never spoken.<Cite s="2026-05-07-secret-intelligence-report-attack-iran" t="26:57" /><Cite s="2026-05-18-covert-operations-insight" t="20:30" />

Kiriakou identifies fellow VIPS member Bill Binney as the former number-four official at the NSA, and cites Binney's study concluding that the data downloaded from the DNC server was extracted at a speed no internet connection could achieve remotely, implying a local, not Russian, leak.<Cite s="2019-04-18-scott-horton" t="28:22" />

## Sam Adams Award to Joe Kent (2026)

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) said on the PBD Podcast in April 2026 that when [Joe Kent](/wiki/joe-kent) went public with the identity of the American intelligence figure whose leak scooped the [Iran strike](/wiki/iran-12-day-war), Kiriakou immediately reached out for Kent's contact information and, on behalf of [Veteran Intelligence Professionals for Sanity](/wiki/veteran-intelligence-professionals-for-sanity), presented Kent with the group's Sam Adams Award for Integrity in Intelligence — despite the two men never having spoken directly.<Cite s="2026-04-23-pbd-podcast-ex-cia-officer-confronted-over-zionist-accusa" t="27:03" /><Cite s="2026-04-23-pbd-podcast-ex-cia-officer-confronted-over-zionist-accusa" t="27:35" />

## The letter to Ecuador (2018)

After Ecuador cut off [Julian Assange](/wiki/julian-assange)'s communications in 2018, VIPS wrote to the Ecuadorian president through the country's embassy in Washington. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), who delivered the petitions, says the letter carried *"something like three or four dozen signatures of retired intelligence professionals from CIA, NSA, FBI, Homeland Security and the uniformed military and military intelligence services — so we like to think we're a group of people who know what we're talking about."* The delegation was *"received very warmly"* at the embassy. *"I like to think that the letter had some impact. I'm not sure, but at the very least it didn't hurt."*<Cite s="2019-04-07-unity4j-vigil-confirm-not-panel" t="36:04" /><Cite s="2019-04-07-unity4j-vigil-confirm-not-panel" t="36:35" />

Four days before Assange was removed from the London embassy, Kiriakou said the group had not yet discussed repeating the exercise but that *"the time is ripe — we need to do it now, like this weekend, immediately,"* and volunteered to take the lead.<Cite s="2019-04-07-unity4j-vigil-confirm-not-panel" t="35:33" />
