[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) treats the various schemes for resettling the Palestinian population outside the territory as a single recurring idea that resurfaces in each decade under a new label, and rejects all of them on the same ground.

## The 1980s "disputed territory" plan

The version he recalls from his own early career concerned a small wedge of land on the frontier between Saudi Arabia and Iraq. *"They used to talk, in the 1980s… along the border of Saudi Arabia and Iraq there was this diamond that was called the disputed territory. And it was a couple of hundred square miles — very, very small. There was talk about just naming that Palestine and moving them all there."*<Cite s="2026-04-02-kim-iversen-israel-and-al-qaeda-cia-whistleblower-exposes" t="14:34" />

His objection was not political but physical. *"The thing is, nothing can live there. I mean, even insects don't live there. It's just dust. It has no water, it has no oil, it has no trees, no shrubs, no shade, no nothing."* The proposal died for an unrelated and undramatic reason: *"and then the Iraqis and the Saudis came to a border agreement, and that idea went away."*<Cite s="2026-04-02-kim-iversen-israel-and-al-qaeda-cia-whistleblower-exposes" t="15:06" />

## The general objection

Whatever the destination on offer, Kiriakou's verdict on the category is the same, and he states it as a matter of historical precedent rather than of sympathy: *"this idea of just picking up an entire people and moving them — it's unprecedented really in the history of the world, and it's just not tenable."*<Cite s="2026-04-02-kim-iversen-israel-and-al-qaeda-cia-whistleblower-exposes" t="15:06" />

He applies the same reading to the contemporary version. Of Donald Trump's suggestion that [Gaza](/wiki/gaza-famine) could become *"the next Monaco,"* Kiriakou's response is that the aspiration is unobjectionable and the premise is not: *"Gaza can be better than Monaco… but he meant without any Palestinians, and that's a non-starter."* He notes that within twenty-four hours of the ceasefire between Hamas and Israel, Israeli forces launched a major offensive in the West Bank, which he takes as confirmation that the operative policy is *"to either kill or throw out every Palestinian"* — and again the objection is feasibility: *"that is unsustainable. You just can't — it can't work. It won't work."*<Cite s="2026-04-02-kim-iversen-israel-and-al-qaeda-cia-whistleblower-exposes" t="13:00" /><Cite s="2026-04-02-kim-iversen-israel-and-al-qaeda-cia-whistleblower-exposes" t="13:31" />

Having visited Israel twice in the preceding year, he adds a structural observation about what the arrangement already looks like on the ground: *"there's no other country on earth like Israel, where you have the first world and the third world living side by side separated only by high walls and barbed wire — and that's not a sustainable model for a state."*<Cite s="2026-04-02-kim-iversen-israel-and-al-qaeda-cia-whistleblower-exposes" t="14:04" />
