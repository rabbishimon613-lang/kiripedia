The **BDS movement** — Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions — is the campaign of economic pressure on Israel that [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says he and many others support in principle. He notes that some 40 to 42 U.S. states have passed anti-BDS laws, so that a speaker invited to a publicly funded university may have to sign a document pledging support for Israel in order to be paid.<Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="26:39" /><Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="27:13" /> Kiriakou refuses: *"I will not pledge fealty to a foreign government."* He frames the laws — now under constitutional appeal — as part of a climate in which any criticism of Israel or [Netanyahu](/wiki/benjamin-netanyahu) is branded antisemitic.<Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="27:13" /><Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="26:07" />

## Florida and the UK (The Inquiry)

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) cites the spread of anti-Palestinian speech restrictions: in Florida it is, for now, illegal to express support for Palestinians on social media, and he says Miami police knocked on the door of a woman who did so on Facebook. He pairs it with the UK's [secrecy laws](/wiki/uk-secrecy-laws), under which support for Palestinian rights can bring prosecution.<Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="20:25" /><Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="20:57" />
