**Torture as a recruiting tool** is the argument [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) makes when asked whether the practices of the war on terror did more harm than good. His answer is not hedged: *"I am absolutely positive that that has been the case."*<Cite s="2015-02-10-alittlepart-ofme-callmeco-alittlepart-ofme-callmeco" t="04:39" />

## The claim

The formulation he gives it is deliberately unflattering to his own side, and it covers more than interrogation:

> *"We always seem to step in a mud puddle, so to speak, where it comes to things like interrogations or drone strikes or attacks on individuals. These activities always serve to promote terrorist recruitment."*

The occasion for it was a question about conditions at [Guantánamo](/wiki/guantanamo-bay) having become a recruiting tool for jihadists; Kiriakou accepted the premise and widened it, putting the targeted-killing programme in the same category as the [interrogation](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation) one.<Cite s="2015-02-10-alittlepart-ofme-callmeco-alittlepart-ofme-callmeco" t="04:39" />

## The arithmetic

What distinguishes his version of a common argument is that he attaches numbers to it, and dates them. *"When we first began fighting al-Qaeda in Afghanistan we were talking about a core of just several hundred people. Well, now look at ISIS, which is sort of the next generation of al-Qaeda — we're talking about fighting something like 35,000 people."*<Cite s="2015-02-10-alittlepart-ofme-callmeco-alittlepart-ofme-callmeco" t="05:10" />

The conclusion is stated as a share of responsibility rather than as a sole cause: *"and I think that we've had a hand in that recruitment."*<Cite s="2015-02-10-alittlepart-ofme-callmeco-alittlepart-ofme-callmeco" t="05:10" />
