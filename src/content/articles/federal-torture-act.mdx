The **Federal Torture Act**, as [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described it, was in force well before the September 11 attacks and had concrete enforcement history. Following World War II, the United States executed Japanese soldiers who had [waterboarded](/wiki/waterboarding) American POWs — establishing waterboarding as a death-penalty offense under U.S. law.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="23:37" />

In January 1968, when a *Washington Post* photograph showed a U.S. soldier waterboarding a North Vietnamese prisoner, Secretary of Defense Robert McNamara ordered an immediate investigation. The soldier was arrested, convicted of torture, and sentenced to twenty years at Leavenworth.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="24:07" />

Kiriakou juxtaposed this enforcement history with the post-9/11 [CIA](/wiki/cia) program: *"But then in 2002, like magic, it's all legal."*<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="24:07" />
