In December 2007, John Kiriakou blew the whistle on the CIA's torture program, asserting that torture was official U.S. government policy personally approved by the president. <Cite s="2026-05-31-john-kiriakou-podcast-john-kiriakou-new-posdcast-12-secrets-that-have" t="21:43:00" />

## 'In for a penny, in for a pound'

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) describes deciding to speak to ABC's [Brian Ross](/wiki/brian-ross) in 2007 after concluding the White House would scapegoat him for the [torture program](/wiki/cia-torture-program-whistleblowing): *"In for a penny, in for a pound. Whatever he asks me, I'm just going to tell the truth."* He says he had long assumed someone else — a doctor, an officer who fainted at a secret prison — would come forward first, and none did.<Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="2:20:41" /><Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="2:22:16" /><Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="2:22:48" />

## 'I only wish I'd had the guts'

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says the most important lesson of his [whistleblowing](/wiki/cia-torture-program-whistleblowing) was realizing he was not alone: the day after he went public, a retired deputy director of the CIA — a name the public would recognize — emailed him, *"You've chosen a difficult path, but I'm glad somebody did. I only wish I had had the guts to do it myself."*<Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="1:01:27" /><Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="1:02:00" />

## Fewer than two dozen knew (The Inquiry)

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says fewer than two dozen people in the entire U.S. government knew of the [torture program](/wiki/cia-torture-program-whistleblowing) when he was told, yet the congressional oversight committees both authorized and funded it — so later claims that Congress *"had no idea"* were *"just a lie."*<Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="04:45" /><Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="06:18" /> He explains that the Bush administration investigated him for a year and closed the case because it is illegal to classify a criminal act — and the FBI had deemed torture a crime — but the Obama administration, under [John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan), reopened it, recast the torture as not-a-crime, and charged him under the Espionage Act.<Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="12:33" /><Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="16:46" />

## The hooding that started it (IRONCLAD)

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says his turn into *"the human rights guy"* came incrementally, beginning the night a special-operations officer on loan to him brought in prisoners with hoods over their heads. Told hooding is a war crime under the Geneva Convention, the officer refused — *"I'm going to report you to headquarters"* — and Kiriakou shot back, *"I'm already reporting you for committing a war crime."* They reported each other. It crystallized his sense that *"we're just going to pretend these laws don't exist because we're the good guys."*<Cite s="2025-05-28-ironclad-hidden-terror-program" t="10:56" /><Cite s="2025-05-28-ironclad-hidden-terror-program" t="11:27" /><Cite s="2025-05-28-ironclad-hidden-terror-program" t="11:58" />
