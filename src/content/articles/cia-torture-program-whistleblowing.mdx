In December 2007, [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) became the first former [CIA](/wiki/cia) officer to publicly confirm the agency's [enhanced interrogation](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation) program, asserting that torture was official U.S. government policy personally approved by the president.<Cite s="2026-05-31-john-kiriakou-podcast-john-kiriakou-new-posdcast-12-secrets-that-have" t="21:43:00" />

## An incremental turn: the hooding incident

Kiriakou describes his path to whistleblowing as gradual rather than sudden — a slide into becoming *"the human rights guy"* that began years earlier in the field, the night a special-operations officer on loan to him brought in prisoners with hoods over their heads. Told hooding is a war crime under the Geneva Convention, the officer refused to remove them and threatened, *"I'm going to report you to headquarters."* Kiriakou shot back, *"I'm already reporting you for committing a war crime."* They reported each other. The episode crystallized, for Kiriakou, a broader pattern: *"we're just going to pretend these laws don't exist because we're the good guys."*<Cite s="2025-05-28-ironclad-hidden-terror-program" t="10:56" /><Cite s="2025-05-28-ironclad-hidden-terror-program" t="11:27" /><Cite s="2025-05-28-ironclad-hidden-terror-program" t="11:58" />

## Waiting for someone else to speak first

For years afterward, Kiriakou says he assumed someone else — a doctor present at a black site, an officer who had fainted during an interrogation — would be the one to go to the [press](/wiki/brian-ross) first. No one did.<Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="2:20:41" />

## Going to ABC News, December 2007

Kiriakou describes deciding to speak to ABC's [Brian Ross](/wiki/brian-ross) after concluding the White House would scapegoat him for the torture program regardless of what he did: *"In for a penny, in for a pound. Whatever he asks me, I'm just going to tell the truth."*<Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="2:22:16" /><Cite s="2025-10-02-truth-hurts-epstein-mossad-blackmail-playbook" t="2:22:48" />

## 'I only wish I'd had the guts'

Kiriakou says the most important lesson of his whistleblowing was realizing he was not alone in his misgivings: the day after he went public, a retired deputy director of the CIA — a name the public would recognize — emailed him, *"You've chosen a difficult path, but I'm glad somebody did. I only wish I had had the guts to do it myself."*<Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="1:01:27" /><Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="1:02:00" />

## Congressional knowledge and the reversal under Brennan

Kiriakou says fewer than two dozen people in the entire U.S. government knew of the torture program when he was told of it, yet the congressional oversight committees both authorized and funded it — so later claims by members of Congress that they *"had no idea"* were, in his words, *"just a lie."*<Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="04:45" /><Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="06:18" />

He recounts that the Bush administration investigated him for a year and closed the case, because it is illegal to classify a criminal act and the FBI had already deemed torture a crime. The Obama administration, under [John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan), reopened the case, recast the torture program as not having been a crime, and charged Kiriakou under the [Espionage Act](/wiki/espionage-act).<Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="12:33" /><Cite s="2026-03-01-the-inquiry-torture-assassinations-epstein" t="16:46" />
