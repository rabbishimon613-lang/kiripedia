Per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), the [FBI](/wiki/fbi) times arrests strategically to maximize pressure on detainees. He says the FBI "loves" to make arrests on Fridays, or on Thursdays after 5 p.m., because there are no federal arraignments on Fridays — meaning someone arrested Thursday evening spends Thursday, Friday, Saturday, and Sunday nights in jail before finally being arraigned on Monday.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="55:49" /><Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="56:19" /> He describes this stretch in jail — which in Washington, D.C. he calls a very dangerous place — as designed to leave detainees "beaten up a couple of times and softened up" and more willing to accept a plea deal by the time they are arraigned.<Cite s="2025-09-30-deprogram-show-with-ted-rall-and-jamarl-thomas-deprogram-with-ted-ral" t="43:18" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="49:32" />

Kiriakou says he personally experienced this: FBI agents told him to turn himself in at their Washington field office on a Monday morning, after having kept close surveillance on him "from Thursday to Monday."<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="56:19" /> Just a couple of days after that arrest, he met with the [Government Accountability Project](/wiki/government-accountability-project)'s attorney Jesselyn Raddack, who told him that under the legal definition of whistleblowing, he qualified regardless of his own motivation.<Cite s="2025-09-30-deprogram-show-with-ted-rall-and-jamarl-thomas-deprogram-with-ted-ral" t="07:47" /> He says the tactic was "painfully evident" again years later, when the FBI applied the same Thursday-arrest, no-Friday-arraignment strategy to people arrested in connection with January 6.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="49:00" />
