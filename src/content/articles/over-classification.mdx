**Over-classification** is, in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s telling, a behavioural problem before it is a legal one — the reason the United States produces *"billions and billions of classified documents being created every single year, with no mechanism to police them or to eventually declassify them."*<Cite s="2023-01-28-kevin-gosztola-every-official-everywhere-with-classified-doc" t="28:21" />

## The incentive

Asked whether officials stamp documents secret to make their work seem important to superiors, Kiriakou agreed without hesitation, and located the effect outside the intelligence core: *"not so much at the CIA, because everything's classified anyway — but at some of these lesser agencies, absolutely. Absolutely."*<Cite s="2023-01-28-kevin-gosztola-every-official-everywhere-with-classified-doc" t="28:52" />

What the stamp buys is standing. *"It makes you feel like you know, you're one of the in-crowd, you're one of the special people that has access. Maybe the guy you sit next to, he doesn't have access — so you're more important than he is. You see that every day."*<Cite s="2023-01-28-kevin-gosztola-every-official-everywhere-with-classified-doc" t="29:24" />

Inside the agency the mechanism is habit rather than status: [everything produced carries a classification](/wiki/classified-email), including a lunch invitation, because that is how officers are trained.<Cite s="2023-01-28-kevin-gosztola-every-official-everywhere-with-classified-doc" t="27:19" /><Cite s="2023-01-28-kevin-gosztola-every-official-everywhere-with-classified-doc" t="27:51" />

## The question the press does not ask

Kiriakou's complaint about coverage of the [documents scandals](/wiki/2023-classified-documents-scandal) is that it treats the story as a contest. *"There are very, very few journalists — certainly none in the mainstream — that I've encountered who really have a full appreciation for what this issue is."*<Cite s="2023-01-28-kevin-gosztola-every-official-everywhere-with-classified-doc" t="30:55" />

The reframing he offers is one sentence: *"the problem isn't who found how many documents in whose garage. The problem is why are these documents classified in the first place."*<Cite s="2023-01-28-kevin-gosztola-every-official-everywhere-with-classified-doc" t="31:28" />

## A felony nobody has ever been charged with

Kiriakou files over-classification in a category of statutes that exist to be ignored. Asked in 2019 whether there would be consequences for the DNI's inspector general sending [the Ukraine complaint](/wiki/ukraine-call-whistleblower) to the Justice Department instead of the congressional oversight committees, he said no, and reached for over-classification as the parallel: *"just like over-classification is actually a felony, and it's never ever been prosecuted, nor will it ever be."*<Cite s="2019-10-03-rob-kall-bottom-up-show-discusses-ukraine-phone-call" t="16:12" /><Cite s="2019-10-03-rob-kall-bottom-up-show-discusses-ukraine-phone-call" t="16:44" />

The point is not that the law is weak but that it is unenforced by design, and so *"just going to be ignored and lost in the greater context"* of whatever case raises it.<Cite s="2019-10-03-rob-kall-bottom-up-show-discusses-ukraine-phone-call" t="16:44" />

## A crime cannot be classified

The converse of over-classification is a rule Kiriakou states as settled law and uses as the basis of his defence of [Julian Assange](/wiki/julian-assange): *"it is illegal in this country to classify a crime. If something is a crime, by definition it cannot be classified."*<Cite s="2020-02-25-cafe-weltschmerz-dutch-dutch-language-framing-confirm-john-speaks-no" t="11:06" />

From that he draws the conclusion that publishing such material is not an offence but the correction of one — the American people *"have the right to know what their government is doing in their name,"* and releasing evidence of war crimes is therefore *"a public service. It's not a crime."*<Cite s="2020-02-25-cafe-weltschmerz-dutch-dutch-language-framing-confirm-john-speaks-no" t="11:06" /><Cite s="2020-02-25-cafe-weltschmerz-dutch-dutch-language-framing-confirm-john-speaks-no" t="11:39" />

The boundary matters to his own position, because he does not extend it to secrets as such. In the same conversation he defended [the Crypto AG operation](/wiki/crypto-ag) as legitimately secret and squarely in the public interest.<Cite s="2020-02-25-cafe-weltschmerz-dutch-dutch-language-framing-confirm-john-speaks-no" t="20:31" />
