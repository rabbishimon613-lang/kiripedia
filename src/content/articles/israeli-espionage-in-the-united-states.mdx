**Israeli espionage in the United States** is a subject [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) approaches from institutional memory rather than from documents: he was taught it as a new officer, and he argues the picture has since been politically reversed without the underlying facts changing.

## What he was told in 1990

Kiriakou's first week at the [CIA](/wiki/cia), in January 1990, was spent in an auditorium receiving briefings. The director of security gave practical pointers — never eat at a particular steakhouse down the street, because the KGB assumed agency staff ate there and had taken every table — and then gave the counterintelligence picture. There were two official Israeli intelligence representatives in Washington, based at the embassy: one from [Mossad](/wiki/mossad) and one from Shin Bet, both conducting liaison. Against those two, the [FBI](/wiki/fbi) had identified 187 Israeli spies spread across America, mostly infiltrating American defence contractors to steal secrets — and, as the security director put it, it had not ended with the arrest of [Jonathan Pollard](/wiki/jonathan-pollard).<Cite s="2026-07-10-the-young-turks-former-cia-agent-explains-why-we-need-to-brea" t="20:19" /><Cite s="2026-07-10-the-young-turks-former-cia-agent-explains-why-we-need-to-brea" t="20:50" /><Cite s="2026-07-10-the-young-turks-former-cia-agent-explains-why-we-need-to-brea" t="21:21" /> Israel was ranked a critical threat for counterintelligence alongside Russia, China, Cuba and North Korea, and possibly Iran.<Cite s="2026-07-10-the-young-turks-former-cia-agent-explains-why-we-need-to-brea" t="21:52" />

## Barred from the building

When Kiriakou joined, he says, "we did not like the Israelis at all. They were spying on us. They made it clear. They openly spied on us." They brought gifts with listening devices embedded in them — to the point that they were not allowed into the headquarters building at all, and the agency had to rent space elsewhere to hold liaison meetings with them.<Cite s="2026-07-10-the-young-turks-former-cia-agent-explains-why-we-need-to-brea" t="04:13" /><Cite s="2026-07-10-the-young-turks-former-cia-agent-explains-why-we-need-to-brea" t="04:44" />

## 2026

Kiriakou sets that history against two contemporaneous developments. A provision of the National Defense Authorization Act for 2027 would co-locate Israeli personnel with the Pentagon and permit [Five Eyes](/wiki/five-eyes)-style liaison with the CIA.<Cite s="2026-07-10-the-young-turks-former-cia-agent-explains-why-we-need-to-brea" t="04:44" /> At the same time, the director of the Defense Intelligence Agency sent an all-hands email warning that the Israelis were being unusually aggressive in trying to recruit assets inside the agency and that all employees needed to be extra careful.<Cite s="2026-07-10-the-young-turks-former-cia-agent-explains-why-we-need-to-brea" t="05:14" />

His objection is to holding both positions at once: "Either the Israelis are ranked as critical threat for counter intelligence … or we're joined at the hip. But it can't be both."<Cite s="2026-07-10-the-young-turks-former-cia-agent-explains-why-we-need-to-brea" t="05:45" /> Asked whether an ally stealing sensitive technology repeatedly is normal, his answer was that if it were any other country in the world "we would be on the brink of war with them right now."<Cite s="2026-07-10-the-young-turks-former-cia-agent-explains-why-we-need-to-brea" t="08:52" />

Asked what changed, he attributes it to politics rather than blackmail: a succession of presidents he characterises as Zionist in policy terms, and an incremental campaign on Capitol Hill through [AIPAC](/wiki/aipac) that has reached the point where disagreeing with [Benjamin Netanyahu](/wiki/benjamin-netanyahu) as a person gets a person labelled antisemitic.<Cite s="2026-07-10-the-young-turks-former-cia-agent-explains-why-we-need-to-brea" t="06:50" /><Cite s="2026-07-10-the-young-turks-former-cia-agent-explains-why-we-need-to-brea" t="07:21" />
