[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described an incident from his posting as economic officer at the American Embassy in Bahrain, 1994 to 1996, in which he served as control officer for a visit by David Rockefeller and subsequently learned the real purpose of the trip.

## The official visit

Ambassador [David Ransom](/wiki/david-ransom) assigned [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) to manage Rockefeller's schedule and logistics for the duration of the visit. The stated occasion was an award from the US-Bahrain Banking Society — a large state dinner to which the entire cabinet and diplomatic corps were invited. Three days after the dinner, Rockefeller departed.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="36:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="37:00" />

## The country-team debrief

At the country-team meeting the following day — attended by the ambassador, the deputy chief of mission, and senior staff — [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) said he did not understand why a man of Rockefeller's age and stature would fly halfway around the world to collect a meaningless trophy from an obscure banking society.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="37:30" />

The ambassador replied: *"John, you're supposed to be the smart one."* He explained: Rockefeller had not come for the award. The award was his cover stop. He was the secret liaison to [Saddam Hussein](/wiki/saddam-hussein). After appearing publicly in Bahrain — with his presence fully documented in the local papers — he had taken a military flight to Baghdad to deliver a message to Saddam Hussein: stop violating sanctions, or the United States would take military action again.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="37:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="38:00" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="38:30" />

[Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) used this account to illustrate the mechanics of back-channel diplomacy and how public figures are used as deniable intermediaries in sensitive communications with adversarial governments.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="38:30" />
