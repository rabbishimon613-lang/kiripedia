Asked who would govern Venezuela the day after [Nicolás Maduro](/wiki/maduro-capture) was removed, [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) answered first by ruling people out — *"it''s not going to be [Juan Guaidó](/wiki/juan-guaido), whom nobody had ever heard of; it''s not going to be this woman that just won the [Nobel Peace Prize](/wiki/nobel-peace-prize) of all things"* — and then by setting out why the question was academic.<Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="35:39" /><Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="36:09" />

## The checklist

*"This is a lesson that we just seem to never learn. It''s easy to overthrow a government. I mean, they actually teach you at West Point the modus operandi by which you can overthrow a government. You have to take control of the presidential palace and either arrest, kill or exile the leader. You have to take control of the radio and television stations. You have to take control of the major intersections in the capital. And you have to have the support of the military."*<Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="36:09" /><Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="36:41" />

## Where it fails

*"Well, we don''t have the support of the Venezuelan military. Maduro has the support of the Venezuelan military. So what does that mean? Does that mean we have to send in ground troops and fight a hot war on the ground in Venezuela, when the Venezuelans are no threat to us? I just don''t see any upside."*<Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="36:41" /><Cite s="2025-12-09-dialogue-works-charlie-kirk-s-story-cracks-hegseth" t="37:12" />
