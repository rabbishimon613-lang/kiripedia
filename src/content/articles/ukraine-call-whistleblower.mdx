**The Ukraine call whistleblower** is the intelligence officer whose August 2019 complaint about a call between [Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump) and the president of Ukraine led to Trump's first impeachment. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) has consistently declined to treat him as a whistleblower, and is equally consistent that this does not license naming him.

## The procedural objection

Kiriakou puts the disclaimer first: *"I'm on record as saying that I don't believe that this is a real whistleblower — and it's not because the whistleblower has taken one partisan position or taken another partisan position."*<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="23:03" />

The objection is the route. There is *"a very specific MO that one has to go through to report waste, fraud, abuse or illegality in the intelligence community: you go to your supervisor, from there to the Inspector General, the Inspector General then goes to the oversight committees. That's not what happened. Not even remotely close to what happened in this case."*<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="23:03" /><Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="23:34" />

What did happen, as he traces it: the whistleblower went to his supervisor, the supervisor to the Inspector General, the Inspector General to the General Counsel's office, the General Counsel to CIA leadership, back to the Inspector General, on to the Director of National Intelligence's Inspector General — *"and then not to the oversight committees, but to the Justice Department."*<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="23:34" />

## The stylistic tell

The second objection is textual. The published complaint is *"not a straight account of what the whistleblower claims to have seen or wants to reveal. It's a very legalistic document, clearly written by a team of attorneys."*<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="24:04" />

Kiriakou grounds that in training. CIA analysts go through six months of writing instruction at [the Farm](/wiki/the-farm), *"because they want every CIA analyst to write in exactly the same style — so that when the president reads the [PDB](/wiki/presidents-daily-brief) every morning, no matter who wrote it, it looks exactly the same."* The complaint *"is not in that CIA DI writing style. It's in a style of attorneys."*<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="24:37" />

His conclusion: whatever the officer originally wanted to report *"was overtaken by the CIA's general counsel and the CIA's leadership,"* and what emerged was, in the description he gave on air at the time, *"an anonymous source for the Democratic staff of the House Intelligence Committee."*<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="25:07" />

## On motivation

Kiriakou notes that the professional consensus cuts against him here: whistleblower attorneys hold that motivation is irrelevant so long as waste, fraud, abuse, illegality or a threat to public health or safety is reported. *"But I struggle with that, because in this case it seems to me that motivation would be the heart of the argument. I would want to know what the whistleblower's motivation was, because it would help me to conclude whether or not it was a real whistleblower."*<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="27:45" />

## Against naming him

The defence is unqualified. *"I can't see any reason, any legitimate reason, why you would have to reveal the identity of the whistleblower. That just doesn't make any sense to me."*<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="25:39" />

He describes the consequence in career terms he knows well: internally the man *"is probably never going to work in a serious job again, even if he's able to remain inside the CIA — the battle lines are going to be drawn, his career is finished,"* and once named he will have trouble even getting follow-on employment. *"And that's just not fair. We can disagree about the methodology, we can disagree about the information or the import of the information — but ruining the guy on purpose is exactly what we should be standing up and opposing."*<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="25:39" /><Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="26:10" />

## And on Republican access

Asked whether Republicans had a fair complaint about being unable to interview the officer, Kiriakou said yes, and argued it from his own case. After blowing the whistle on the torture programme he was subpoenaed by the Senate Armed Services Committee and spent *"a solid eight or nine hours"* with staff — half Democratic, half Republican — *"in the vault, the SCIF it's called, so we could speak freely … in a classified setting. And the questions from both sides were all relevant. They were all down in the weeds. They were all investigative in nature."*<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="28:48" />

However partisan Capitol Hill had become, *"I can't help but to think that the staffs at least would have been professional, and the Republicans probably should have had the opportunity to interview that whistleblower."*<Cite s="2020-01-03-kevin-gosztola-kevin-gosztola" t="29:19" />
