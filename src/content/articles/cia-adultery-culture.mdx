**CIA adultery culture** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s recurring characterization of the agency's officer corps as saturated with adultery and casual sex — which he argues is not gossip but a systemic security problem, since a lonely, alienated officer is the easiest kind to recruit.<Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="2:24:14" /> Among his examples: a CIA-specific strain of gonorrhea that spread among officers in Iraq in 2003–2004, requiring doctors from the Office of Medical Services to be flown out<Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="2:18:35" /><Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="2:19:06" />; a deputy director who married five times, each wife having first been his secretary<Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="2:19:37" />; and two senior officers, Mike and Gene, who hated each other because each had slept with the other's wife on successive tours in Karachi and Bogotá.<Cite s="2026-06-15-jason-jones-3-hours-kiriakou" t="2:23:12" /> He cites reports of [Brett McGurk](/wiki/brett-mcgurk) as a further example.
