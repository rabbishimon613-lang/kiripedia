The **Pike Committee** was convened in 1975 as the House counterpart to the Senate's [Church Committee](/wiki/church-committee). Together, the two bodies constituted what [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) has described as the last genuine exercise of congressional oversight over the intelligence community: *"What we need to reform it is a period like we saw in the mid-1970s with the creation of the Church and Pike Committees,"* Kiriakou said, arguing that similar reform today would require an equivalent national consensus that American government is in crisis.<Cite s="2026-05-19-ffn-jeff-dornik-swamps-holy-mess" t="14:35" />

The committee's work led to the creation of the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence, which Kiriakou says functioned as a genuine oversight body for approximately seven years before degrading into what he described as a rubber stamp.<Cite s="2026-05-19-ffn-jeff-dornik-swamps-holy-mess" t="22:22" />
