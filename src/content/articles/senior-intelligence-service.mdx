The **Senior Intelligence Service** is the CIA's executive rank, sitting above GS-15, the highest grade on the federal General Schedule. It is the level at which, in Kiriakou's phrasing, *"you're the directors"*.<Cite s="2026-05-09-the-never-broken-podcast-the-never-broken-podcast" t="4:10" />

## What it is worth

The pay step from GS-15 to the first Senior Intelligence Service grade is on the order of $25,000. The larger difference is that Senior Intelligence Service officers also draw annual corporate-style bonuses that the rest of the workforce does not get.<Cite s="2026-05-09-the-never-broken-podcast-the-never-broken-podcast" t="4:10" />

## What it requires

Eligibility for promotion from GS-15 to SIS-1 requires two tours outside an officer's own line of work: at least one in another of the CIA's four [directorates](/wiki/cia-directorates) — operations, intelligence, science and technology, and administration — and one in the policy community, meaning the State Department or the Department of Defense.<Cite s="2026-05-09-the-never-broken-podcast-the-never-broken-podcast" t="4:43" />

## The consequence

Kiriakou's objection is not to the rule but to how it is satisfied. An officer who lacks one of the two tours needs a posting that will check the box, and the agency has been willing to supply one regardless of whether the officer is trained for it. [Jennifer Matthews](/wiki/jennifer-matthews), an analyst who had already done her policy tour at the State Department, needed the operational half and was given a base command in Afghanistan without operational or security training. She and six other officers were killed there.<Cite s="2026-05-09-the-never-broken-podcast-the-never-broken-podcast" t="5:14" /><Cite s="2026-05-09-the-never-broken-podcast-the-never-broken-podcast" t="5:44" /><Cite s="2026-05-09-the-never-broken-podcast-the-never-broken-podcast" t="9:21" />
