**Waterboarding** is a technique [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described as invented by the Chinese approximately in the 15th century. The procedure involves strapping a prisoner to a board with his feet elevated above his head, placing cloth or burlap over his mouth, and pouring water onto his face. The sensation is of drowning; in many cases, Kiriakou said, the prisoner actually does take in water: *"In the case of [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah), we drowned him. His heart stopped beating and he had to be revived so that he could be tortured more."*<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="24:39" />

## Legal history before 2002

Kiriakou cited three data points establishing waterboarding's prior criminal status. First, the [Federal Torture Act](/wiki/federal-torture-act) of 1946 explicitly prohibited it. Second, following the Second World War, the United States executed Japanese soldiers who had waterboarded American POWs — waterboarding was at that time a capital offense.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="23:37" />

Third, in January 1968, the *Washington Post* published a front-page photograph of a U.S. soldier waterboarding a North Vietnamese prisoner. The Secretary of Defense, Robert McNamara, ordered an investigation the day the photograph appeared. The soldier was arrested, convicted of torture, and sentenced to twenty years at Leavenworth.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="24:07" />

Then in 2002, Kiriakou said, *"like magic, it's all legal."*<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="24:07" />

## Learned-helplessness doctrine

The [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s stated goal for waterboarding was not intelligence extraction per se but the induction of [learned helplessness](/wiki/learned-helplessness) — a psychological state of total subjugation in which the prisoner becomes so terrified of the interrogator that he will confess to anything on demand: *"The prisoner is so terrified of you, so terrified of what you can do to him, that he'll whimper as soon as you walk into the room and just confess everything that you want him to confess to."*<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="25:10" />

Kiriakou described the result: because torture produces confessions the prisoner believes the interrogator wants to hear, not accurate information, the intelligence generated is unreliable. He cited American POWs held in North Vietnam who, when asked to name crew members, recited the Pittsburgh Steelers offensive line or invented childhood friends' names to make the questioning stop.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="25:10" />

## Abu Zubaydah and KSM

[Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) was waterboarded 83 times. [Khalid Sheikh Mohammed](/wiki/khalid-sheikh-mohammed) was waterboarded 187 times. Under the accumulated pressure, KSM confessed to the murder of journalist Daniel Pearl — a confession Kiriakou said was demonstrably false, as KSM was not even in Pakistan when Pearl was killed. When shown video evidence disproving his claim, KSM argued that the arm visible in the video was hairy enough to be his.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="27:13" />

## *"The law never changed. We changed."*

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) gave a specific date for the *Washington Post* photograph of an American soldier [waterboarding](/wiki/waterboarding) a North Vietnamese prisoner: *"January the 11th, 1968."* Secretary of Defense Robert McNamara ordered an immediate investigation. The soldier was arrested, charged with torture, and sentenced to twenty years of hard labor at Leavenworth.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:37:00" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:37:30" />

Kiriakou's framing of the 2002 reauthorization: *"The law never changed. We changed."* He cited the [Federal Torture Act](/wiki/federal-torture-act) of 1946, the post–WWII executions of Japanese soldiers for waterboarding, and the 1968 Leavenworth sentence as the unbroken legal record — then contrasted it with the 2002 claim that waterboarding was suddenly legal. The [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s justification, as Kiriakou characterized it: *"We can do it because we're the good guys."*<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:37:30" />

## McCain-Feinstein amendment — December 2014

The McCain-Feinstein anti-torture amendment passed into law in December 2014. Senator John McCain credited [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) on the Senate floor, stating that without his public disclosures, the American people would never have known the [CIA](/wiki/cia) was torturing prisoners. The amendment formally ended the program in law.<Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:39:00" /><Cite s="2026-01-19-diary-of-a-ceo-they-can-see-all-your-messages" t="1:39:30" />
