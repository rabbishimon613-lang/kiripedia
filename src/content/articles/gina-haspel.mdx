**Gina Haspel** was the Director of the Central Intelligence Agency under President Trump's first term. Per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) she and Mike Pompeo were the two *"very, very bad people"* whom Trump appointed to the position in his first term.<Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="25:51" />

## "Bloody Gina"

Kiriakou: *"The other one was bloody Gina Haspel. We called her bloody Gina for a good reason. She flew out to the secret site to sit in on one of the torture sessions just because she could. Just to sit there and enjoy it and take it all in. What kind of sick person does something like that?"*<Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="25:51" /><Cite s="2026-04-27-carlos-watson-part-everyone-missed" t="26:23" />

## *"Bloody Gina"*

When [Gina Haspel](/wiki/gina-haspel)'s appointment as [CIA](/wiki/cia) Director was announced, [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) wrote an op-ed in the *Washington Post* opposing her nomination. He stated that in the hallways of the CIA, officers referred to her as *"Bloody Gina Haspel"* — because she traveled to the secret prison site specifically to observe torture sessions, in Kiriakou's description, *"just for the kick of watching the torture."*<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="38:30" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="39:00" />

Kiriakou characterized her as *"1,000% pro-torture."* He also described her, in the context of the broader debate about the deep state, as *"the definition of the deep state"* — a reference to her more than thirty years at the [CIA](/wiki/cia).<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="39:00" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="39:30" />

## Al-Nashiri — USS Cole — Haspel's personal supervision

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) stated that [Gina Haspel](/wiki/gina-haspel) was his second-line boss and was intimately involved in the [enhanced interrogation program](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation) at every level: planning, execution, management, and administration. At the end of September 2002, she flew to the secret site and personally took over leadership. He identified the specific prisoner whose torture she supervised personally as Rahim al-Nashiri, alleged mastermind of the USS Cole bombing.<Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="12:30" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="13:00" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="13:30" />

## *"Who takes pleasure in watching torture?"*

[Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) stated he made this characterization publicly on *Democracy Now!* and did not do so lightly. His reasoning: managing a secret site where torture is occurring is one thing; attending torture sessions as an observer — when not operationally required to be present — is another. His conclusion: *"Who takes pleasure in watching the torture of another human being? I don't care if the guy is an alleged terrorist."*<Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="13:30" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="14:00" />

## Confirmation — closest in CIA director history

[Haspel](/wiki/gina-haspel)'s Senate confirmation vote was the closest of any [CIA](/wiki/cia) director in history. She secured confirmation by attracting five Democratic votes, doing so by promising she would never institute such a program again. [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s observation: *"When you have to promise not to break the law again, maybe you're the wrong person for the job. Maybe we need a person in the job who wouldn't break the law in the first place."*<Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="14:30" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="15:00" />

## Army Field Manual — a stroke of the president's pen

[Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described the McCain-Feinstein Amendment as requiring all U.S. government interrogators to abide by the Army Field Manual. The limitation of this protection: the Army Field Manual is an executive-branch document. A president can amend it unilaterally through an executive order. Congress does not need to weigh in; Congress does not even need to be notified. *"If this president or some subsequent president wanted to reinstate a torture program, he could do so in five minutes."*<Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="16:30" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="17:00" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="17:30" />
