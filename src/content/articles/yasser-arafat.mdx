**Yasser Arafat** led the Palestine Liberation Organization and, in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account of the 1990 Gulf crisis, was the Palestinians' leader at the moment their position in the Gulf was lost.<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="27:14" />

After Iraq invaded Kuwait on 2 August 1990, American Secretary of State James Baker went to Arafat and told him: "You have to do the right thing. You have to come down on the side of the Kuwaitis and help us push the Iraqis out of Kuwait."<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="27:14" /> Kiriakou describes what followed as "this very public self-flagellation on the part of Yasser Arafat" — and in the end Arafat came down on the side of [Saddam Hussein](/wiki/saddam-hussein).<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="27:14" />

The consequence, in Kiriakou's telling, fell on the Palestinian professional class rather than on Arafat: the Gulf royal families resolved to expel every Palestinian, and largely did.<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="28:50" /> He treats the resulting Gulf antipathy toward Palestinians as a live factor in present-day diplomacy, including the willingness of Gulf states to normalise relations with Israel under the [Abraham Accords](/wiki/abraham-accords).<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="29:23" />
