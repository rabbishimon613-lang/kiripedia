**Whistleblower psychology** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s recurring attempt to explain why a very small number of people inside an institution act on what everyone in it can see.

## The trait

He credits [Daniel Ellsberg](/wiki/daniel-ellsberg) with teaching him the framing — *"one of the things that dan uh taught me uh early on was that uh there's a greater good uh there's a greater good with whistleblowing"* — and then points to research: *"one of the things that i learned from a recent study on whistleblowers is that there that whistleblowers have a much more highly defined sense of right and wrong than the general population this is something borne out in psychological uh studies."*<Cite s="2021-11-27-live-on-the-fly-randy-credico-ellsberg-kiriakou-rowley-hail-to-the-whistleb" t="12:34" /><Cite s="2021-11-27-live-on-the-fly-randy-credico-ellsberg-kiriakou-rowley-hail-to-the-whistleb" t="13:06" />

The practical consequence is a calculation. When a whistleblower is weighing disclosure of *"waste fraud abuse or illegality or threats to the public health or public safety,"* the greater good enters the reckoning — as does the expectation of vindication on a long horizon: *"eventually history is going to catch up with the issue and with everybody else uh you may be vilified the way dan was vilified the way [julian assange](/wiki/julian-assange) is being vilified right now but eventually the rest of the world will catch up we've seen this happen throughout history."*<Cite s="2021-11-27-live-on-the-fly-randy-credico-ellsberg-kiriakou-rowley-hail-to-the-whistleb" t="13:06" /><Cite s="2021-11-27-live-on-the-fly-randy-credico-ellsberg-kiriakou-rowley-hail-to-the-whistleb" t="13:39" />

## The cost

Against that Kiriakou sets an unqualified claim about the aftermath. Asked whether there is a feeling of loneliness, isolation and hopelessness, he answered: *"i would defy you to to name one whistleblower who hasn't considered suicide uh in in his or her darkest days after going public and realizing just how little public support there is out there."*<Cite s="2021-11-27-live-on-the-fly-randy-credico-ellsberg-kiriakou-rowley-hail-to-the-whistleb" t="34:37" />

His own experience of that absence was specific. A retired deputy director of the [CIA](/wiki/cia) wrote to him privately — *"you've chosen a difficult road but i'm glad somebody did i just wish i had had the courage to do it myself"* — a message Kiriakou says he saved, and which *"translated into nothing for me no support at all no public support."* He was ostracized by his former colleagues *"even if they were whispering very quietly among themselves that i had done the right thing."*<Cite s="2021-11-27-live-on-the-fly-randy-credico-ellsberg-kiriakou-rowley-hail-to-the-whistleb" t="35:11" /><Cite s="2021-11-27-live-on-the-fly-randy-credico-ellsberg-kiriakou-rowley-hail-to-the-whistleb" t="35:42" />

He attributes part of the isolation to structural conditions in the press rather than to public indifference alone: when the media is owned by large corporations and the big newspapers no longer fund investigative reporting, they rely on press releases and on friendly relations with the agencies' public affairs offices — *"a whistleblower doesn't have a chance you don't have a chance because you don't have the same kind of access to this corporate media that the rest of them do you're on your own."*<Cite s="2021-11-27-live-on-the-fly-randy-credico-ellsberg-kiriakou-rowley-hail-to-the-whistleb" t="35:42" /><Cite s="2021-11-27-live-on-the-fly-randy-credico-ellsberg-kiriakou-rowley-hail-to-the-whistleb" t="36:17" />
