Among the consequences [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) attributes to his December 2007 disclosure of the [CIA](/wiki/cia) torture program, the earliest was fiscal rather than criminal. The Justice Department had declined the agency''s first criminal referral on the ground that the information was already public — but, he says, *"immediately, within weeks, I was audited by the IRS. I''ve been audited by the IRS every single year since giving that interview in 2007."*<Cite s="2015-01-09-terrorpedia-video-center-aggregator-channel-likely-re-upload" t="31:35" />

He made the statement in January 2013, days after being sentenced to thirty months, which places the run of audits at five consecutive years by that point and puts them five years ahead of the [Espionage Act](/wiki/espionage-act) charges eventually brought against him. He does not claim direct knowledge of who initiated them; he lists them factually, in sequence with the [crimes reports](/wiki/cia-crimes-reports) the agency filed after each of his public appearances and the [FBI](/wiki/fbi) surveillance he and his wife lived under.<Cite s="2015-01-09-terrorpedia-video-center-aggregator-channel-likely-re-upload" t="31:35" /><Cite s="2015-01-09-terrorpedia-video-center-aggregator-channel-likely-re-upload" t="32:37" />
