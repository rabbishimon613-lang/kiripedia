**Leonie Brinkema** presides in the Eastern District of Virginia, which [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described as *"the Espionage Court"* — the home jurisdiction of the Pentagon, the [CIA](/wiki/cia), and all major defense contractors. No national-security defendant has ever won a case in that district.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="1:33:12" /> Kiriakou identifies her as a Ronald Reagan appointee and calls her *"a hanging judge"* — *"everybody said it, everybody knew it."*<Cite s="2026-04-27-dead-drop-s1e25-the-whistleblower" t="14:11" />

Kiriakou described Brinkema's practice of self-assigning national-security cases. Cases are supposed to be randomly distributed among judges; Brinkema, he said, reserved major national-security matters for herself. Her docket included Kiriakou, [Jeffrey Sterling](/wiki/jeffrey-sterling), Zacarias Moussaoui (the alleged twentieth 9/11 hijacker), and the Julian Assange extradition proceedings.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="1:36:20" />

In Kiriakou's case, Brinkema ruled on 100 defense motions to use classified discovery documents at once, denying all of them from the bench before arguments were heard: *"I'm going to make everybody's day much easier and I'm going to just deny all 100 of these motions."* When Kiriakou's attorney said the classified documents constituted the entire defense, Brinkema replied: *"Classified is classified."*<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="1:33:43" />

The judge further ruled that the word *"whistleblower"* was itself classified and could not be used in court. Kiriakou and his attorneys were required to use substitute language.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="1:34:15" />

Brinkema's legal definition of espionage — that it required only the provision of national-defense information to a person not entitled to receive it, with no requirement of harm or foreign recipient — differed from a ruling in the [Tom Drake](/wiki/tom-drake) case in the District of Maryland, which required a showing of harm to national security. Brinkema refused to apply that precedent.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="1:35:48" />

At [Jeffrey Sterling](/wiki/jeffrey-sterling)'s sentencing, Brinkema stated she was giving Sterling *"Kiriakou plus 12 months."*<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="1:37:22" />

## Sentencing day

Kiriakou describes sitting in Brinkema's courtroom on his own sentencing day, going last because his case was high-profile. She sentenced twenty to twenty-five other people first that day — mostly drug cases — issuing what he calls draconian terms of 20, 30, and 50 years while, after each one, declaring the sentence *"fair and appropriate,"* breaking up families as children and spouses in the gallery cried.<Cite s="2026-04-27-dead-drop-s1e25-the-whistleblower" t="14:41" /> Kiriakou says she had *"a bad habit of reserving all national security cases for herself"* — Zacarias Moussaoui, [Edward Snowden](/wiki/edward-snowden), [Julian Assange](/wiki/julian-assange), Daniel Hale, and [Jeffrey Sterling](/wiki/jeffrey-sterling) among them — sending every one of them to prison, including him.<Cite s="2026-04-27-dead-drop-s1e25-the-whistleblower" t="15:14" /><Cite s="2026-04-27-dead-drop-s1e25-the-whistleblower" t="15:46" />

## 'You either did it or you didn't' (News Beat)

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) describes Judge [Leonie Brinkema](/wiki/leonie-brinkema), a Reagan appointee who reserved most national-security cases for herself — his, [Jeffrey Sterling](/wiki/jeffrey-sterling)'s, Zacarias Moussaoui's, and the still-open [Edward Snowden](/wiki/edward-snowden) and [Julian Assange](/wiki/julian-assange) matters.<Cite s="2024-07-29-news-beat-assange-israel-security-state" t="05:39" /> When his lawyer noted he had no criminal intent, she said she would *"not respect that decision"*; asked whether a person could *"accidentally commit espionage,"* she answered, *"that's exactly what I'm saying,"* then denied all 110 of his declassification motions, leaving him unable to defend himself.<Cite s="2024-07-29-news-beat-assange-israel-security-state" t="06:42" /><Cite s="2024-07-29-news-beat-assange-israel-security-state" t="07:13" />
