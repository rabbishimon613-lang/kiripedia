A **dead drop** is the tradecraft technique of passing material between an officer and an agent through a concealed location, with no meeting — and the namesake of [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s [podcast](/wiki/dead-drop-podcast).

## How it actually works

Kiriakou's point about dead drops is that the drop is the easy part. In a country with a sophisticated intelligence service — the United States, Russia, China, Cuba, Israel, even France — merely identifying a viable dead drop site is very, very difficult. Then comes the choreography: a two-to-three-hour [surveillance detection route](/wiki/surveillance-detection-route) before the drop — eight hours in Russia or China — the drop itself, and another route after.<Cite s="2025-03-14-jack-hopkins-inside-the-mind-of-a-cia-whistleblower" t="48:24" /><Cite s="2025-03-14-jack-hopkins-inside-the-mind-of-a-cia-whistleblower" t="48:54" />

Concealment is its own art: one colleague, Kiriakou recounts, put a drop inside a condom — *"because who's going to touch a condom?"*<Cite s="2025-03-14-jack-hopkins-inside-the-mind-of-a-cia-whistleblower" t="48:24" /> Objects, not just information, move this way — money, passports, whatever an agent needs.<Cite s="2025-12-11-deep-focus-jack-barsky-kgb" t="22:00" />
