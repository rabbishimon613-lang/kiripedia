Kiriakou has said he is proud to sit on the Julian Assange Defence Advisory Committee. Asked about the case in May 2021, he acknowledged that Assange was suffering mightily, and then said something more optimistic than the circumstances suggested: that he thought Assange actually had the upper hand.<Cite s="2021-05-13-green-socialist-notes-episode-056-whistleblowers-with-special-guest" t="52:14" />

## What the United States has to prove

The reason is the shape of the legal question. To secure the extradition, Kiriakou said, the Justice Department has to prove to the British Supreme Court that the way the United States practises solitary confinement is not a form of torture.<Cite s="2021-05-13-green-socialist-notes-episode-056-whistleblowers-with-special-guest" t="52:44" />

That, in his assessment, is going to be impossible — and he gave two reasons why, which together with the first make three.

## The precedents

The United Nations, he noted, has already declared solitary confinement to be a form of torture.<Cite s="2021-05-13-green-socialist-notes-episode-056-whistleblowers-with-special-guest" t="52:44" />

Beyond that, two other British courts have blocked extraditions to the United States of prisoners who would have been subject to solitary confinement here, on the ground that it is a form of torture.<Cite s="2021-05-13-green-socialist-notes-episode-056-whistleblowers-with-special-guest" t="52:44" />

So there are, he concluded, three separate precedents serving right now to protect Julian Assange.<Cite s="2021-05-13-green-socialist-notes-episode-056-whistleblowers-with-special-guest" t="52:44" />

## The prediction

Kiriakou's forecast was correspondingly confident about the law and unsparing about the treatment. The British, he said, were being jerks about it and would not let Assange out of Belmarsh prison pending the appeal. But honestly, he added, he did not see how the United States could win this appeal — they were going to have to let Julian go.<Cite s="2021-05-13-green-socialist-notes-episode-056-whistleblowers-with-special-guest" t="53:16" />

The argument is one Kiriakou is unusually well placed to make and unusually invested in. His own account of the American prison system, and his description of solitary confinement as the only escape available to a prisoner who cannot defend himself, supplies the factual premise on which the whole legal objection rests.
