**Enhanced interrogation techniques** (EIT) was the official designation used by the United States [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia) for a set of interrogation methods authorized for use on detainees in the years following the September 11, 2001 attacks. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) describes the program consistently as torture and the official terminology as a euphemism. In a December 2007 ABC News interview Kiriakou became the first U.S. official to publicly confirm that the CIA had waterboarded detainees and the first to characterize the technique as torture; he was subsequently prosecuted by the Obama administration under the Espionage Act for an unrelated confirmation of a covert officer's name.

## Origin and timeline

The program was designed and operated by two American psychologists, [Mitchell and Jessen](/wiki/mitchell-and-jessen), who pitched the techniques to then-CIA Director [George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet) at a cocktail party in late October 2001. The CIA signed their contract in January 2002.<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="55:34" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="56:05" />

## The May 2002 cafeteria offer

The certification offer made to Kiriakou took the form of an informal hallway conversation in the CIA cafeteria at agency headquarters in May 2002. A friend of his from the [Counterterrorism Center](/wiki/cia) approached him and said, *"Hey, I'm so glad I ran into you — I meant to ask, do you want to be certified in the use of enhanced interrogation techniques?"* Kiriakou had not previously heard the term. The friend listed ten techniques — waterboarding, sleep deprivation, *"smack in the face,"* and others — and stated that *"the President approved it and the Justice Department approved it."*<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="00:31" /><Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="01:02" />

Kiriakou asked for time to think. He went to the agency's seventh floor and consulted a very senior CIA officer he had worked for ten years earlier. The senior officer's advice:

> First of all, let's call a spade a spade — this is a torture program. And you know how these guys are: somebody's going to go overboard, and they're going to kill a prisoner. And when that happens, there's going to be a congressional investigation and then a Justice Department investigation, and somebody's going to go to prison. You want to go to prison?<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="01:32" />

Kiriakou returned downstairs and declined certification.<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="02:03" />

In total, **fourteen** CIA officers were offered EIT certification. Two initially declined. One subsequently changed his mind. Kiriakou was the only one in the end who refused.<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="02:03" />

## Approved vs. unauthorized techniques

A central distinction in the program — and the basis of Kiriakou's recurring argument for selective prosecution — is between techniques authorized by the U.S. Department of Justice's Office of Legal Counsel and those administered by individual CIA officers on their own initiative.

**Among the approved techniques:**

- Waterboarding
- [Sleep deprivation](/wiki/sleep-deprivation) (authorized up to 12 days continuous)
- [Walling](/wiki/walling), in its formally designed variant (plywood with give, towel around the neck)
- Other techniques on the standard ten-item list<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="01:02" />

**Among the unauthorized techniques applied in practice:**

- Rectal feeding with pureed [hummus](/wiki/hummus)
- Russian roulette
- The [cockroach-in-coffin treatment](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) applied to Abu Zubaydah (ten days, just a diaper)
- The [cold cell](/wiki/cold-cell) (50°F, hourly bucket of ice water — killed two prisoners)
- [Walling](/wiki/walling) as administered against concrete-block walls without protective equipment ([Muhammad Atar](/wiki/muhammad-atar) permanently brain-damaged)
- Sexual assault using broomsticks<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="02:34" /><Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="04:09" /><Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="05:13" /><Cite s="2015-02-09-democracy-now-torture-report" t="00:13" />

## The "godfather"

While [John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan) — who later became Director of the CIA — has publicly opposed torture, Kiriakou identifies him in private as the senior architect of the program. *"He's a guy who runs out and says, 'Yeah, we shouldn't torture people.' When he wrote the fucking torture — he was the godfather of the torture program."*<Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="27:34" />

## Learned helplessness theory

The psychological theory underpinning many of the techniques, as conceived by [Mitchell and Jessen](/wiki/mitchell-and-jessen), is *"learned helplessness"* — Pavlovian conditioning in which the prisoner comes to associate a sensory cue (such as the rolled towel placed around the neck in walling) with imminent impact, eventually crumpling on presentation of the cue alone.<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="06:48" />

## The "ticking time bomb" justification

The standard public justification for EIT is the [ticking time bomb scenario](/wiki/ticking-time-bomb). Kiriakou rejects it on three grounds: it is illegal; there is no real-world ticking time bomb; and torture produces what the prisoner thinks the interrogator wants to hear, requiring six months of analyst review to separate signal from noise — by which time the hypothetical bomb has already gone off.<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="07:52" /><Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="08:23" />

## FBI walkouts

Every time [Mitchell and Jessen](/wiki/mitchell-and-jessen) took charge of an interrogation session at a CIA black site, every FBI representative in the country would leave. *"The FBI didn't even want to be in the same country where this torture was taking place."*<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="09:28" />

## Inadmissibility of obtained statements

Nothing obtained under EIT from Khalid Sheikh Mohammed, [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah), [Ramzi bin al-Shibh](/wiki/ramzi-bin-al-shibh), or Abd al-Rahim al-Nashiri is admissible at trial — *"because the CIA tortured it out of him."* The U.S. government has been left with the choice of continuing to hold the detainees indefinitely without charge or releasing them.<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="08:54" />

## The Mitchell and Jessen contract value

[Mitchell and Jessen](/wiki/mitchell-and-jessen)'s 2001–2002 contract with the CIA — to develop the EIT program from their reverse-engineering of the U.S. military's SERE training — was worth **$108 million**. *"We paid those guys $108 million to say, 'Oh, we think you should torture people. Here are the torture techniques. Just let us know when you want us to start.'"*<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="28:40" />

## The bodies-buried-on-site protocol

When prisoners died during EIT sessions — fatalities occurring particularly with the [cold cell](/wiki/cold-cell) (hypothermia) and prolonged [sleep deprivation](/wiki/sleep-deprivation) (organ failure) — the standard disposal procedure at the secret site was on-site burial: *"When people would die, they would just dig a hole next to the interrogation building, put him in the hole, cover it up, and then bring the next guy in."* In at least one documented case, the disposal was delayed pending direction from headquarters; the on-site instruction was *"put him on ice until we can figure out what to do,"* interpreted literally by personnel on the ground, who placed the body in a bathtub filled with ice until the body began to decompose.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="20:55" />

## The secretary who fainted

A documented internal-personnel consequence of EIT, recorded by Kiriakou from his post as Executive Assistant to the Deputy Director for Operations, was the case of a CIA secretary who fainted during an [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) interrogation session and subsequently *"curtailed her assignment"* — sending an unprompted cable to headquarters announcing her return. *"That is a career-ending decision to curtail an assignment."* The episode was, per Kiriakou, one of several similar curtailments demonstrating that he was not alone in his moral assessment of the program — and that nonetheless *"certainly somebody's going to come out and say something. And nobody did."*<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="17:45" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="18:16" />

## Twenty-two years, no prosecutions

As of the most recent source in KiriPedia's corpus, no [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia) officer has been criminally prosecuted in connection with any technique applied under the EIT program — authorized or unauthorized. *"Here we are twenty-two years after 9/11 — literally no one has been prosecuted for that crime. No one. Nor will anyone be prosecuted, because the CIA ensured that no prosecutions could be carried out."* The only person to have served federal prison time in connection with the program is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) himself, for an unrelated Espionage Act prosecution.<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="09:28" /><Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="10:30" />

## The sociopath-in-authority framing

Kiriakou attributes the program's existence less to deliberate malice than to a CIA personnel-selection problem: *"You put sociopaths in positions of authority where they come to believe that they're above the law, and that because they're the good guys they can and should do anything that they want to do."* He explicitly extends this characterization to [Jose Rodriguez](/wiki/jose-rodriguez), [James Mitchell, and Bruce Jessen](/wiki/mitchell-and-jessen), all of whom he believes genuinely think they are the good guys.<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="11:33" /><Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="12:04" />

## Unauthorized techniques

Per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), the [enhanced interrogation](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation) program rapidly exceeded the techniques approved by the Justice Department and the President. *"They did many, many, many things — not just to [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) but to most if not all of our high-value prisoners — that were never authorized."*<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:49:11" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:49:43" />

Kiriakou's specific examples of unauthorized techniques:

- **Forced rectal feeding with [hummus](/wiki/hummus)** — *"you can't force hummus upside someone's rectum."*
- **Russian roulette with prisoners.**
- **The Abu Zubaydah cockroach coffin** — Abu Zubaydah had an *"irrational fear of insects"*; interrogators put him in a coffin, dumped a box of cockroaches in with him, and closed it for ten days *"just with a diaper."*
- **[Sleep deprivation](/wiki/sleep-deprivation)** authorized for twelve days, even though *"the American Psychological Association which is done studies on this kind of thing — people begin to go insane at seven days with no sleep, they begin to die of organ failure with nine days."* People died of it.
- **The [cold cell](/wiki/cold-cell)** — never authorized — *"two prisoners with that technique."*
- **[Walling](/wiki/walling)** — supposed to use a rolled towel around the neck against a fiberboard wall; in practice, no towel, against a concrete wall. [Muhammad Atar](/wiki/muhammad-atar) is now permanently brain-damaged and unable to participate in his own defense.<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:49:43" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:50:14" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:50:46" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:51:16" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:51:47" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:52:18" />

In late August 2023 the Department of Defense conceded that [Ramzi bin al-Shibh](/wiki/ramzi-bin-al-shibh) — one of the 9/11 co-conspirators — cannot be tried, because the Pentagon's own psychiatrist has determined him clinically insane as a direct result of [sleep deprivation](/wiki/sleep-deprivation).<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:52:18" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:52:48" />

## Shutdown of the program

According to [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), the [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s enhanced interrogation program ran for approximately four years before being shut down in 2006. The internal determination was that the information being obtained under torture *"wasn't amounting to anything."* The shutdown decision was attributed by Kiriakou to CIA Director Porter Goss.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="8:21" />

## CIA training versus actual conduct

Kiriakou has noted the direct contradiction between the program and CIA training doctrine. CIA operational training explicitly teaches that coercion and threats are never to be used in recruiting or handling sources — not only because they are unethical, but because they are ineffective and can provoke violent retaliation. Recruits were trained that asset development works through establishing genuine friendship and rapport.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="40:40" />

## Revenge as motivator

Kiriakou has described the dominant emotional driver behind the program not as a belief in its effectiveness, but as a desire for revenge. CIA leaders who had spent decades building careers felt that their legacies would be defined by the failure to prevent the September 11 attacks, and *"had to do literally anything to stop them. And even if it's not to stop them, it's to pay them back for what they did."*<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="48:29" /> Kiriakou recalled a CIA supervisor whispering *"Kill them all"* as he departed for Pakistan months after 9/11.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="49:34" />

## Ineffectiveness

Kiriakou has described the practical problem with torture-derived intelligence: it generates information that must be verified, consuming months of analyst time and producing false leads that allow adversaries to continue planning. Subjects under torture will provide partial truths — *"a kernel of truth"* — sufficient to stop the pain in the moment but designed to waste the interrogator's time. *"It's terrible. But we can't underestimate the role of revenge."*<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="47:59" />

## Presidential authorization

The [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s enhanced interrogation program was not a rogue operation. According to [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), torture was official U.S. policy, personally authorized by President George W. Bush. Bush signed off on the program and its specific techniques. Kiriakou described this as a distinction that matters: the people who designed and conducted the program were following orders from the highest level of the executive branch, not acting outside sanctioned channels.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="20:00" />

[John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan), who later became [CIA](/wiki/cia) Director, was part of the leadership structure that oversaw the program during this period.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="20:30" />

## The *"legal"* transformation of 2002

Kiriakou described the legal architecture around the program as a deliberate reinterpretation of existing law. The [Federal Torture Act](/wiki/federal-torture-act) had been on the books since 1946, with documented enforcement — including executions of Japanese soldiers after World War II. In 2002, Justice Department opinions effectively suspended that history. In Kiriakou's framing: *"But then in 2002, like magic, it's all legal."*<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="22:00" />

## Origin — cocktail party, October 2001

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described the origin of the [enhanced interrogation program](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation) as beginning approximately one month after September 11, 2001, when someone approached [CIA](/wiki/cia) Director [George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet) at a cocktail party and proposed it. This person was in or had proximity to the leadership of the Counterterrorism Center. He told Tenet about two psychologists who had worked for the Air Force's SERE training program — survival, evasion, resistance, and escape — and had reverse-engineered those techniques. For $108 million, they would contract with the CIA and implement what they proposed to call *"enhanced interrogation techniques."* Contracts were signed.<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="11:30" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="12:00" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="12:30" />

## Only one of fourteen refused

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) was approached in May 2002 while standing in the [CIA](/wiki/cia) cafeteria sandwich line by a senior officer who casually asked if he wanted to be certified in the use of enhanced interrogation techniques. Kiriakou had never heard the term. After hearing a description of the techniques, he said: *"Buddy, that sounds like a torture program."* He consulted a very senior officer on the CIA's seventh floor (executive floor) — someone he had worked for in the Middle East — who told him: *"Let's call a spade a spade here. This is a torture program. They're cowboys and somebody's going to kill a prisoner. When that happens, there's going to be a congressional investigation and somebody's going to go to prison. Do you want to go to prison?"*<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="18:00" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="18:30" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="19:00" />

Kiriakou declined. He stated that fourteen people in total were approached. He was the only one who said no. His reflection: *"The crazy thing is I knew these guys. I was friends with these guys. Our wives were friends. Our kids played together. I had no idea that they had the ability to become monsters, murderers."*<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="19:00" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="19:30" />

## FBI removed all personnel

The FBI was so deeply at odds with the [CIA](/wiki/cia) over the torture program that it removed all FBI personnel from the country where the secret prison was located — not wanting to be in the same country while the torture was occurring.<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="24:30" />

## Mitchell and Jessen fabricated the Zubaydah result

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described his error in the December 2007 Brian Ross interview: he said that [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) had been waterboarded once and that it worked. He now characterizes this as incorrect. The reason he said it: Mitchell and Jessen had reported to the [CIA](/wiki/cia) that they waterboarded Zubaydah once and he produced intelligence.<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="36:30" />

The reality, which was not revealed until 2005 when the [CIA](/wiki/cia) inspector general investigated: the FBI interrogator [Ali Soufan](/wiki/ali-soufan) had been getting actionable intelligence from Zubaydah daily through rapport-based interrogation, cabling his findings back to the FBI. Those cables were never reaching CIA because the FBI and CIA computer systems were incompatible with each other. On August 2, 2002, Director [Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet) asked President Bush to transfer primacy of the Zubaydah interrogation from the FBI to the CIA. That day, CIA began torturing Zubaydah; he immediately went silent and stopped providing intelligence.<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="35:30" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="36:00" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="36:30" />

Mitchell and Jessen then went into the FBI's system, pulled Soufan's cables, retyped them in the CIA system, and reported: *"We waterboarded him once and look what he told us."* The [CIA](/wiki/cia) inspector general confirmed in 2005 that this had occurred — the intelligence had been fabricated as their product. The finding was not declassified until 2009.<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="37:00" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="37:30" />

## Videotape destruction — Miers warning ignored

White House counsel Harriet Miers explicitly told Jose Rodriguez (Deputy Director for Operations) and [Gina Haspel](/wiki/gina-haspel) (head of counterterrorism at the time) not to destroy the videotapes of the torture sessions. As soon as Rodriguez and Haspel returned to [CIA](/wiki/cia) headquarters, they put all the tapes in an industrial grinder and destroyed them.<Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="38:00" /><Cite s="2025-07-09-pbd-podcast-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell-israel" t="38:30" />

## The cold cell — death in custody

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) disclosed, as an executive assistant to the [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s Deputy Director at the time, that he received a cable reporting that a prisoner in a [cold cell](/wiki/cold-cell) *"didn't make it through the night."* The facility did not know what to do with the body. They could not bury him locally. They could not return him to his family. They packed the body in ice and cremated it. The man's existence was effectively erased.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="1:07:00" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="1:07:30" />

## Sleep deprivation — authorized twelve days

[Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described [sleep deprivation](/wiki/sleep-deprivation) as authorized up to twelve days under the [CIA](/wiki/cia) program. He cited the American Psychological Association's finding that people begin to lose their minds at day seven and enter organ failure at day nine without sleep. He noted that Don Rumsfeld had a stand-up desk and claimed to work twenty-four hours without sitting; Rumsfeld did not believe prolonged sleep deprivation was unusual. Kiriakou's assessment: keeping someone awake for twelve days is torture.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="1:08:00" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="1:08:30" />

## Liberty University ticking-bomb exam

A colleague at Liberty University, where [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) taught for several years, sent him a final exam consisting of four questions on the [ticking time-bomb](/wiki/ticking-time-bomb) scenario. The first three questions escalated progressively: would you use enhanced interrogation on the suspect? Would you use it on his wife? Would you beat the children in front of the parents? Each asked the student to explain their answer. The fourth and final question: *"You've died and you're standing before the judgment seat of Christ and he tells you to explain your actions. What do you tell him?"* Kiriakou described this as leaving him speechless and said he still thinks about it.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="55:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="56:00" />

## Rectal feeding and cultural humiliation (Theo Von, June 2026)

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described rectal feeding as an authorized technique in the [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s [enhanced interrogation program](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation): tubes were inserted and liquid food — he specified hummus — was pumped in. He described the purpose as cultural rather than nutritional, exploiting the particular shame attached in Islam to nakedness and bodily humiliation, especially before women.<Cite s="2026-06-05-theo-von-podcast-661" t="1:08:30" /><Cite s="2026-06-05-theo-von-podcast-661" t="1:09:00" />

Kiriakou described the deliberate use of female interrogators as a humiliation mechanism: prisoners were stripped naked in the presence of women specifically because of those beliefs, the combination of nudity, [cold-cell](/wiki/cold-cell) temperatures, and female presence designed to break religious dignity alongside physical endurance.<Cite s="2026-06-05-theo-von-podcast-661" t="1:08:00" /><Cite s="2026-06-05-theo-von-podcast-661" t="1:08:30" />

## Sensory deprivation — *"like you're dead"*

Kiriakou described sensory deprivation as, in his assessment, worse than [waterboarding](/wiki/waterboarding): prisoners placed in an isolation tank — total silence, total darkness, surrounded by water — for periods of up to three weeks. His description of the psychological experience: *"You're complete and total. Like you're dead."*<Cite s="2026-06-05-theo-von-podcast-661" t="1:07:00" /><Cite s="2026-06-05-theo-von-podcast-661" t="1:07:30" />
