**Kiriakou's television pilots** are, in his own framing, a hobby rather than a second career: "I as a hobby I like to write television pilots. And I've been, knock on wood, I've been very successful in the last 15 years. I've sold eight of them."<Cite s="2026-07-09-byrna-how-to-spot-danger-before-it-s-too-late" t="28:12" />

The first he sold was *Bomb Squad*, a procedural following a day in the life of the New York City bomb squad.<Cite s="2026-07-09-byrna-how-to-spot-danger-before-it-s-too-late" t="28:12" /> He wrote it because of something a friend assigned to the squad told him, which he equates with the lesson of surveillance: fewer than one in a hundred bomb threats called in to the police has anything to do with terrorism, maybe one in a thousand. Almost every bomb threat, bomb scare or actual device is a jilted spouse or lover, an insurance scammer, or a mafia hit.<Cite s="2026-07-09-byrna-how-to-spot-danger-before-it-s-too-late" t="28:44" />

The conclusion [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) draws from that is the same one he draws about [situational awareness](/wiki/kiriakou-situational-awareness) generally: it means the victims are ordinary people going about their normal daily business, so everybody has to be aware of their surroundings and personally responsible for their own safety.<Cite s="2026-07-09-byrna-how-to-spot-danger-before-it-s-too-late" t="29:16" />

The pilots sit alongside his nine published books and a tenth commissioned as his first novel.<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="04:42" />
