**William J. "Bill" Casey** served as Director of Central Intelligence under President Ronald Reagan during the 1980s. Within KiriPedia's source corpus he is referenced principally as the founder of the [CIA National Resources Division](/wiki/cia-national-resources-division), the agency's domestic intelligence-collection arm.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="39:27" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="40:01" />

Casey is characterized as *"the notorious criminal that he was"* — but as having been *"actually right on"* about the National Resources Division concept. His insight: large numbers of patriotic American businesspeople routinely travel to "denied areas" — countries such as China, the Soviet Union, and Cuba where the CIA cannot maintain a clandestine presence — and would willingly debrief on what they had observed if asked overtly.<Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="40:01" /><Cite s="2023-11-12-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-1" t="40:33" />

## Sidelining Congress and Iran-Contra

A former OSS officer and self-described *"true believer,"* Casey spent his tenure as CIA director trying to cut Congress out of intelligence oversight entirely, ending the brief post-[Church Committee](/wiki/church-committee) period of legitimate congressional oversight around 1982.<Cite s="2026-06-16-julian-dorey-daily-mkultra-twin-experiment" t="20:51" /> Kiriakou says those efforts to sideline Congress helped produce the Iran-Contra scandal; Casey died of a stroke followed by a brain aneurysm shortly before the end of his term, with oversight never fully restored by the time he was gone.<Cite s="2026-06-16-julian-dorey-daily-mkultra-twin-experiment" t="21:21" />

In 1986, amid the fallout, the Senate held a no-confidence vote on Casey as Director of Central Intelligence. Republicans negotiated the wording so that it passed only in the anodyne form: *"William Casey is not unqualified to continue as director."*<Cite s="2026-06-16-julian-dorey-daily-mkultra-twin-experiment" t="21:51" />
