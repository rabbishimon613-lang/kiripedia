**Christopher Hitchens** was a British-American journalist and author whose 2008 filmed self-experiment with [waterboarding](/wiki/waterboarding) [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) treats as the canonical rebuttal to those who describe the technique as *"not a big deal."*<Cite s="2026-04-23-pbd-podcast-ex-cia-officer-confronted-over-zionist-accusa" t="1:22:29" /><Cite s="2026-04-23-pbd-podcast-ex-cia-officer-confronted-over-zionist-accusa" t="1:23:34" /> Kiriakou recounts the sequence: Hitchens was placed on his back, a towel was laid across his face, and he expressed misplaced confidence he would be able to breathe. He lasted, in Kiriakou's telling, about *"seventeen seconds"* before he jumped up.<Cite s="2026-04-23-pbd-podcast-ex-cia-officer-confronted-over-zionist-accusa" t="1:22:29" />

What mattered more to Kiriakou than the length of the demonstration was what followed. *"For the rest of his life, every time he couldn't breathe for a second, he would have panic attacks. Because of the impact of waterboarding had on him."*<Cite s="2026-04-23-pbd-podcast-ex-cia-officer-confronted-over-zionist-accusa" t="1:23:01" /> Kiriakou closes the anecdote with the point he says the corollary makes on the non-specialists: *"Believe me, it's torture."*<Cite s="2026-04-23-pbd-podcast-ex-cia-officer-confronted-over-zionist-accusa" t="1:23:34" />
