**Intelligence peddlers** occupy the middle band of [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s taxonomy of people who volunteer information to an intelligence service.

Most approaches are worthless: [probes](/wiki/the-walk-in-probe) sent by a hostile service, or stories simply made up. Of the remainder, Kiriakou says, some are what the trade calls intelligence peddlers — people who genuinely do hold a small nugget of information. They bring it in, ask for money, and are given a hundred or a hundred and fifty dollars. Then they take the same nugget to the British embassy, the French embassy, the Russian embassy and the Chinese embassy: *"and that's a month's salary."*<Cite s="2026-01-05-dead-drop-s1e9-the-hothead" t="28:51" />

The remaining one per cent are what he calls the real McCoy — the nuclear scientist, the three-star general who took the day off, *"somebody really great who you would never encounter in your day-to-day life."*<Cite s="2026-01-05-dead-drop-s1e9-the-hothead" t="29:22" />

That distribution produces the working rule. Because the genuine article is rare and unannounced, *"you have to treat everybody equally just in case it's that one out of the hundred that's gonna make your career."*<Cite s="2026-01-05-dead-drop-s1e9-the-hothead" t="29:22" />
