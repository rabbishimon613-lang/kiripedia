**The Green Line** divides Nicosia between the Republic of Cyprus and the Turkish-occupied north. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) was taken to it in July 2026 during a three-day visit that also included the old town inside the Venetian walls, the Troodos Mountains, and the eleventh-century Saint John Lampadistis Monastery, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="32:16" /><Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="32:47" />

He said he had forgotten how powerful it is to see the line, the occupation forces and the settlers on the other side, and was explicit that he did not want to make the situation sound worse than it is — but characterised much of what happens along it as done "just in order to humiliate." He cited one of the biggest flags in the world laid out on the mountainside and lit at night, visible every day and every night for fifty-two years of occupation, and the call to prayer pointed at the Greek neighbourhoods of the city.<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="33:18" /><Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="33:49" />

His conclusion was that the Turks are not interested in a settlement: "They like the status quo just the way it is and that's not acceptable."<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="33:49" />

The city's mayor took him up to the roof of the municipality, about seventy metres from the gate leading into the occupied sector. From there, Kiriakou said, one side is modern and first world with tall buildings and a moving economy, and on the other "there's literally nothing happening there. It's like out of the early '60s."<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="34:20" /><Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="34:50" />
