The **Federal Correctional Institution at Loretto** (FCI Loretto) is a U.S. federal prison in Loretto, Pennsylvania. It is classified by the Bureau of Prisons as low-medium security — *"a low security prison, but it's called a low medium, and then there's a high medium. So this was low medium."* [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) served twenty-three months of his thirty-month Intelligence Identities Protection Act sentence at FCI Loretto between 2013 and 2015.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:01:33" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:02:03" />

## The countermanded sentence designation

At Kiriakou's sentencing, the presiding judge — over no objection from the Justice Department — designated him for a minimum-security work camp: *"no bars on the windows, no locks on the doors, you're free to come and go as you please, you're just on your honor not to abscond."* Most inmates in that designation work at a small nearby college.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="59:29" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:00:00" />

On Kiriakou's arrival three months later, however, the receiving officer told him his paperwork directed him not to the work camp across the street but to the main FCI prison. The discrepancy, Kiriakou subsequently learned, was the result of direct administrative pressure from [John Brennan](/wiki/john-brennan): *"Brennan was so angry at the shortness of my sentence that he told them, 'Make it as difficult as possible.'"*<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:00:31" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:01:03" />

A guard's only on-arrival advice: *"If anybody comes into your cell uninvited, that's an act of aggression."*<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:01:33" />

## The Italian-mafia placement

Within twenty minutes of being placed in his cell, two inmates — *"one with a swastika tattoo across his neck and onto his face, the other with FUCK YOU tattooed on his eyelids"* — entered uninvited and asked Kiriakou whether he was *"a fag,"* *"a rat,"* or *"a chomo"* (child molester). On answering no to each, Kiriakou was assigned to sit with the Aryans at meals.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:13:27" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:13:58" />

The assignment was overturned the following day by an inmate in the cell across the hall — the boss of the Bonanno crime family, known to Kiriakou as Pete — who, on observing Kiriakou's seating placement, told him: *"From today, you're with the Italians."* Kiriakou subsequently learned that another Philadelphia mafia inmate, Mark Lanzelotti, had — on reading in the *New York Times* that Kiriakou would be placed at FCI Loretto — gone to every Italian inmate in the institution to brief them: *"There's a CIA guy coming here. He's not an FBI agent. The FBI are cops and rats. The CIA protected us from the Muslims."* The Italian placement made Kiriakou's tenure at the prison non-violent.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:13:58" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:14:29" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:14:59" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:15:33" />

## The two documented intelligence-service entrapment attempts

Kiriakou identifies two episodes at FCI Loretto in which prison guards — per Kiriakou, on direction from outside the prison — attempted to provoke an incident that would have lengthened his sentence:

- **The Iraqi imam setup.** Three days after Kiriakou's arrival, a cellmate (an Australian arsonist) walked him around the facility and introduced him to an Iraqi Kurdish imam who spoke no English and had been jailed on a terrorism charge for translating a Stinger-missile bill of sale. Kiriakou was called to the lieutenant's office the next day and told the imam had — after meeting Kiriakou — called a number in Pakistan to arrange Kiriakou's killing. The imam was simultaneously told the same about Kiriakou. Kiriakou figured out the trick on noticing the imam spoke only Arabic and Kurdish, not the language of the alleged Pakistan contact, then reported the setup in his *Letter from Loretto* (published by Arianna Huffington at *Huffington Post*) — at which point Jake Tapper drove to the prison to interview him and the story reached CNN, *Time*, *Playboy*, and NPR.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:17:36" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:19:10" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:20:12" />
- **The Taliban-spokesman handshake.** A separate inmate (the spokesman for the Taliban, on transfer from New Jersey for four days only) approached Kiriakou in the yard with hand extended; Kiriakou observed a guard in the woods outside the perimeter operating a long-lens camera. Kiriakou refused contact, the spokesman left, and the inmate was transferred out shortly afterward.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:23:20" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:23:51" />

## The warden incident

After the *Letter from Loretto* attracted national press coverage, Kiriakou was summoned to the warden's office and threatened with solitary confinement. His reply:

> Warden, with all due respect, I've gone nose-to-nose with al-Qaeda, with Hezbollah, with the Iranians, and you want me to be afraid of you? Give me some credit. I've lived in Yemen, in Pakistan. I'm not afraid of your Loretto, Pennsylvania solitary. Besides — go ahead and send me to solitary. CNN's going to be waiting for you next to your car in the parking lot.<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:21:15" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="1:21:46" />

Kiriakou was not sent to solitary at any point during his sentence.

## Camera resets and the midnight gap

Kiriakou disputes conspiracy theories about gaps in Epstein's prison surveillance footage, drawing on his own experience: *"Every camera that works resets itself every night at midnight. And they record over the previous day's recording."* He adds that cameras in federal prisons routinely malfunction regardless: *"The cameras never work in any prison. When I was in prison, because everybody has their heads up their asses working in these prisons."* The chapel at FCI Loretto served as a de facto meeting place for sexual activity among inmates specifically because *"the cameras didn't work"* there and everyone knew it.<Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:03:43" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:04:13" />

## The Taliban spokesman setup

Approximately three months into his sentence, Kiriakou's attorney had warned him that the CIA was *"very upset"* about the shortness of his sentence and might attempt to set him up in prison. An Afghan-American pharmacist serving time for an opioid offense informed Kiriakou that *"the Taliban spokesman"* — identified as a man from New Jersey — had requested an introduction. Kiriakou declined. Days later, while exercising in the prison yard, the man approached him with an outstretched hand. Kiriakou put both hands up: *"Don't fucking touch me."* Looking past the man, he spotted a guard in the woods outside the fence photographing the exchange with a long-distance lens. Kiriakou refused all physical contact and continued declining until the man left. The man was transferred out after four days. Later, passing the guard: *"Nice try, amateur."*<Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:20:13" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:20:43" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:21:15" /><Cite s="2026-05-05-morgan-nelson-cia-whistleblower" t="1:21:45" />

## Assassination setup attempt

Shortly after arriving, [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) was called to a lieutenant's office and shown a photograph of a fellow prisoner — a man he had met briefly days earlier. Officers told him the prisoner had made a call to Pakistan after their meeting and been instructed to kill Kiriakou. Skeptical of the story, Kiriakou later approached the prisoner directly and learned that the prisoner had been told the same thing in reverse: that Kiriakou had called a Washington number and been instructed to kill him. The two recognized the setup as a manufactured pretext and *"got a laugh about how ham-handed this was."*<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="19:50" /> Kiriakou identified the conduct as a Class D felony — conspiracy to promote violence in a federal facility — and wrote about the incident publicly.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="19:50" />

## Media access and the warden's calculation

After Kiriakou wrote about the assassination setup on his blog, the post was amplified by Arianna Huffington on the Huffington Post. This attracted visits from Jake Tapper, *The Economist*, *The Atlantic*, and CNN. Kiriakou leveraged his media access to write consistently about prison conditions, medical facilities, and guard-encouraged violence. He described the warden's decision not to send him to solitary confinement as a calculation: *"Oh, so I could have CNN standing in next to my car in the parking lot tomorrow? No, thank you."*<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="21:22" /> As long as Kiriakou did not name guards individually, the administration tolerated his reporting.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="21:52" />

## Mail reading

Prison officials were found to be slitting the sides of Kiriakou's outgoing letters and resealing them with tape. Policy in a low-security federal facility is to inspect incoming mail for contraband but not to read outgoing mail. Kiriakou went to the lieutenant's office and demonstrated the correct technique — rolling the letter inside the envelope with tongs to extract it without cutting — and told them they should use it. The monitoring stopped after that.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="21:52" />

## Guard culture

Kiriakou encountered hostility only from guards, never from fellow prisoners. He quoted sociologist Peter Moskos, a professor of criminal justice at John Jay College, whose book *In Defense of Flogging* described the Bureau of Prisons as *"little more than an employment agency for otherwise unemployable undereducated rural white men"* — people who washed out of the military or failed police academy training, paid modest wages but with federal benefits, and in Kiriakou's view inclined toward bullying because the position gave them authority over people who could not retaliate.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="25:29" />

## Prison social structure

Kiriakou described the social organization of the prison as resembling *"1950s America,"* with rigid racial segregation in all common areas including the cafeteria and television rooms. White prisoners were subdivided by allegiance: one Aryan Brotherhood table, one table shared between Italian organized crime members and Native Americans, and remaining white tables reserved for sex offenders and informants. Black prisoners occupied roughly half the cafeteria; Hispanic prisoners sat separately at the back.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="29:10" />

Upon arriving, Kiriakou was approached by two Aryan prisoners who vetted him with three questions: whether he was a rat, whether he was gay, and whether he was a *"chomo"* (child molester). Having answered no to all three, he was invited to sit with the Aryans.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="27:05" /> He instead ended up under the protection of Italian organized crime figures after one member — who had read in the *New York Times* that Kiriakou was arriving — personally explained to every Italian prisoner, including bosses of two crime families, the distinction between a CIA officer and an FBI agent: *"FBI agents are cops. CIA officers protected us from the terrorists."* From that point, Kiriakou sat with the Italians and was left alone by all other factions.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="28:07" />

## Violence

In two years at Loretto, Kiriakou witnessed three physical altercations: two over control of a television, and one in which an Aryan prisoner called a white inmate a rat and was beaten by him. Kiriakou attributed the low level of violence to the fact that most prisoners had worked their way down from higher-security facilities and were unwilling to jeopardize their placement.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="29:43" />
