**The spy shop** was a London retailer with a sister shop in New York. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says he went in every time he passed through London and bought small items — a pinhole camera, something of that sort — partly for fun, and that "we bought a lot of the equipment that we needed at the spy shop."<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="08:18" /><Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="08:49" />

The rationale he gives is economic: "Why reinvent the wheel? Why spend a million dollars developing a tool when you can go to the spy shop and buy it for a thousand dollars?"<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="08:49" />

He has not been able to find it since, and believes the business model was overtaken by the pace of technological change. He cites a Washington attorney friend who quit law to move to Southern California and open a spy-gadget store and development shop, which lasted about five years — by the time an order arrived, the item was already outdated.<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="08:49" /><Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="09:19" />

The episode is part of his broader answer on whether the [CIA](/wiki/cia) has James Bond gadgets: twenty or twenty-five years ago it did not, because buying what an operation needed was far easier and cheaper than building it. That changed after 9/11, when Congress gave the agency a waiver to invest in technology companies — including, as its first investor, [Palantir](/wiki/palantir).<Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="06:44" /><Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="07:15" /><Cite s="2026-07-09-part-2-how-well-do-you-really-know-him" t="07:46" />
