[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) argues that national-security prosecutions are best understood as deterrence rather than law enforcement, and that the agencies involved want different things from them. *"The [CIA](/wiki/cia) doesn't care about prosecutions. The CIA cares about sending a message … The [FBI](/wiki/fbi), sure, they care about prosecutions, because that's how they get promoted."*<Cite s="2019-04-07-unity4j-vigil-confirm-not-panel" t="16:46" /><Cite s="2019-04-07-unity4j-vigil-confirm-not-panel" t="17:17" />

Applied to his own case, the arithmetic of the sentence was beside the point: *"the point wasn't to see how many years in prison I could get. The point was to frighten anybody else who was even considering blowing the whistle on waste, fraud, abuse or illegality."*<Cite s="2019-04-07-unity4j-vigil-confirm-not-panel" t="17:17" />

## The measured effect

Kiriakou gives one concrete measure of whether it worked. *"A New York Times reporter told me that on the day of my arrest, every national security source that the New York Times had went completely silent. That's exactly what they wanted to happen."*<Cite s="2019-04-07-unity4j-vigil-confirm-not-panel" t="20:58" />

The companion case he returns to is [Thomas Drake](/wiki/thomas-drake), who followed every internal channel available to him and was charged with nine felonies including seven counts of espionage without ever having given classified information to a journalist. *"Even though the charges against Tom were all dropped, the government declared victory — because they ruined him, and they made an example of him. So it silenced anybody else at NSA who was thinking of going forward."*<Cite s="2019-04-07-unity4j-vigil-confirm-not-panel" t="22:01" /><Cite s="2019-04-07-unity4j-vigil-confirm-not-panel" t="22:32" />

## Applied to Assange

Kiriakou applied the same logic to [Julian Assange](/wiki/julian-assange) during his embassy confinement, arguing the [CIA](/wiki/cia) had no particular interest in a trial: *"operationally it's better for the CIA the longer Julian remains in the embassy … the message is, we have kept this man in what is in effect solitary confinement for the last six and a half years, and there's nothing he can do about it."*<Cite s="2019-04-07-unity4j-vigil-confirm-not-panel" t="16:46" /><Cite s="2019-04-07-unity4j-vigil-confirm-not-panel" t="17:17" /> What made an example necessary at all, on his account, was that WikiLeaks worked: *"he created something that actually worked … something that actually protected its sources and that actually exposed war crimes and crimes against humanity — and so they had to make sure that that ended."*<Cite s="2019-04-07-unity4j-vigil-confirm-not-panel" t="17:49" />

He cautions against treating the pressure as coming from one place. The government, he says, is not acting as a monolithic organisation in such cases: the CIA has its own interests, the FBI and the Justice Department have theirs, and the White House and a State Department then led by [Mike Pompeo](/wiki/mike-pompeo) have theirs again.<Cite s="2019-04-07-unity4j-vigil-confirm-not-panel" t="19:25" />
