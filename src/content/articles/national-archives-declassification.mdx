**National Archives declassification** is [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account of why U.S. government records stay secret long past the 30-to-35-year statutory-declassification deadline. The [National Archives](/wiki/national-archives-declassification), the ultimate repository of classified documents, runs a computer system 20 years out of date; its head, he says, has repeatedly asked Congress for a *smaller* budget; and the system has no connectivity, forcing agencies to mail documents double-wrapped by hand.<Cite s="2024-07-29-news-beat-assange-israel-security-state" t="38:43" /><Cite s="2024-07-29-news-beat-assange-israel-security-state" t="39:45" /><Cite s="2024-07-29-news-beat-assange-israel-security-state" t="40:15" /> The result is a Freedom of Information Act backlog he estimates at 500 years — he filed a request in 2015 about a 1950s science-fiction writer's possible CIA work, and nine years later was told he would be answered in *"about 30 more."*<Cite s="2024-07-29-news-beat-assange-israel-security-state" t="40:47" /><Cite s="2024-07-29-news-beat-assange-israel-security-state" t="41:19" /> As an analyst, he once spent six weeks of 12-hour days clearing an eight-foot stack of Middle East FOIA documents, almost none still classified.<Cite s="2024-07-29-news-beat-assange-israel-security-state" t="43:25" /><Cite s="2024-07-29-news-beat-assange-israel-security-state" t="43:58" />
