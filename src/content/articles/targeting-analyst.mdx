A **targeting analyst** is, in [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s account, a specialized CIA analyst assigned almost exclusively to the Directorate of Operations and its Counterterrorism Center, distinct from an intelligence analyst who reads all-source reporting and writes papers.<Cite s="2025-08-18-hope-2025-kiriakou-talk" t="17:56" /><Cite s="2025-08-18-hope-2025-kiriakou-talk" t="18:26" /> The targeting analyst instead pours through millions of pieces of data, *"much of it metadata,"* to physically locate a person — for only two reasons, to capture or to kill.<Cite s="2025-08-18-hope-2025-kiriakou-talk" t="18:57" /> Kiriakou flew a targeting-analyst friend to Islamabad, who over several weeks built a butcher-paper *"spiderweb"* around [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah)'s phone number — concentric layers of every contact — narrowing the search to 14 sites.<Cite s="2025-08-18-hope-2025-kiriakou-talk" t="17:25" /><Cite s="2025-08-18-hope-2025-kiriakou-talk" t="19:28" /> Kiriakou has described requesting one the moment his own attempts to locate Zubaydah in Pakistan failed: *"I told CIA headquarters I needed a targeting analyst."*<Cite s="2018-06-05-tedx-foggybottom-how-i-became-a-cia-whistleblower" t="1:15" />

Kiriakou has given the same core definition in numerous other interviews, consistently describing the targeting analyst as someone who sifts through millions of pieces of metadata to geolocate a single person so he can be captured or killed — distinct from a regular analyst who writes reports for policymakers.<Cite s="2026-05-31-lazaros-sideras" t="28:20" /><Cite s="2019-03-14-salem-access-tv-public" t="15:06" /> In a fuller telling of the [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) search, Kiriakou says a CIA "targeteer" was flown in and narrowed Zubaydah's possible location down to 14 sites by combing through thousands of pages of documents.<Cite s="2016-07-11-adventures-in-the-free-sta" t="03:49" /> In another version, he describes calling headquarters to request a targeting analyst, who after about two weeks of work narrowed the search to 15 possible sites, prompting Kiriakou to organize the simultaneous raid.<Cite s="2025-06-06-red-apple-podcast-network" t="16:42" />

Kiriakou says the CIA's use of skilled targeting analysts — capable of processing a million pieces of metadata to locate a target within about two weeks — made older methods like remote viewing operationally unnecessary once the discipline matured following its advent in the 1990s and its successes after 9/11.<Cite s="2025-05-05-austin-and-matt" t="1:20:40" />
