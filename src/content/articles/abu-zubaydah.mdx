**Abu Zubaydah** — real name **Zayn al-Abidin Muhammad Hussein**, known to his attorneys as *"Zay"* — is a detainee held by the United States since March 2002, when he was captured in Pakistan by a [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/cia) team led by [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou). At the time of his capture he was believed to be a senior al-Qaeda figure. He was the first prisoner on whom the agency's [enhanced interrogation techniques](/wiki/enhanced-interrogation) program was applied; the program had been pitched to CIA Director [George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet) by [Mitchell and Jessen](/wiki/mitchell-and-jessen) in late October 2001, contracted in January 2002, and was first deployed against Abu Zubaydah on **August 2, 2002**.<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="02:34" /><Cite s="2026-01-16-julian-dorey-french-intelligence" t="56:05" />

## The Soufan period

In the early months of detention, Abu Zubaydah was handled by [FBI](#) special agent [Ali Soufan](/wiki/ali-soufan) under the rationale that the September 11 attacks remained an open criminal investigation in which the Bureau retained primacy. Soufan's standard FBI rapport-building approach — coffee, oranges, permission to write to family — produced, after weeks of initial silence, the only operationally actionable intelligence ever obtained from Abu Zubaydah: the al-Qaeda cell-structure framework (Dusseldorf example) and the first identification of *"Mukhtar"* — Abu Zubaydah's correction of Soufan — as [Khalid Sheikh Mohammed](#). The disclosure ended a six-year U.S.-intelligence blind spot dating to the 1996 [Bojinka plot](/wiki/bojinka-plot).<Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="22:30" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="23:00" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="25:34" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="26:05" />

## Approved techniques

Among the techniques approved by the U.S. Department of Justice and applied to Abu Zubaydah was waterboarding. During one waterboarding session, his heart stopped; the on-scene doctor revived him so the session could continue. *"Didn't the Germans do that? Come on now. Now we're doing it."*<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="00:00" /><Cite s="2025-10-10-joe-rogan-experience-2392" t="11:30" />

## The cockroach-in-coffin treatment

CIA interrogators determined that Abu Zubaydah had an irrational fear of insects. They placed him in a coffin, dumped a box of cockroaches inside, closed the coffin, and left him in it for **ten days** wearing only a diaper. The treatment was not among the techniques authorized by the Department of Justice or the President.<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="03:07" />

## Other unauthorized techniques

A number of other techniques applied to Abu Zubaydah and to other high-value detainees were similarly unauthorized:

- Rectal feeding with pureed [hummus](/wiki/hummus)
- Russian roulette<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="02:34" />

## Inadmissibility of obtained statements

Nothing obtained from Abu Zubaydah under interrogation is admissible against him at trial, because the [CIA](/wiki/cia) tortured it out of him. This is also the case for statements obtained from Khalid Sheikh Mohammed and Abd al-Rahim al-Nashiri. *"What do we do? We continue to hold them illegally without charge, or we let him go. The bottom line here is the CIA royally fucked this up."*<Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="08:54" /><Cite s="2023-11-26-dalton-fischer-enhanced-interrogation" t="09:28" />

## Civil litigation against contractors

Abu Zubaydah was the named plaintiff in the 2025 case before the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit in which he sought to sue the private contractors who had tortured him during his interrogation. The Ninth Circuit ruled — in approximately July 2025 — that contractors operating under written CIA contract are legally protected as agents of the agency under [Executive Order 12333](/wiki/executive-order-12333). See [Ninth Circuit contractor ruling (2025)](/wiki/ninth-circuit-contractor-ruling-2025).<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="1:05:16" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="1:05:46" />

## Recent contact and release that did not occur

As of the August 2025 source, [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) is in periodic contact with Abu Zubaydah's attorneys. Per the attorneys, Abu Zubaydah first learned of Kiriakou's December 2007 ABC News interview confirming CIA waterboarding from a friendly Guantanamo guard who came to his cell that day to tell him *"a CIA man went public today about what happened to you."* Per Abu Zubaydah, *"it was the first time he had experienced a sensation of hope."* He has subsequently asked his attorneys to convey to Kiriakou his hope that the two of them may someday have dinner *"as free men."*<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:31:23" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:32:26" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:32:58" />

Abu Zubaydah was, per Kiriakou, weeks from release at the end of the Biden administration; the administration had made the decision to release him and was in negotiations with foreign governments willing to take him, but had not closed any of those agreements before the November 2024 election. *"And then Trump won. And so he's not going anywhere."*<Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:32:58" /><Cite s="2025-08-31-dalton-fischer-mossad-blackwater" t="3:33:28" />

## The bedside conversation

After [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah)'s [capture](/wiki/abu-zubaydah-capture), [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) sat at his bedside for fifty-six straight hours under direct orders from [George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet): *"CIA eyes on, 24/7 — do not leave his bedside."* When Abu Zubaydah finally came out of his coma, his first lucid words were *"I will not speak to you in God's language"* — in perfect English.<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:04:43" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:09:56" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:10:28" />

His next request, through the oxygen mask: *"Please brother, kill me. Take the pillow and kill me — smother me."* Kiriakou: *"No, nobody's going to kill you. We've been looking for you for a long time."*<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:10:59" />

Kiriakou: *"Your life is over and the rest of it can be easy or it can be terrible. My friends, they aren't nice like I am — I'm the nicest guy you're going to meet in this experience. So if you do anything, it's that you have to cooperate."* Abu Zubaydah: *"You seem like a nice man, but you're the enemy and I'll never cooperate."*<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:10:59" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:11:31" />

## *"I never wanted to attack the United States"*

After Kiriakou pushed back on Abu Zubaydah framing himself as a victim — *"there were 50,000 people in those towers. Did you really think we weren't going to try to find you?"* — Abu Zubaydah's answer: *"I never wanted to attack the United States. I wanted to attack Israel. All I ever wanted to do was to kill Jews."*<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="1:14:08" />

## Intelligence provided to Ali Soufan

According to [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah)'s cooperation with FBI agent [Ali Soufan](/wiki/ali-soufan) — obtained without coercion — produced three significant intelligence outputs. First, he provided the al-Qaeda organizational structure below the level of Osama bin Laden and Ayman al-Zawahiri, which the [CIA](/wiki/cia) had not previously mapped.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="42:49" /> Second, he described al-Qaeda's general methodology for planning attacks, naming specific individuals, telephone numbers, addresses, and weapons sources in response to hypothetical scenarios posed by Soufan — intelligence that was passed to allied services to neutralize active cells.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="43:20" /> Third, and most consequentially, he identified the true name of a figure the CIA had known only as Mukhtar. When Soufan asked about Mukhtar, Abu Zubaydah laughed and said, *"You don't know who Mukhtar is? It's Khaled Shik Muhammad."*<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="43:51" /> That identification led directly to the targeting and eventual capture of [Khalid Sheikh Mohammed](/wiki/khalid-sheikh-mohammed).

## Waterboarding and cardiac arrest

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) stated that during the [CIA](/wiki/cia)'s [waterboarding](/wiki/waterboarding) of Abu Zubaydah, Zubaydah's heart stopped. CIA officers had to revive him — and then continued waterboarding him. Zubaydah was waterboarded eighty-three times in total.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="25:42" /> Kiriakou presented this sequence as evidence that the program's purpose was not intelligence collection: a subject who has just been resuscitated from cardiac arrest and is waterboarded again is not being interrogated for information.<Cite s="2025-06-04-tucker-carlson-cia-torture-mk-ultra-911" t="26:10" />

## *"I never wanted to attack the United States"*

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described a personal conversation with [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) in which Zubaydah was crying. Kiriakou told him: *"You're not the victim here. There were 50,000 people in those towers. What did you think we were going to do — that we wouldn't try to hunt you down, to capture you or kill Bin Laden?"*<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="58:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="59:00" />

Zubaydah's response, as Kiriakou recounted it: *"I never wanted to attack the United States. I wanted to attack Israel."* He then said: *"I never had anything against the United States until I saw a video of an Israeli soldier with his boot on the neck of a Palestinian grandmother."*<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="59:00" />

Kiriakou's reaction: *"How are you going to argue with that?"*<Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="59:00" /><Cite s="2026-04-20-tommy-g-israel-double-agents-directed-energy" t="59:30" />

## Night of capture — paddy wagon and hoods

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described the night [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) was captured, noting that dozens of other al-Qaeda fighters were taken simultaneously. So many prisoners were captured that they had to be brought into the [CIA](/wiki/cia) safe house for interrogation in shifts — ten at a time in a paddy wagon — because the facility could not hold them all at once.<Cite s="2025-12-04-dave-smith-part-of-the-problem-1335" t="16:30" />

When the first group of prisoners arrived, they had bags over their heads. Kiriakou asked the officer who had transported them why. The officer said: *"We don't want them to see our faces."* Kiriakou's response: *"Are you seriously telling me that you have never read the Geneva Convention? It is a war crime to put hoods on them. Take the hoods off."*<Cite s="2025-12-04-dave-smith-part-of-the-problem-1335" t="17:00" />

The officer refused: *"Don't take the hoods off."* He told Kiriakou: *"I'm going to report you to headquarters."* Kiriakou replied: *"Oh, I'm already reporting you to headquarters for committing a war crime. Take the hoods off."* The hoods were removed. Both officers reported each other. Kiriakou was reprimanded.<Cite s="2025-12-04-dave-smith-part-of-the-problem-1335" t="17:00" /><Cite s="2025-12-04-dave-smith-part-of-the-problem-1335" t="17:30" />

## Biden's quiet release decision — no country would accept him

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) disclosed that President Biden had made a decision to release [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) from Guantanamo. The process was handled quietly, without public announcement. Biden's conclusion was that Abu Zubaydah was innocent: he had never been charged with a crime, had been tortured mercilessly by the [CIA](/wiki/cia), and it was time to release him. The release did not happen because the United States could not find a single country willing to accept him. Donald Trump was then elected, ending any near-term prospect of release.<Cite s="2026-03-17-deep-focus-irans-future-cia-background-checks-us-revolution" t="32:30" /><Cite s="2026-03-17-deep-focus-irans-future-cia-background-checks-us-revolution" t="33:00" /><Cite s="2026-03-17-deep-focus-irans-future-cia-background-checks-us-revolution" t="33:30" />

Kiriakou stated he is publicly on record that Abu Zubaydah should be released and should have been released years ago — as soon as it became clear he was not the number three in Al-Qaeda, was not guilty of war crimes or crimes against humanity, and could not be charged because the evidence against him was tainted by torture.<Cite s="2026-03-17-deep-focus-irans-future-cia-background-checks-us-revolution" t="33:30" />

## Two Abu Zubaydahs — the first cousin

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) disclosed that there were in fact two individuals going by the name [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) — Abu Zubaydah himself and a first cousin who used the same name. This was not known before the capture. As a result, all the intelligence flowing into [CIA](/wiki/cia) headquarters about Abu Zubaydah was a composite of two people, which made the target appear to be what Kiriakou described as a *"terrorist Superman."* His conclusion: *"I think they were both bad guys — we just didn't realize there were two of them, and so all the intelligence that was coming in made him look like he was more important than he was."*<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="28:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="29:00" />

## The hunt — six weeks, Lahore to Faisalabad

The hunt for [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) lasted approximately six weeks. Intelligence placed him in either Lahore or Faisalabad; he was constantly moving. [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou)'s team broke down the doors of safe houses and apartments repeatedly. Some days they were a day or two late. Some days they missed him by fifteen minutes — the cigarette on the table was still lit.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="28:00" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="28:30" />

## The capture — Punjab Elite Force, one rule

On the night of the capture — 2:20 a.m. — [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) stood on a coffee table addressing thirty-six [CIA](/wiki/cia) and FBI officers plus approximately fifty members of the Pakistani Punjab Elite Force, a special-forces unit who wore t-shirts bearing a 9mm pistol image and did not wear bulletproof vests. Kiriakou's only stated rule: don't shoot them. His orders were to take them alive.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="31:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="32:00" />

When the door of [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah)'s house was breached, Abu Zubaydah, a Syrian bomber, and Abu Zubaydah's bodyguard went to the roof and attempted to jump to the neighboring building. A Pakistani policeman on the street opened fire with an AK-47. The Syrian bomber was killed instantly — dead before he hit the ground. Abu Zubaydah was shot three times.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="32:00" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="32:30" />

[Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) has stated he has wrestled with the question of whether the Pakistanis knew who the target was and shot him deliberately. The [CIA](/wiki/cia) never told the Pakistanis who they were hunting.<Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="32:30" /><Cite s="2026-04-16-hang-out-with-sean-hannity-lawfare-template" t="33:00" />

## Fourteen sites simultaneously — *"we'd never hit more than two before"*

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described bringing in a targeting analyst from headquarters who narrowed [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah)'s probable location to one of fourteen possible sites. Kiriakou noted they had never hit more than two sites in a single night before. He cabled headquarters requesting a large team; thirty-six men arrived, half [CIA](/wiki/cia) and half FBI, along with pallets of weapons, ammunition, night-vision goggles, battering rams, satellite dishes, and other equipment. In late March 2002 at 2:00 a.m., all fourteen sites were hit simultaneously. Abu Zubaydah was in the final site.<Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="2:30" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="3:00" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="3:30" />

## Ear scan — *"no two people have the same ears"*

The only photograph of [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) on file was an old passport photo showing a handsome, thin, clean-shaven young man with close-cropped hair. The man on the ground was overweight, long-haired, and unshaven — they were uncertain if it was him. The targeting analyst instructed [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) to attempt a retinal scan, but Abu Zubaydah's eyes were rolled back and he was bleeding to death. The analyst then said to photograph his ear. Kiriakou did not know until that night that no two people on earth have the same ears — they are as distinctive as fingerprints. The ear photograph was sent to headquarters; confirmation came back within minutes: it was him.<Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="4:30" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="5:00" />

## Shifa Hospital — al-Qaeda opens fire

[Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) was rushed to Shifa Hospital — which [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described as *"filthy, terrible, disgusting."* Word reached the al-Qaeda network that he had been captured. Vehicles began driving past the hospital and opening fire on the building. Kiriakou told the Pakistani major he was working with: *"If they realize how lightly armed we are, we're dead — we only had a couple of handguns."* The major called a helicopter; twenty minutes later it arrived. Kiriakou entered the operating room, told the doctor to wrap up, and they loaded Abu Zubaydah for transfer to a Pakistani military base approximately one hour away.<Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="5:30" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="6:00" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="6:30" />

## *"24/7 CIA eyes on"*

[CIA](/wiki/cia) Director [George Tenet](/wiki/george-tenet) relayed an instruction through the targeting analyst: twenty-four-seven CIA eyes on [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah). [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) tore up a sheet, tied him to the bed, and sat in a chair at the foot of the bed to keep watch, afraid of falling asleep.<Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="6:30" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="7:00" />

## *"You're not the victim here"*

Twenty-four hours after the capture, [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) came to consciousness. He motioned for [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) to come close, and Kiriakou moved his oxygen mask and said: *"Shukran — what is your name?"* Abu Zubaydah shook his head. Kiriakou repeated the question in Arabic. Abu Zubaydah replied in English: *"I'm not going to speak to you in God's language."* Kiriakou said: *"That's okay, Abu Zubaydah. We know who you are."* Abu Zubaydah began crying. He asked to be smothered with a pillow. Kiriakou told him: *"Nobody's going to kill you. We've been looking for you for a long time. You're going to get the best medical care the American government can provide."*<Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="7:00" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="7:30" />

Abu Zubaydah cried at length. He said he would never know the touch of a woman or the joy of fatherhood. Kiriakou replied: *"You're not the victim here. There were fifty thousand people in those towers. What did you think we were going to do?"* Abu Zubaydah told him he had never wanted to attack the United States — he had wanted to attack Israel, but had been overruled.<Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="7:30" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="8:00" />

Over the following day, Abu Zubaydah recited poetry he had written, spoke about his family, and discussed Christianity versus Islam with Kiriakou. Kiriakou's reflection: *"I remember thinking, I should hate you. I should want to kill you. And I don't. You're pathetic."* He also thought: *"This is the fearsome al-Qaeda. This is what we're so afraid of. He's practically a child."*<Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="8:00" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="8:30" />

## *"I'm the nicest guy you're going to meet"*

Kiriakou described delivering a final message before [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) was transferred: *"I'm going to give you some advice. I am the nicest guy that you're going to meet in this experience. My colleagues are not as nice as I am. So if you do one thing, it's that you have to cooperate."* Abu Zubaydah replied: *"You seem like a nice man. But you're the enemy, and I'll never cooperate."* Kiriakou: *"Suit yourself."*<Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="8:30" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="9:00" />

## The transfer — luggage rack, squeezed hand

An unmarked private jet landed on the tarmac. Three FBI agents and [Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) loaded [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah) onto the plane. They laid him across the luggage rack in the back and tied him down. Kiriakou leaned over and said one last time: *"Remember — you have to cooperate."* Abu Zubaydah squeezed his hand. The plane took off. Kiriakou never saw him again.<Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="9:00" />

## Coffin with bugs — two weeks; dog cage — three weeks

[Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described the beginning of [Abu Zubaydah](/wiki/abu-zubaydah)'s torture regimen. Interrogators learned he had an irrational fear of insects. They placed him in a coffin, dumped a box of bugs inside, and closed it. He was left there for two weeks. Subsequently, he was kept in a dog cage for three weeks — too small to stand, lie flat, or stretch his legs. Within weeks, through what Kiriakou described as the technique of [learned helplessness](/wiki/learned-helplessness), he was mentally broken.<Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="11:30" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="12:00" /><Cite s="2018-06-14-rdap-dan-capture-torture-abu-zubaydah" t="12:30" />

## Pakistan: the flood strategy and the spiderweb

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described a strategy he proposed to accelerate al-Qaeda captures in Pakistan. CIA officers were stationed in villages all along the Pakistan-Afghanistan border, exposed and unprotected, catching al-Qaeda fighters one at a time as they fled the US bombing of Afghanistan. Kiriakou suggested pulling everyone off the border and letting the fighters flood into Pakistani cities — Rawalpindi, Islamabad, Faisalabad, Lahore, Karachi, Quetta — because *"they're going to make a mistake."* The first thing the fighters did upon arrival: get cell phones and call home. *"Thank you for your locational information."* The CIA then raided safe houses and grabbed dozens at a time, filling the Rawalpindi jail within roughly six weeks.<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="35:30" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="36:00" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="36:30" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="37:00" />

Kiriakou brought in a targeting analyst friend (flown in within 24 hours) to locate Abu Zubaydah. The analyst took a piece of butcher-block paper *"the size of a small American billboard,"* wrote *"Abu Zubaydah"* in the center, and built concentric rings: everyone in direct contact (phone, email, address — mostly travel agencies, since Abu Zubaydah was al-Qaeda's logistics man), then a tertiary ring of contacts-of-contacts, with connecting lines. The result *"looked like a spiderweb — very pretty, artistic."* It narrowed the location to fourteen sites — but the CIA had never hit more than two sites in a single night.<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="42:30" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="43:00" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="43:30" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="44:00" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="44:30" />

## "Mr. Fish" — buying two mansions in cash

Because the operation was so highly classified and the Pakistanis were not trusted with the target's identity, Abu Zubaydah was code-named *"Mr. Fish."* Kiriakou requested $10 million in cash, 36 personnel (half CIA, half FBI), weapons, battering rams, and scrambled satellite comms — all delivered within 48 hours on an unmarked 737. He hired a real estate agent and bought, in cash, a ten-bedroom mansion and a seven-bedroom mansion (one in each city) to use as interrogation safe houses, plus rented an entire floor of an A-list hotel as a temporary base.<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="45:00" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="45:30" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="46:00" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="46:30" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="47:00" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="47:30" />

During the dry run of all fourteen sites, Kiriakou cut one immediately: it was a shishkabob stand with a pay phone. Al-Qaeda was clearly in the neighborhood using the pay phone, but *"you can't raid the shishkabob stand at 2 o'clock in the morning — it's closed."*<Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="49:00" /><Cite s="2023-11-19-dalton-fischer-kiriakou-part-2" t="49:30" />
