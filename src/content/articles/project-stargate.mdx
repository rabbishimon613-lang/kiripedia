Per John Kiriakou, the CIA ran a remote-viewing program he calls Project Stargate that he says failed for decades before the agency gave up on it. <Cite s="2026-06-02-it-s-rainmaking-time-john-kiriakou-unfinished-business-a-raw-exchange" t="59:06" /> A separately produced narration segment inserted into the same program — addressing Kiriakou in the third person rather than quoting him — describes Stargate as a legitimate U.S. intelligence program that operated from the early 1970s through 1995, originally as an Army intelligence effort. <Cite s="2026-06-02-it-s-rainmaking-time-john-kiriakou-unfinished-business-a-raw-exchange" t="1:01:09" />

## 'I don't have time for this'

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says he never sat in a [Project Stargate](/wiki/project-stargate) briefing and dismissed the psychic-spying effort as *"so out there"* — telling a colleague he needed intelligence *"from the source's mouth,"* not from *"some guy going into a trance."* He drew a sharp line between Stargate and clinical [hypnosis](/wiki/walk-in), which he found genuinely effective.<Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="39:05" /><Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="39:36" /><Cite s="2026-06-07-jack-neel-epstein-mossad-mi6-cia" t="40:07" /> A separately produced narration segment names Stanford Research Institute scientist [Russell Targ](/wiki/russell-targ) as an originator of the program's remote-viewing protocols.
