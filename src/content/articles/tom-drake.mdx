**Tom Drake** is a former NSA senior executive turned whistleblower. Per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou), Drake is the source for the public account of NSA Director [Mike Hayden](/wiki/mike-hayden)'s September 11, 2001 statement to NSA personnel — that the agency had been *"waiting for a 9/11 so that they could implement the bigger program and grab everybody's electronics."*<Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="50:01" />

Kiriakou's framing of Drake's position: *"Tom Drake, the NSA whistleblower, will tell you that the systems already exist to spy on our enemies, protect Americans, and preserve civil liberties all at the same time. They existed."* Hayden's post-9/11 program scrapped that approach.<Cite s="2026-05-04-cleared-hot-446-cost-of-truth" t="50:01" />

## Stellar Wind

Per [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) — relaying [Tom Drake](/wiki/tom-drake) himself, *"a great great friend of mine"* — the pre-9/11 NSA program Drake had built was called **Stellar Wind**. *"Stellar Wind would be able to collect the same kind of data while protecting Americans, and [Hayden](/wiki/mike-hayden) decided no, we want to grab everything."* This is the program Edward Snowden later disclosed publicly.<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="2:24:26" /><Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="2:26:36" />

Drake — together with NSA colleague Bill Binney — used NSA's internal whistleblower process to flag the unconstitutional expansion. Per Kiriakou: *"They arrested everybody. They arrested Bill Binney. Bill Binney, who lost both of his legs to a flesh-eating bacteria, is in the shower on a Saturday morning when the FBI breaks down the door."*<Cite s="2024-11-13-julian-dorey-250" t="2:28:10" />

## Aftermath and personal losses

[Drake](/wiki/tom-drake)'s pursuit of internal channels ended in criminal charges rather than resolution. He was charged with nine felonies — seven counts of espionage and two counts of theft of government property, the *"property"* being defined as the classified information he carried in his memory when he left the building.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="6:16" /> He faced nearly one hundred years in prison.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="6:16" /> The charges were dismissed the night before trial, but only after Drake had lost his pension, his savings, and his wife — also an NSA employee who chose to remain with the agency rather than stay with him — as well as his five children. He subsequently worked as a technician at an Apple Store Genius Bar in Bethesda, Maryland.<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="6:48" />

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) cited [Drake](/wiki/tom-drake)'s case as the definitive example that the internal whistleblower mechanisms established for national security employees *"just simply don't work."*<Cite s="2025-04-23-dr-phil-podcast-secrets-lies-price-of-truth-part2" t="7:19" />

## *"On 9/11 it was almost giddy"* — Kiriakou's account

[John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) described Tom Drake's case as one whose story would make your *"hair stand up."* September 11th, 2001 was Drake's first day at the [NSA](/wiki/cia) in the Senior Intelligence Service. He described arriving to find an atmosphere he characterized as almost celebratory — NSA had developed programs they had been unable to implement because they knew them to be illegal. The attacks gave them the cover they needed: all they had to say was *"national security"* and they could implement anything.<Cite s="2022-05-23-danny-jones-cia-spy-betrayed" t="21:30" /><Cite s="2022-05-23-danny-jones-cia-spy-betrayed" t="22:00" />

Drake identified a specific program called [Stellar Wind](/wiki/thin-thread-stellar-wind) that could identify when a communication involved a suspected bad actor and intercept only those targeted communications — rather than intercepting the phone calls, text messages, and emails of all Americans. He said: *"Instead of just grabbing everybody's phone calls and text messages and emails, it only grabs the bad guys'."* He brought this to his supervisor, who told him: *"You're new. This is none of your business."*<Cite s="2022-05-23-danny-jones-cia-spy-betrayed" t="22:00" /><Cite s="2022-05-23-danny-jones-cia-spy-betrayed" t="22:30" />

Drake followed every available channel: his supervisor dismissed him; the NSA Inspector General told him the mass interception wasn't happening (the IG wasn't cleared for the program); the NSA General Counsel told him he was *"in way over your head"*; the Pentagon Inspector General destroyed the evidence he brought. He finally went to the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence. The response: NSA raided his home and arrested him. He was charged with seven counts of espionage and two counts of theft of government property — the allegation being that he stole classified information *"by carrying it inside his head when he left the building."* All charges were ultimately dropped.<Cite s="2022-05-23-danny-jones-cia-spy-betrayed" t="22:30" /><Cite s="2022-05-23-danny-jones-cia-spy-betrayed" t="23:00" /><Cite s="2022-05-23-danny-jones-cia-spy-betrayed" t="23:30" /><Cite s="2022-05-23-danny-jones-cia-spy-betrayed" t="24:00" /><Cite s="2022-05-23-danny-jones-cia-spy-betrayed" t="24:30" />
