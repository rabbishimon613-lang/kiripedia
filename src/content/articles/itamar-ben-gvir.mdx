**Itamar Ben-Gvir** is Israel's far-right Minister of National Security. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) cites him as an example of what happens when people *"convicted of anti-Arab or anti-Muslim hate crimes"* are put in charge of security over their victims.<Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="23:59" /> He describes Ben-Gvir more specifically as a convicted felon, found guilty by Israeli courts on multiple counts of anti-Arab and anti-Muslim hate crimes, now serving as national security minister with oversight of security in Gaza and the West Bank.<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="1:51:17" /> Before the [October 7 attack](/wiki/october-7-attack), Kiriakou says, Ben-Gvir — thinking he was *"smarter than everybody else"* — insisted any attack would come from the West Bank rather than Gaza, and had response teams transferred there, contributing to the delayed response.<Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="43:31" /><Cite s="2024-07-27-saltcube-cia-enemy-of-the-state" t="44:05" /> In a separate account, Kiriakou attributes the pre-attack Israeli belief that any conflict would occur in the West Bank rather than Gaza directly to Ben-Gvir, describing him as "the new extreme right-wing" National Security Minister.<Cite s="2023-12-11-danny-jones-whats-really-happening-in-israel" t="1:46:52" /> A co-guest on the same program described Ben-Gvir's hate-crime convictions in similar terms, adding that he had displayed in his home a portrait of a man who committed a massacre at the Cave of the Patriarchs.<Cite s="2024-08-12-danny-jones-loyal-officer-vs-dissident-spy" t="2:23:41" />

Kiriakou also notes that the Knesset passed a law, under Ben-Gvir's national-security portfolio, reinstating the death penalty specifically for Palestinian detainees — the first such measure since Israel abolished capital punishment in 1956, with the sole exception made for Adolf Eichmann.<Cite s="2026-04-06-danny-jones-iran-war-trumps-nightmare" t="1:50:47" />
