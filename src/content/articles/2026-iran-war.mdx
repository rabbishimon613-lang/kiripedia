The **2026 Iran war** is the open-ended American and Israeli campaign against Iran that [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) covered week by week on his own show through July 2026. His running position is that it was won on its own stated terms within days, has been fought for months since without any further objective, and cannot be won on any other terms.

## The decision, days before

Kiriakou reported the decision to attack before it was public. On 23 February 2026 he said a former CIA officer who had been at the White House that morning talking to friends told him *"that a decision has been made to attack Iran on Monday or Tuesday"* — this after the president had given Iran ten days to accept an end to its ballistic-missile programme, an end to uranium enrichment, and an end to its support for Hamas, Hezbollah and the Houthis.<Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="3:00:57" /> He did not expect the deadline to be honoured: *"He'll give you 10 days, he'll give you two weeks, and then he'll just attack two days into it,"* on the theory that it keeps adversaries off balance.<Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="3:01:28" />

The disposition of forces, as he set it out, made the timeline credible: the USS Abraham Lincoln had been in the Arabian Sea for weeks with roughly two dozen support ships across the Persian Gulf, the Arabian Sea, the Red Sea and the Eastern Mediterranean, and the USS Gerald R. Ford was passing through the Strait of Gibraltar that day, bound for the Eastern Mediterranean and probably the Suez Canal. *"We're going to have enough assets to attack Iran in the next couple of days."*<Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="3:01:28" /><Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="3:02:00" />

### The battle lines inside the administration

The same source described the internal split: JD Vance and Tulsi Gabbard against the war, and Marco Rubio, Pete Hegseth *"and now the Joint Chiefs of Staff"* for it. Kiriakou stopped him there, because the alignment inverted his own experience — at the start of the Iraq war *"the most stridently anti-war component was the Joint Chiefs of Staff. They were always the last ones that wanted to attack, because they know what war is."*<Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="3:02:31" /> The explanation offered was one he says he had forgotten: over the preceding twelve months Trump had replaced every one of the Joint Chiefs, firing the four-star generals who had risen through the ranks under Biden and promoting officers he judged politically loyal.<Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="3:03:02" />

### The diplomacy it overrode

Kiriakou, who describes himself as a glass-half-full man, had been publicly optimistic until that morning. Two rounds of talks — with the Omanis in Muscat and the Swiss in Geneva — had been called productive by both sides, a third was scheduled, and the Omanis had asked Washington to relax and let them work. He rates Omani diplomacy highly and explains Iranian trust in it simply: *"they're honest, so the Iranians trust them as well."* His conclusion was that none of it mattered any more — *"I think a decision's been made."*<Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="3:03:33" /><Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="3:04:07" />

Asked whether the timing was intended to distract from the [Epstein](/wiki/jeffrey-epstein) disclosures then dominating coverage, he answered *"probably in part,"* noting the simultaneous announcement that UFO files would be released, and reaching for *Wag the Dog* — a film whose premise, a war to distract from a presidential scandal, once made people's hair stand on end and now reads as quaint. *"Same movie over and over again. Different actors, just remakes. And people have short memories."*<Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="3:04:07" /><Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="3:04:39" />

His objection on the merits was that the White House had *"bitten right into this Israeli propaganda, the same kind of propaganda we got in 2003"* — that Iranians would greet an invasion as liberators and everyone would get rich on new oil deals — against a country bigger than Texas with 92 million people. *"That's just not going to happen. It never happens."*<Cite s="2026-02-23-epstein-lies-satanic-elite-mossad" t="3:05:39" />

## "Month five of our supposedly three-week war"

Kiriakou dates the sales pitch precisely. The Iranian government "was supposed to collapse like a house of cards," and *"anybody with any background in the Middle East said at the time that that was absurd."* By his third episode he was calling it *"month five of our supposedly three-week war with Iran."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="01:01" />

The premise came from [Benjamin Netanyahu](/wiki/benjamin-netanyahu): that Iran would fall apart, the Shah's son would return, and *"it's going to be the land of milk and honey… they're going to be waving little American and Israeli flags."* Kiriakou's verdict on the people who believed it is that they were *"idiotically confident."*<Cite s="2026-07-28-john-kiriakou-shocking-new-footage-of-lindsey-graham-moments" t="06:50" /><Cite s="2026-07-28-john-kiriakou-shocking-new-footage-of-lindsey-graham-moments" t="07:51" />

The decapitation strikes at the outset were, by his account, comprehensive: the Ayatollah killed, almost every Iranian nuclear scientist killed, multiple cabinet members, and just about every senior figure in the military leadership. *"The war was supposed to last a day. A day. Because once these people were dead… the Iranian people were going to rise up."* It never happened.<Cite s="2026-07-14-john-kiriakou-my-honest-thoughts-about-lindsey-graham-ep" t="20:32" /><Cite s="2026-07-14-john-kiriakou-my-honest-thoughts-about-lindsey-graham-ep" t="21:04" />

## Why it cannot be won

Kiriakou's argument against victory is demographic and geographic before it is political. Iran has 93 million people. *"We couldn't defeat Afghanistan with less than a quarter of that population. We couldn't defeat Iraq with… less than half that population."* The country is the size of the whole of Western Europe and is "surrounded on three sides by formidable mountain ranges." Actually replacing the government, he estimates, *"we would need a million ground troops. It's not going to happen."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="03:05" /><Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="03:36" />

He grants the degradation and disputes the word used for it. When a cable host repeated that the United States had "obliterated" Iran's military, Kiriakou's correction was flat: *"No, we haven't. We have degraded Iran's military, but obliterated — that's a word that the president used. That's just simply not true."* And degradation of the leadership is temporary: *"there's always going to be somebody else that pops back up into a leadership role."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="02:34" /><Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="03:36" />

### The drone asymmetry

Where Iran has succeeded, he says, is with suicide drones — and the exchange rate is the war's defining fact. The United States was firing *"$100,000 missiles against five or ten thousand dollar drones."* Iran can produce tens of thousands of them and buys whatever it does not build from China and Russia, sending *"wave after wave after wave."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="04:07" />

The logic that follows is what makes air defence futile: *"if you've got this cheap drone and you just strap some explosives to it, and then you send it to its target — what do you care if it's shot down by a $100,000 missile? It doesn't make any difference."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="04:07" />

He applies the same arithmetic to missile defence and to carriers. Systems like Iron Dome can shoot down incoming missiles, *"but you can't shoot down 5,000 incoming drones. You don't have 5,000 missiles."* And an aircraft carrier strike group — a carrier plus eleven or twelve ships — has no answer to *"suicide drones that are just going to fly into its side."*<Cite s="2026-07-14-john-kiriakou-my-honest-thoughts-about-lindsey-graham-ep" t="17:18" /><Cite s="2026-07-14-john-kiriakou-my-honest-thoughts-about-lindsey-graham-ep" t="17:51" /><Cite s="2026-07-14-john-kiriakou-my-honest-thoughts-about-lindsey-graham-ep" t="18:24" />

His summary is unqualified: *"we can't beat Iran. Nobody can beat Iran."* The reasons he lists are the age of the culture, that the Iranians "live in the area," and that the nature of warfare has changed.<Cite s="2026-07-14-john-kiriakou-my-honest-thoughts-about-lindsey-graham-ep" t="16:47" />

## The infrastructure exchange

The escalation Kiriakou treats as most dangerous is reciprocal strikes on civilian infrastructure. Iran hit a desalination plant in Kuwait — *"That's technically a war crime. You can't hit infrastructure"* — a day after the United States destroyed an Iranian desalination plant. These are tit-for-tat.<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="04:39" /><Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="05:11" />

He also notes Iranian state media reporting that a US strike hit a nuclear power plant still under construction — *"That would be a violation of international law if that turns out to be true."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="16:43" />

The strategic dilemma he identifies is that Iran's reach is wider than the Gulf. Its ability to strike partners extends past Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, Oman, the UAE and Saudi Arabia to Jordan, Iraq and the Red Sea. So while Washington has threatened Iran's bridges, power plants and desalination capacity, Iran retains the ability to do the same across the Gulf Cooperation Council states.<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="17:46" />

That, he argues, is what restrains the White House — the allies. *"US allies in the region have little interest in seeing this conflict expand. I'll rephrase: they have zero interest."* His prediction follows: *"the United States probably will not ramp up attacks on civilian infrastructure."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="18:17" /><Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="18:47" />

## What the public is not told

Kiriakou repeatedly flags the reporting gap rather than the fighting. The New York Times, he notes, found that in the week before an Iranian attack that killed two American soldiers and left one missing in Jordan, Iran had carried out three other strikes on US forces there — injuring dozens of service members and damaging several helicopters — none of which the Pentagon disclosed.<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="05:11" /><Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="05:43" />

His explanation is legal, not conspiratorial, and he anticipates sounding repetitive about it: *"they are not compelled. The Pentagon is not compelled to report these figures. There's no law that says they have to."* And there is no law *"because Congress is asleep at the switch. And they never passed any such law."* The consequence is predictable — *"Why would we inform Capitol Hill? Why would we inform the media… when we don't have to?"*<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="05:43" /><Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="06:13" />

The cost figure he cites is about $132 billion and rising — *"thousands and thousands of dollars for each American… not even a taxpayer, for each American citizen"* — and his gloss is that this is *"a bigger problem over a longer term than our government has led us to believe."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="16:11" /><Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="16:43" />

## An undeclared war

Kiriakou's structural objection is constitutional. *"Congress never declared war on Iran. It's an undeclared, illegal war. We did it at the behest of a foreign government, Israel, and we can't get out."*<Cite s="2026-07-28-john-kiriakou-shocking-new-footage-of-lindsey-graham-moments" t="21:25" />

The analogy he reaches for is the one he has used since the first missile: *"To me, this feels like the start of the Iraq war."* His warning then was that there was no exit strategy — *"It's easy to bomb a country. It's easy to overthrow a government if you want to put troops on the ground. It's almost impossible to get out. And we never learn our lesson."*<Cite s="2026-07-28-john-kiriakou-shocking-new-footage-of-lindsey-graham-moments" t="21:25" />

## The grade, and the forecast

His assessment is delivered as a report card: *"I want to give a grade of D for how this war is going."* The reasoning is that the objectives were all met and the fighting did not stop. If the goal was to kill the Iranian political leadership, "they've all been murdered." The military leadership — "they're gone. They're all dead." Almost all the nuclear scientists are dead. *"So why are we still fighting in Iran?"*<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="18:47" /><Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="19:18" />

He expects months more, ending not in settlement but in announcement — that one day *"we're probably all going to wake up and Donald Trump will have posted on Truth Social: 'We won. It's all over. Everybody's coming home. It's the greatest victory in the history of victories.'"* And then: *"really, in the end, nothing's going to change."*<Cite s="2026-07-21-john-kiriakou-this-is-why-iran-s-missiles-will" t="19:18" />

## The effect on Iranians

The outcome Kiriakou treats as the war's most durable is the one it was least meant to produce. *"What we've done with this war against Iran is we've driven Iranians together. And now the Iranians are united against us, against Israel, and against our Arab allies."*<Cite s="2026-07-28-john-kiriakou-shocking-new-footage-of-lindsey-graham-moments" t="08:22" />

He is equally sceptical of claims that Tehran is suing for peace. When Trump told reporters that Iranian officials had "called a little while ago and want to make a deal," Kiriakou's response was that it was *"just simply not true… That's made up. That's for domestic US political consumption. The Iranians are willing to fight to the very death to protect their country."*<Cite s="2026-07-14-john-kiriakou-my-honest-thoughts-about-lindsey-graham-ep" t="19:59" />

## The asymmetry

Assessing the resumed fighting in July 2026, [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) states the conclusion bluntly: "We can't beat Iran. Nobody can beat Iran." He gives three reasons — a culture seven thousand years old, the fact that Iranians live in the region, and a change in the nature of warfare itself.<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="16:48" />

The third reason he illustrates with cost. The United States deployed two carrier strike groups, each an aircraft carrier plus eleven or twelve escorting ships. Iran is fighting with suicide drones that cost between five and ten thousand dollars to build. A system like Iron Dome can shoot down incoming missiles, but, as he puts it, "you can't shoot down 5,000 incoming drones. You don't have 5,000 missiles" — and there is no defence for a carrier against drones simply flying into its side.<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="17:18" /><Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="17:49" />

He also treats the war's founding premise as already disproven. The opening decapitation strikes killed Ayatollah Khamenei, nearly every Iranian nuclear scientist, multiple cabinet members and most of the senior military leadership, on the expectation that the Iranian people would then rise up and the war would last a day. That never happened, and Kiriakou argues anyone who follows Iranian politics could have predicted it would not.<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="20:27" /><Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="21:01" />

He likewise dismisses the claim that Iranian officials had telephoned asking to make a deal as invented for domestic American consumption: "The Iranians are willing to fight to the very death to protect their country."<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="19:56" /> The comparison he reaches for is Afghanistan and Iraq — twenty-year wars entered on the advice of people who thought a country of ninety-three million the size of Western Europe, backed by Russia and China, could be beaten in a week.<Cite s="2026-07-18-national-security-files-john-kiriakou-former-cia-officer-exposes-the" t="15:45" />
