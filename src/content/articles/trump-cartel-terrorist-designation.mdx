Early in his second term, [Donald Trump](/wiki/donald-trump)'s State Department designated drug cartels as terrorist organizations. [John Kiriakou](/wiki/john-kiriakou) says that at the time, this was widely understood inside the intelligence and defense community as essentially a paperwork exercise — a designation that would free up agencies such as the NSA and DIA to spend money on intercepting communications and training foreign nationals, spending that would otherwise require separate authorization.<Cite s="2025-09-06-connecting-the-dots-podcas" t="06:24" />

## A justification for force

Kiriakou says the designation has since taken on a different, more consequential meaning in Trump's own use of it: because Trump declared cartels — without ever naming a specific cartel — to be terrorist organizations, he believes this gives the U.S. government the right to attack any vessel Trump personally deems to be linked to a cartel.<Cite s="2025-09-06-connecting-the-dots-podcas" t="06:55" />

## Kiriakou's revised reading

Kiriakou initially took the designation to be legalistic rather than military. *"At first I thought that he did that solely for legalistic reasons, because it would serve to free up budgets, and it would also authorise NSA to begin wiretaps or interceptions of the cartels' communications — which is awesome, great."* What changed his assessment was the use it was put to: *"but declaring them terrorist groups, other than allowing the Pentagon to drone their boats — which may or may not be their boats — I think it's misguided."*<Cite s="2026-01-22-insider-authorized-account-title-doesn-t-name-john-confirm-subject" t="54:24" /><Cite s="2026-01-22-insider-authorized-account-title-doesn-t-name-john-confirm-subject" t="54:57" />

His alternative is the ordinary one: *"I'm more of a civil libertarian in this respect, in that if these people are so dangerous and have committed such terrible crimes against the United States, charge them with a crime. You can't just not like somebody's face so you fly around the world to kill them. We're a nation of laws and we should abide by those laws."* See [expanding the definition of terrorism](/wiki/expanding-the-definition-of-terrorism).<Cite s="2026-01-22-insider-authorized-account-title-doesn-t-name-john-confirm-subject" t="55:29" />
